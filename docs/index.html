What this repository computes

The work evaluates a finite-deformation Biot coefficient for a body containing one deformable solid and one water phase. Global fields are Q2 displacement and continuous Q1 water pressure. Solid mass conservation determines partial density as its reference value divided by the deformation Jacobian. A local constitutive update supplies the intrinsic solid density and solid volume fraction. Backward Euler differences the complete reference fluid mass, including the converged plastic contribution. Matched logarithmic skeleton and mineral stress laws give a scalar mineral solve and a closed-form fixed-pressure Biot coefficient. A general implicit tangent and centered differences independently verify the coefficient.

A common stored energy connects mineral compression, permanent pore-volume change, and pressure coupling. The reversible Biot coefficient measures the response to a small perturbation at fixed plastic history. A finite pressure change relative to the zero-pressure drained stress generally has a different multiplier. The constitutive examples explain this distinction and its role in plastic flow.

The three worked examples

1 · Water-filled Mandel benchmark

The plane-strain Mandel consolidation problem solved with the closed-form Biot coefficient, compared against the analytical Cheng–Detournay solution, with a finite-deformation continuation that reports the departure of the Biot coefficient from its reference value.

2 · Poroplastic Biot coefficient

An implicit exponential return mapping couples plastic distention, mineral compression and the Biot coefficient. Independent state and derivative checks support the loading-history and pressure-feedback demonstrations.

3 · Coupled poroplastic compression

Plastic pore-volume change enters water storage in a drained compression and hold calculation. Independent storage derivatives, mesh and time refinement, and perfect-plastic controls check the coupled response and the selected hardening law.
