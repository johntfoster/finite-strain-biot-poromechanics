\section{Mandel verification}
\label{sec:mandel-verification}

\subsection{Boundary-value problem}
\label{sec:mandel-boundary-value-problem}

The Mandel problem considers a saturated plane-strain specimen compressed
between impermeable, frictionless rigid platens.  Water drains through the
lateral faces \citep{chengdetournay1988}.  Let \(X\) and \(Y\) denote the
horizontal and vertical reference coordinates, and let \(a\) and \(b\) be the
quarter-domain width and height, respectively.  Symmetry reduces the
calculation to
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_0=\left\{(X,Y):0\leq X\leq a,\ 0\leq Y\leq b\right\},
 \qquad
 a=1\ \mathrm{m},
 \qquad
 b=0.1\ \mathrm{m}.
 \label{eq:mandel-quarter-domain}
\end{equation}
The mechanical symmetry conditions and drained pressure condition are
\begin{subequations}
 \label{eq:mandel-boundary-conditions}
 \begin{align}
  u_x(0,Y,t)&=0,
  &u_y(X,0,t)&=0,
  \label{eq:mandel-symmetry-boundaries}\\
  p(a,Y,t)&=0,
  &\mbf W_f\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf N&=0
  \quad\text{on the remaining fluid boundaries}.
  \label{eq:mandel-fluid-boundaries}
 \end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here \(u_x\) and \(u_y\) are the components of \(\mbf u\), and \(\mbf N\) is
the outward unit normal to the reference boundary.
The top displacement is spatially uniform and follows the analytical
constant-load solution of \citet{chengdetournay1988}.  The computed vertical
nominal-stress resultant then provides an independent check of the applied
\(100\ \mathrm{kPa}\) compression.  The right face is mechanically
traction-free, and the prescribed-normal-displacement boundaries are
frictionless.  The initial material state is
\(\mbf u=\mbf0\), \(p=0\), and \(\rho_s=\rho_{s0}\); the local constitutive
state then has \(\bar\rho_s=\bar\rho_{s0}\), \(\phi_s=\phi_{s0}\), and
\(\bar J=1\).  The first time step applies the analytical platen
displacement, after which its time history follows
\eqref{eq:mandel-vertical-displacement-series}.  Because the vertical
displacement is imposed and is linear in \(Y\), its profile is a
boundary-condition reproduction check rather than an independent benchmark
observable.

The spatial solid-mass residual in \eqref{eq:solid-mass-weak-residual} remains
active throughout the Mandel solve.  The global field \(\rho_s\) supplies the
referential solid mass \(J\rho_s\), the elastic update supplies the intrinsic
solid state and its residual derivatives, and the fixed-pressure tangent
calculates \(B\) at every quadrature point
and time step.  The solid volume fraction is derived from the partial and
intrinsic solid densities, while water
pressure determines \(\bar\rho_f\) through
\eqref{eq:barotropic-water-density}.  The small applied load keeps
the coefficient near its reference value while exercising the same nonlinear
implementation used away from the reference state.

\subsection{Analytical solution}
\label{sec:mandel-analytical-solution}

Cheng and Detournay give the plane-strain analytical solution for constant
poroelastic coefficients \citep{chengdetournay1988}.  For the parameters in
\cref{tab:mandel-parameters}, the analytical storage coefficient is obtained by
linearizing the equations solved by the finite-element model.  At
\(J=1\), \(\rho_s=\rho_{s0}\), \(\phi_s=\phi_{s0}\), and \(\bar J=1\), source-free solid
mass gives \(\phi_s=\phi_{s0}\bar J/J\), and hence
\(J(1-\phi_s)=J-\phi_{s0}\bar J\).  Differentiation of this identity,
using \eqref{eq:elastic-mineral-jacobian-derivative} and
\(\partial\bar J/\partial p=-\bar J/K_s\), gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left[J(1-\phi_s)\right]
 =B_0\dot J+\frac{\phi_{s0}}{K_s}\dot p.
 \label{eq:mandel-linearized-pore-volume-rate}
\end{equation}
Substitution into the exact water balance yields
\begin{equation}
 B_0\dot J
 +\left(
  \frac{\phi_{s0}}{K_s}+\frac{\phi_{f0}}{K_f}
 \right)\dot p
 +\frac{1}{\bar\rho_{f0}}
 \nabla_{\mbf X}\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf W_f=0.
 \label{eq:mandel-linearized-water-balance}
\end{equation}
This reduction defines the linearized storage modulus \(M_{\mathrm{lin}}\) for
the analytical comparison, obtained from the solved phase states.  Following
\citet{chengdetournay1988}, the remaining poroelastic parameters are
\begin{subequations}
 \label{eq:mandel-derived-parameters}
 \begin{align}
  \frac{1}{M_{\mathrm{lin}}}
  &=\frac{\phi_{s0}}{K_s}+\frac{\phi_{f0}}{K_f},
  \label{eq:mandel-storage-modulus}\\
  K_u&=K+B_0^2M_{\mathrm{lin}},
  \label{eq:mandel-undrained-bulk-modulus}\\
  B_{\mathrm{Sk}}&=\frac{B_0M_{\mathrm{lin}}}{K_u},
  \label{eq:mandel-skempton-coefficient}\\
  \nu&=\frac{3K-2G}{2(3K+G)},
  \qquad
  \nu_u=\frac{3K_u-2G}{2(3K_u+G)},
  \label{eq:mandel-poisson-ratios}\\
  c&=\frac{
   2G(1-\nu)(\nu_u-\nu)\kappa
  }{
   B_0^2(1-\nu_u)(1-2\nu)^2\mu_f
  }.
  \label{eq:mandel-consolidation-coefficient}
 \end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here \(K_u\) is the undrained bulk modulus, \(B_{\mathrm{Sk}}\) is the Skempton
coefficient, \(\nu\) and \(\nu_u\) are the drained and undrained Poisson
ratios, respectively, and \(c\) is the consolidation coefficient.

The storage in \eqref{eq:mandel-storage-modulus} is specific to the elastic
mineral law in \eqref{eq:elastic-mineral-density}.  The classical single-mineral Biot
storage relation would instead give
\begin{equation}
 \frac{1}{M_{\mathrm{Biot}}}
 =\frac{B_0-\phi_{f0}}{K_s}+\frac{\phi_{f0}}{K_f}
 =\frac{\phi_{s0}}{K_s}-\frac{K}{K_s^2}
 +\frac{\phi_{f0}}{K_f}.
 \label{eq:classical-biot-storage-comparison}
\end{equation}
The extra \(K/K_s^2\) in \(1/M_{\mathrm{lin}}\) follows from the selected
dependence of the elastic mineral law on the volumetric skeleton stress.  The
analytical series
therefore provides a model-consistent benchmark for the discretized equations;
it does not independently validate this constitutive storage choice against
classical Biot theory or experimental data \citep{biotwillis1957}.

The Cheng--Detournay characteristic equation defines \(\alpha_n\) as its
positive roots \citep{chengdetournay1988}:
\begin{equation}
 \tan\alpha_n
 =\frac{1-\nu}{\nu_u-\nu}\alpha_n.
 \label{eq:mandel-root-equation}
\end{equation}
The corresponding analytical water pressure is
\citep{chengdetournay1988}
\begin{align}
 p(X,t)
 ={}&\frac{2P_0B_{\mathrm{Sk}}(1+\nu_u)}{3}
 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
 \frac{
  \sin\alpha_n\cos\left(\alpha_n X/a\right)
  -\sin\alpha_n\cos\alpha_n
 }{
  \alpha_n-\sin\alpha_n\cos\alpha_n
 }
 \notag\\
 &\mathbin{\times}
 \exp\left(-\frac{\alpha_n^2ct}{a^2}\right).
 \label{eq:mandel-pressure-series}
\end{align}
The corresponding displacement fields are \citep{chengdetournay1988}
\begin{subequations}
 \label{eq:mandel-displacement-series}
 \begin{align}
  u_x(X,Y,t)
  ={}&\left[
  \frac{P_0\nu}{2G}
  -\frac{P_0\nu_u}{G}
  \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
  \frac{\sin\alpha_n\cos\alpha_n}
  {\alpha_n-\sin\alpha_n\cos\alpha_n}
  \exp\left(-\frac{\alpha_n^2ct}{a^2}\right)
  \right]X
  \notag\\
  &\mathbin{+}\frac{P_0a}{G}
  \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
  \frac{\cos\alpha_n\sin\left(\alpha_n X/a\right)}
  {\alpha_n-\sin\alpha_n\cos\alpha_n}
  \exp\left(-\frac{\alpha_n^2ct}{a^2}\right),
  \label{eq:mandel-horizontal-displacement-series}\\
  u_y(X,Y,t)
  ={}&\left[
  -\frac{P_0(1-\nu)}{2G}
  +\frac{P_0(1-\nu_u)}{G}
  \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
  \frac{\sin\alpha_n\cos\alpha_n}
  {\alpha_n-\sin\alpha_n\cos\alpha_n}
  \exp\left(-\frac{\alpha_n^2ct}{a^2}\right)
  \right]Y.
  \label{eq:mandel-vertical-displacement-series}
 \end{align}
\end{subequations}
The comparison retains the first twelve roots in
\eqref{eq:mandel-pressure-series} and
\eqref{eq:mandel-displacement-series}.  Against a 128-root evaluation over
401 positions and all reported times, this truncation changes pressure by at
most \(1.5067\times10^{-5}p_{\mathrm{scale}}\) and displacement by
\(5.3513\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{m}\).

\subsection{Finite-deformation continuation}
\label{sec:mandel-finite-deformation-continuation}

The analytical solution in
\eqref{eq:mandel-pressure-series} and
\eqref{eq:mandel-displacement-series} verifies the
small-load limit.  A second calculation uses the same water-filled domain,
Q2/Q1/Q2 approximation, spatial solid-mass residual, elastic mineral update,
and Biot transform to measure the finite-deformation response.  The platen displacement increases
linearly from zero to \(-0.1b\) over \(0.2\ \mathrm{s}\) and is then held
fixed.  This imposed history defines a displacement-controlled numerical
experiment; the resulting lateral displacement, pressure, nominal stress, and
Biot coefficient are predictions of the coupled model.

\subsection{Single-element poroplastic demonstration}
\label{sec:poroplastic-demonstration}

The activated inelastic update is demonstrated at a single material point
under prescribed drained homogeneous compression.  The point is loaded by a
uniform diagonal deformation gradient (uniaxial strain) and is otherwise the
same drained skeleton and elastic mineral used in the Mandel calculations:
\(K=1.0\ \mathrm{GPa}\), \(G=0.75\ \mathrm{GPa}\), \(K_s=2.5\
\mathrm{GPa}\), \(\phi_{s0}=0.9\), with the ideal (constant-strength)
Drucker--Prager surface \(M=0.2\), dilation slope \(\beta=0.4\).  The
poroplastic return map of
Sec.~\ref{sec:plastic-loading-incremental-rate} reduces, at constant
strength, to the closed-form radial return for the increment \(\Delta\gamma\);
the plastic pore allocation follows as
\(a^p=\exp(\beta\Delta\gamma)\), and the fixed-pressure, fixed-history
tangent with \(v=\bar J/a^p\) gives the coefficient correction
\begin{equation}
 1-B=\frac{1-B_{\mathrm{el}}}{a^p},
 \label{eq:poroplastic-biot-correction}
\end{equation}
where \(B_{\mathrm{el}}\) is the elastic coefficient of the same drained
skeleton and mineral: plastic pore allocation divides the elastic Biot
deficit \(1-B_{\mathrm{el}}\), so an active mechanism with \(a^p>1\) drives
the coefficient toward unity, while the inactive limit \(a^p=1\) returns
\(B=B_{\mathrm{el}}\).

Two checks verify the update.  First, with yield inactive (a large
\(M\)), the return map collapses exactly onto the elastic response:
\(\Delta\gamma=0\), \(a^p=1\), and \eqref{eq:poroplastic-biot-correction}
gives \(B=B_{\mathrm{el}}\) (\emph{elastic collapse}, \(\Delta B=0\)).
Second, with the surface active, the returned stress lands on the yield
surface to a residual of order \(10^{-9}\) and the pore allocation obeys
\(a^p=\exp(\beta\Delta\gamma)\).  \Cref{fig:poroplastic-delta-b} shows the
effect of activating yield on the coefficient under increasing compression:
the elastic coefficient \(B_{\mathrm{el}}\) decreases with finite strain,
while \(B_{\mathrm{pl}}=1-(1-B_{\mathrm{el}})/a^p\) remains above it, with
\(\Delta B=B_{\mathrm{pl}}-B_{\mathrm{el}}\) growing from \(0.005\) at
\(5\%\) to \(0.061\) at \(30\%\) axial compression.  The single-point
demonstration isolates the mechanism by which inelastic pore allocation
modifies the coefficient that a constant or elastic-only \(B\) cannot
produce; published drained hydrostatic measurements on high-porosity
sandstone report a similar monotone decrease of the drained Biot coefficient
with mean stress into the compactive regime \citep{ingraham2017evolution}.

\subsection{Drained sandstone hydrostatics as a compactive target}
\label{sec:mandel-sandstone-comparison}

Published drained measurements on high-porosity Castlegate sandstone
\citep{ingraham2017evolution} supply a compactive reference for the
pore-allocation mechanism of
Sec.~\ref{sec:inelastic-plastic-driving-stress}.  Along drained hydrostatic
paths the reported coefficient, obtained from unload--reload bulk moduli as
\(\alpha=1-K/K_m\), decreases monotonically from about \(0.88\) at low mean
stress to about \(0.80\) at \(140\)\,MPa effective, while the drained
tangent modulus stiffens from about \(6\) to \(12\)\,GPa and the porosity
falls from \(26\%\) to about \(14\%\) (post-test).
\Cref{fig:sandstone-comparison} places those points against the coefficient
of the present model and against two calibrations of the drained stiffening.

The constant-modulus finite-strain coefficient \(B_{\mathrm{el}}(J)\) of
Sec.~\ref{sec:elastic-verification-closure}, evaluated with the drained
modulus fixed at its low-stress value, declines by less than \(0.01\) over the
measured path (dashed curve): the elastic closure contains no drained
stiffening, so it cannot reproduce the measured drop of about \(0.08\).  Two
mutually consistent calibrations close that gap within the specimen scatter.
One attributes the decline to a pressure-dependent drained tangent modulus
\(K(P_{\mathrm{eff}})\) against a nearly fixed grain reference, so
\(\alpha=1-K/K_m\) falls because the drained modulus stiffens faster than the
unjacketed one.  The other attributes it to plastic porosity loss: a
porosity-dependent drained modulus \(K(\phi)\) with compactive pore
allocation \(a^p<1\) (the compaction branch \(\dot\eta\le0\) of
\eqref{eq:distension-yield}) lowers the coefficient through
\eqref{eq:poroplastic-biot-correction}, and the same law that reproduces
\(\alpha(P)\) implies the monotone porosity reduction in
\cref{fig:sandstone-comparison}, terminating at the independently measured
post-test porosity.  The two readings cannot be separated by \(\alpha(P)\)
alone; the compactive one is anchored by the porosity measurement.

For the present model the comparison is therefore a target, not a validation.
Reproducing it requires engaging the compactive branch of the
pore-allocation mechanism together with a porosity- or pressure-dependent
drained stiffness, a constitutive ingredient that the constant-modulus
elastic closure used in the verification sections does not contain.  With that
ingredient added, \cref{fig:sandstone-comparison} becomes a quantitative
model--data check rather than a statement of the missing physics.

\subsection{Scope and reproducibility}
\label{sec:mandel-scope-and-reproducibility}

The evidence separates implementation verification from constitutive and
physical validation.  The elastic closed-form identity checks the general
local fixed-pressure derivative, the PETSc comparison checks its outer AD propagation, and the
small-load Mandel calculation benchmarks the coupled discretization against a
series formed from the same linearized storage law.  The ten-percent
continuation is a synthetic discrimination test.  None of these calculations
independently validates the mineral EOS, and the reported model is restricted
to a saturated, isothermal, homogeneous, elastic body containing one solid and
one water phase.  That elastic model is the inactive-mechanism limit of the
general interface in
Sec.~\ref{sec:inelastic-plastic-driving-stress}: with the isochoric
true-plastic and scalar pore-allocation mechanisms active, the same implicit
solve returns \(B\) from the active local system, and the poroplastic driving
stress follows through
\(\bs\sigma^{\prime}=\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}+(1-B)p\mbf I\).  The activated
inelastic update is demonstrated at the single material point in
Sec.~\ref{sec:poroplastic-demonstration}; the coupled, momentum-active
Mandel-scale demonstration with the active set engaged remains the next
extension.
