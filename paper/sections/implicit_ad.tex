\section{Nested implicit automatic differentiation}

Let \(\mbf y\) collect local implicit variables such as phase volume fractions,
intrinsic/component densities, composition coordinates, or internal variables.
The local constitutive closure is written
\begin{equation}
 \mbf R(\mbf y,J_s,\bar p_E,\theta_{\mc S},\ldots)=\mbf 0.
 \label{eq:local-constraints}
\end{equation}
Holding \(\bar p_E,\theta_{\mc S},\ldots\) fixed and differentiating
\cref{eq:local-constraints} with respect to \(J_s\) gives
\begin{equation}
 \pder{\mbf R}{\mbf y}\mbf y_{,J}
 +\pder{\mbf R}{J_s}=\mbf 0,
 \qquad
 \mbf y_{,J}=-
 \left(\pder{\mbf R}{\mbf y}\right)^{-1}
 \pder{\mbf R}{J_s}.
 \label{eq:implicit-tangent}
\end{equation}
The derivatives of every solid volume fraction and every referential component
accumulation in \cref{eq:summed-specific-volume} follow from
\cref{eq:implicit-tangent}.  Applying the quotient rule directly to the summed
variables, without introducing a separate aggregate-mass constitutive input,
then supplies the derivative in \cref{eq:nonlinear-biot}.

There are two distinct differentiation levels:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item The \emph{inner} derivative is the semantic constitutive partial in
       \cref{eq:implicit-tangent}.  The held-fixed variables are selected by the
       residual definition and derivative-property names.
 \item The \emph{outer} derivative is MOOSE AD with respect to nonlinear
       finite-element degrees of freedom.  The entries of the local matrix,
       right-hand side, Gaussian-elimination factors, solution \(\mbf y_{,J}\),
       specific-volume tangent, and \(B\) all remain AD-valued.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the local implicit-function theorem is embedded inside the global AD
graph.  Extracting raw scalar values during the arithmetic would destroy the
second derivative level.  Raw values are used only to select a pivot, test for
singularity, and report a diagnostic constraint norm.

The local solve uses partial-pivoted dense Gaussian elimination.  This choice is
appropriate because the number of constitutive variables at a quadrature point
is small and fixed by the registered phase/component closure.  A pivot tolerance
rejects singular local states.  The global Jacobian is subsequently checked by
a PETSc finite-difference Jacobian comparison, including the \(J_s\), \(p_E\),
and implicit-state paths.
