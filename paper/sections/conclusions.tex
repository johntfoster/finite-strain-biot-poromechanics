\section{Conclusions}

We implemented the finite-deformation Biot coefficient as a constrained
constitutive tangent derived from solid phase/component material conservation.
The local implicit-function solve is performed with AD scalars, preserving the
outer MOOSE chain rule through the state-dependent coefficient in the global
residual.  This nested construction separates the fixed-equivalent-pressure
constitutive partial from the total finite-element derivative and avoids an
independent user-specified Biot function.

The implementation recovers the classical limit, preserves intrinsic-density
and specific-volume inverse consistency, and passes analytic, finite-difference,
small-strain, endpoint, storage, undrained, and coupled Jacobian tests. For the
nonreacting Lawal--Kim data, calibration of a positive three-parameter crack-
compliance law to the measured drained tangent modulus reproduces the
coefficient calculated from those same data with errors below 0.02. This result
verifies the calibrated constitutive path and implicit differentiation. An
independent validation requires separate calibration observables or declared
holdout pressures together with a pressure-controlled boundary-value solve.

The resulting implementation supplies the constrained tangent and global AD
path required for coupled calculations in which pressure, deformation,
composition, and reaction state evolve simultaneously. Because
the coefficient remains inside the AD graph, these extensions require new
constitutive constraints and verification rather than hand-derived global
Jacobians.
