\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We derived a closed-form finite-deformation Biot coefficient for a saturated
solid with matched logarithmic skeleton and mineral laws and plastic
distention.  True deformation
measures intrinsic solid volume change, while distention determines its
occupation of mixture volume.  Solid mass and the mineral EOS constrain the
local state.  Implicit differentiation and analytical elimination give
the coefficient directly in the current states, with no state derivatives in
its evaluation.  A scalar equation still determines mineral volume.  The
coefficient connects the two effective stresses and supplies the pressure
contribution to momentum.

The constitutive derivative defining the coefficient holds the current
plastic state fixed.  The active return mapping evolves that state, and the
outer AD derivative retains its effect on both stress and coefficient.  An
implicit exponential update enforces flow and yield consistency at the same
returned state.  Independent mass, EOS, energy-derived stress, flow,
determinant, and derivative checks verify this construction on the smooth
Drucker--Prager branch.

The material-point results show how plastic distention carries loading history
into the coefficient and changes its unloading response.  At nonzero pore
pressure, that change feeds back into plastic flow through the single-prime
stress.  The direction and magnitude of the effect follow from the chosen
energy and flow law.  The coefficient is therefore part of the evolving
constitutive response, with a role in subsequent deformation as well as in
the current pressure contribution to stress.

The coupled finite-element calculations verify the elastic specialization
against the Mandel pressure and displacement solution and resolve coefficient
evolution during finite compression.  These results separate active
constitutive verification, the coupled elastic benchmark, numerical
sensitivity, and physical validation.  The next extension is an active
poroplastic boundary-value problem with consistent plastic contributions to
water storage, followed by calibration and comparison with material data.
