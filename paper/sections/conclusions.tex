\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The formulation solves the solid partial-density balance in spatial form and
pulls it back to the solid reference configuration for finite-element
assembly.  The local constitutive update supplies intrinsic solid density and
the solid volume fraction follows algebraically from the partial and intrinsic
densities.  The water
balance is likewise posed spatially;
water pressure determines intrinsic water density through its barotropic EOS.
The finite-deformation coefficient follows from the fixed-pressure derivative
of the local mineral distension.  That derivative is obtained by
implicit differentiation of the complete local constitutive residual system at
fixed intrinsic phase pressure and referential solid mass \(J\rho_s\).  The intrinsic
pressure is the common solid/fluid pressure, the negative mean intrinsic solid
stress \(\bar p_s=-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\bar{\bs\sigma}_s\), while the
mineral distension \(\bar J\) carries its intrinsic-density response.  The Biot
transform reconstructs
\(\bs\sigma^{\prime}=\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}+(1-B)p\mbf I\), while the
momentum residual uses the total stress
\(\bs\sigma=\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}-Bp\mbf I\).

The implementation uses AD scalar operations for the local constitutive
residual derivatives, implicit tangent, and global balance residuals.
The elastic state and its direct tangent are independent verification oracles;
the coefficient passed to momentum comes from the fixed-pressure constitutive
tangent.
The coefficient agrees with
\eqref{eq:elastic-finite-deformation-biot-coefficient}; the solid referential
mass and local mineral law close to their reported tolerances; and the outer
Jacobian is checked against a PETSc finite-difference comparison.  These tests
verify the constitutive derivative and its propagation through the Newton
system.

The solid--water Mandel benchmark uses a prescribed platen displacement.
Continuous Q2 displacement and Q1 water
pressure agree with the Cheng--Detournay pressure and lateral-displacement
profiles to the reported discretization differences and recover the applied
platen load.  A separate ten-percent
compression continuation decreases the spatially averaged coefficient from
\(0.600\) to \(0.51417\).  Together, the two calculations exercise
finite-deformation momentum, solid mass conservation, mineral compressibility,
nonlinear water accumulation, Darcy transport, and the nonlinear Biot transform in
one monolithic solve.  These reported results exercise the inactive-mechanism
limit of the inelastic update of Sec.~\ref{sec:plastic-loading-incremental-rate}:
with no active yield (\(\Delta\gamma=0\), \(a^p=1\)), the poroplastic return map
collapses exactly onto the elastic closure above, which the agreement verifies.
The ten-percent continuation is a synthetic demonstration rather than physical
validation.

The paper's contribution is the inelastic construction itself: the aggregate
elastic--plastic split with scalar pore allocation \(a^p\), the single-prime
Drucker--Prager-type driving stress that requires the implicit coefficient, and
the rate-independent backward-Euler return map embedded in the fixed-pressure
automatic-differentiation local system of
Sec.~\ref{sec:implicit-ad-implementation}.  The Mandel evidence exercises the
inactive-mechanism (elastic) limit of that system and verifies its collapse onto
the closed-form elastic closure.  At the material level the inelastic update is
now activated and verified separately: the single-point demonstration of
Sec.~\ref{sec:poroplastic-demonstration} returns to the yield surface with
\(a^p>1\), and the fixed-pressure correction
\(B=1-(1-B_{\mathrm{el}})/a^p\) reproduces the elastic coefficient exactly
when yield is inactive and raises the coefficient by up to \(0.043\) over the
elastic value at \(30\%\) compression.  At nonzero pore pressure that
correction is not inert: the implicit coefficient feeds back into the plastic
driving stress through the \((1-B)p\mbf I\) term of the Biot transform, and
the material-level demonstration of
Sec.~\ref{sec:poroplastic-b-feedback} shows that holding the coefficient at
its elastic value over-predicts the plastic increment (by about \(3\%\) at
\(400\,\mathrm{MPa}\) pore pressure and \(20\%\) compression in the
reported range).  The material-level machinery is itself verified in
Sec.~\ref{sec:poroplastic-verification}: the general-path oracle shows by
automatic differentiation that the reduced coefficient
\(B=1-(1-B_{\mathrm{el}})/a^p\) is the fixed-pressure tangent of the active
local system, and the unload-branch check shows the coefficient measured on
an unloading branch is the fixed-history tangent at the plastic state, which
differs from the elastic coefficient.  The next stage is the coupled,
momentum-active demonstration of this
activated update at the finite-element scale, together with the convergence
and Jacobian checks that accompany it.  Independent
physical validation against a particular rock is a separate stage.
