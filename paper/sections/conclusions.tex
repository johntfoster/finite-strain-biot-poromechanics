\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We derived a closed-form finite-deformation Biot coefficient for a saturated
solid with logarithmic skeleton and mineral laws and plastic distention.
A scalar equation determines mineral volume, and the Biot coefficient follows
directly from that volume, total volume, and pore pressure.  The expression
recovers \(B_0=1-K/K_s\) at the reference state and connects the pressure
contribution to total stress with the stress that drives plastic flow.

Plastic distention carries loading history into the Biot coefficient.  The
material-point calculations show that unloading follows a different path
from the virgin response at the same total deformation.  At nonzero
pressure, the change in the Biot coefficient feeds back into the driving
stress and restrains dilation for the chosen energy and flow law.  The implicit return
mapping advances these quantities together, and automatic differentiation
retains their coupled change in the Newton Jacobian.  Independent checks of
mass conservation, the energy-derived stress, the flow rule, yield
consistency, and the derivatives verify the update on the smooth
Drucker--Prager branch.

The coupled finite-element calculations verify the elastic specialization
against the Mandel pressure and horizontal-displacement solution.  A larger
compression test shows how the Biot coefficient varies across the specimen
during drainage.  Mesh and time-step comparisons measure numerical sensitivity;
these calculations provide implementation verification for the chosen laws.
The next steps are to verify coupled flow with plastic deformation,
including its contribution to water storage, and to calibrate and test the
model against material data.
