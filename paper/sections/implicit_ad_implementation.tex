\section{Numerical implementation and verification}
\label{sec:implicit-ad-implementation}

The numerical solution has two levels.  At each finite-element integration
point, a local constitutive calculation determines mineral volume, the Biot
coefficient, and any plastic deformation.  The global calculation uses these
quantities to solve momentum balance and water mass balance.  Solid mass
conservation determines partial density directly from the deformation.
Automatic differentiation (AD) applies the chain rule through these
calculations to obtain the derivatives required by Newton's method.  The
local constitutive Jacobian contains derivatives with respect to the local
unknowns; the global finite-element Jacobian contains derivatives of the
assembled balance residuals with respect to the nodal fields.

\subsection{Mineral state and plastic update}

At each integration point, the elastic or poroplastic constitutive update
solves the nonlinear scalar residual
\eqref{eq:verification-mineral-factors} using
\(x=\ln\bar J\) as the numerical unknown, restricting the solution to satisfy
\eqref{eq:trace-mineral-stability-domain}.  We first find an interval whose
endpoints give opposite signs of the residual and throughout which its
slope is positive.  Bisection then determines the mineral solution.  Newton updates evaluated
with AD variables then recover the solution's derivatives with respect to
elastic volume and pore pressure, which enter the constitutive and global
Newton Jacobians.  After convergence,
the update recovers \(\bar J=\exp x\).  Intrinsic density follows from
\(\bar\rho_s=\bar\rho_{s0}/\bar J\), and the volume fraction is
\(\phi_s=\phi_{s0}\bar J/J\), with solid partial density
\(\rho_s=\rho_{s0}/J\) fixed by solid mass conservation.
The update then evaluates \eqref{eq:poroplastic-biot-correction} and checks
positive volumes, \(0<\phi_s<1\), positive mineral stiffness, and a positive
scalar derivative.

When plastic flow occurs, the seven equations
\eqref{eq:implicit-plastic-flow-increment}--\eqref{eq:discrete-consistency}
determine the six independent components of \(\bs\Delta^p\) and
\(\Delta\gamma\).  Each Newton iteration recomputes \(J^e\), mineral
volume, \(B\), and driving stress from the candidate plastic state.
For positive \(H\), the cohesion is evaluated at
\(\gamma_n+\Delta\gamma\); its derivative therefore enters the same
Jacobian of the local plastic equations.  Both \(\mbf F^p_n\) and \(\gamma_n\) remain
fixed during the current solve.
Forward-mode AD carries derivatives alongside the calculated values and
forms the local Jacobian with respect to these seven unknowns.  Newton
iteration first determines their converged values.  We then differentiate
the converged residual equations with respect to the global fields, holding
the previous time step's history fixed.  Solving the resulting linear systems
with the same local Jacobian gives the derivatives of the converged plastic
update.  These are the outer AD derivatives passed to the global calculation.

To evaluate the matrix exponential, we first scale its argument, evaluate a
Taylor polynomial through order 20, and then recover the exponential by
repeated squaring.  All operations retain AD derivatives.  This procedure
avoids computing derivatives of eigenvectors, which are not uniquely defined
when eigenvalues coincide.

The elastic and poroplastic MOOSE materials share the scalar mineral solver
and closed-form Biot coefficient.  The mineral solve uses \(J^e=J/a^p\), while
the denominator of \eqref{eq:poroplastic-biot-correction} uses total \(J\).
The elastic specialization sets \(a^p=1\).
All current-state calculations retain their
AD derivatives.  The converged plastic factor is stored as ordinary values
for the next time step, while its current AD value remains in stress and
Biot coefficient calculations.  Differentiating the converged discrete update
supplies the derivative of the update that Newton iteration requires
\citep{simotaylor1985,miehe1996}.  MOOSE carries that derivative into the
assembled residual Jacobian \citep{lindsayetal2021ad}.

\subsection{Phase balances and finite-element residuals}
\label{sec:fluid-storage-and-transport}
\label{sec:weak-residuals-and-spaces}

The coupled boundary-value calculations use the elastic and poroplastic
specializations of the preceding constitutive model.  The reference domain
moves with the solid, and neither phase has a mass source.  Integrating
solid mass conservation in time determines its current partial density:
\begin{equation}
 \rho_s=\frac{\rho_{s0}}{J},\qquad
 \phi_s=\frac{\phi_{s0}\bar J}{J}.
 \label{eq:conservative-solid-density}
\end{equation}
Here \(\rho_{s0}=\phi_{s0}\bar\rho_{s0}\) is the solid mass per unit
reference mixture volume.  These identities hold at each integration point;
mineral compression and plastic distention enter through the local solution
for \(\bar J\).

The water mass stored in a material region depends on its pore volume
and water density.  We take water density to depend only on pressure,
using the barotropic equation of state
\begin{equation}
 \bar\rho_f(p)=\bar\rho_{f0}
 \exp\left(\frac{p-p_0}{K_f}\right).
 \label{eq:barotropic-fluid-density}
\end{equation}
Here \(K_f\) is the constant fluid bulk modulus and
\((p_0,\bar\rho_{f0})\) is its reference state.  Substituting
\eqref{eq:conservative-solid-density} into the pore volume per reference
mixture volume, \(J\phi_f=J(1-\phi_s)\), gives
\(J-\phi_{s0}\bar J\).  Let \(\mbf w_f\)
be the fluid mass flux relative to the solid.  Its reference flux is
\(\mbf W_f=J\mbf F^{-1}\mbf w_f\).  The fluid balance is
\begin{equation}
 \left.\pder{}{t}\right|_{\mbf X}
 \left[\bar\rho_f(p)\left(J-\phi_{s0}\bar J\right)\right]
 +\nabla_{\mbf X}\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf W_f=0.
 \label{eq:reference-fluid-mass-balance}
\end{equation}
For constant isotropic permeability \(\kappa\) in the current configuration,
dynamic viscosity \(\mu_f\), and zero gravity, Darcy flow in reference
coordinates is
\begin{equation}
 \mbf W_f=-\bar\rho_fJ\mbf F^{-1}
 \frac{\kappa}{\mu_f}\mbf F^{-T}\nabla_{\mbf X}p.
 \label{eq:reference-darcy-flux}
\end{equation}
The constitutive calculation supplies total first Piola stress
\(\mbf P=\mbf P^{\prime\prime}-BpJ\mbf F^{-T}\).
This stress already includes pressure coupling.  The same mineral volume
determines the stored water mass.

Let \(\Omega_0\) be the solid reference domain, and let \(\delta\mbf u\)
and \(\delta p\) be test functions for displacement
\(\mbf u=\mbf x-\mbf X\) and pressure.  Let
\(\Gamma_t\) and \(\Gamma_f\) be the portions of the reference boundary with
prescribed nominal traction
\(\mbf T_0=\mbf P\mbf N\) and prescribed outward referential fluid mass
flux \(M_f=\mbf W_f\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf N\), respectively, where
\(\mbf N\) is the outward unit normal to the reference boundary.
The test functions vanish where their respective fields are prescribed.
With zero body force, the residual equations are
\begin{align}
 R_u(\delta\mbf u)
 &=\int_{\Omega_0}\nabla_{\mbf X}\delta\mbf u:
 \mbf P\,\dd V_0
 -\int_{\Gamma_t}\delta\mbf u\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf T_0\dd A_0=0,
 \label{eq:momentum-weak-residual}\\
 R_p(\delta p)
 &=\int_{\Omega_0}\left\{\delta p\pder{}{t}
 \left[\bar\rho_f(p)\left(J-\phi_{s0}\bar J\right)\right]
 -\nabla_{\mbf X}\delta p\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf W_f\right\}\dd V_0 \notag\\
 &\quad+\int_{\Gamma_f}\delta p\,M_f\dd A_0=0.
 \label{eq:fluid-pressure-weak-residual}
\end{align}
The time derivative follows a solid material point.  The discrete fields use
\begin{equation}
 \mbf u^h\in[\mathcal Q_2]^2,\qquad p^h\in\mathcal Q_1,
 \label{eq:finite-element-spaces}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal Q_k\) consists of continuous functions that are polynomial
of degree at most \(k\) in each element coordinate.  Displacement therefore
uses quadratic interpolation and pressure uses continuous linear
interpolation without stabilization.  Solid partial density is evaluated
from \eqref{eq:conservative-solid-density} rather than interpolated as an
independent field.

Backward Euler approximates the change of the complete stored water mass
between consecutive time levels.  With \(\Delta t=t_n-t_{n-1}\),
\begin{align}
 &\left.\pder{}{t}\right|_{\mbf X}
 \left[\bar\rho_f(p)\left(J-\phi_{s0}\bar J\right)\right]_{t_n}
 \notag\\
 &\qquad\simeq
 \frac{\bar\rho_f(p^n)\left(J^n-\phi_{s0}\bar J^n\right)
 -\bar\rho_f(p^{n-1})\left(J^{n-1}-\phi_{s0}\bar J^{n-1}\right)}
 {\Delta t}.
 \label{eq:conservative-fluid-mass-step}
\end{align}
The previous mass is stored at the integration point.  At the current time,
the local update determines \(\bar J^n\) from deformation, pressure, and
the converged plastic state.  Its dependence on all current unknowns remains
in the AD Jacobian.  Initialization evaluates the stored mass from the
specified initial deformation, pressure, and constitutive state.

Plastic fluid storage follows from this same mass difference.
The mineral equation uses \(J^e=J/a^p\), so a plastic change in
\(a^p\) changes \(\bar J\) and therefore the pore volume.
Positive plastic distention increases pore volume at fixed total volume
and pressure on the retained stable branch.  The converged plastic update
thus contributes to both the accumulated mass and its Newton derivatives.

For a sealed body, summing the discrete fluid equations conserves its
integrated water mass up to the nonlinear solve tolerance and numerical
integration errors.  With drainage, mass change is balanced by the
boundary discharge accumulated with the same end-step time quadrature.
The pressure-residual reaction supplies the discrete discharge at prescribed
pressure boundaries.  A Darcy flux reconstructed from pressure gradients is
checked separately because its spatial discretization error can differ from
the algebraic balance error.  Independent finite-difference checks verify
the constitutive storage terms and the assembled AD Jacobian.
