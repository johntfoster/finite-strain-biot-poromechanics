\section{Closed-form evaluation and implicit numerical implementation}
\label{sec:implicit-ad-implementation}

The constitutive calculation contains two distinct derivatives.  The definition
of \(B\) holds the current plastic state fixed while perturbing skeleton
volume at fixed pressure.  The global Newton derivative includes the change
of that plastic state during the return mapping.  Analytical elimination gives the former in closed form.  We preserve the
state dependence of that expression in the latter with automatic
differentiation, and retain the general implicit tangent as an independent
verification.

\subsection{The constrained mineral tangent}
\label{sec:fixed-pressure-constrained-tangent}

At a quadrature point, let \(\mbf y=(\bar\rho_s,\phi_s)\) contain the
intrinsic density and solid volume fraction.  For prescribed \(J\), \(p\),
referential mass \(J\rho_s\), and current \(\mbf F^p\), the local state
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \mbf R(\mbf y;J,p,J\rho_s,\mbf F^p)=\mbf0.
 \label{eq:general-local-constitutive-system}
\end{equation}
The two residuals enforce the partial-density identity and mineral EOS,
\begin{align}
 R_m&=J\phi_s\bar\rho_s-J\rho_s=0,
 \label{eq:local-material-mass-constraint}\\
 R_e&=\bar p_s(\bar\rho_s,J^e)-p=0,
 \qquad J^e=\frac{J}{a^p}.
 \label{eq:local-mineral-eos-constraint}
\end{align}
The product \(J\rho_s\) is one independent argument of the local problem.
It remains fixed when \(J\) is perturbed.  On a positive-density branch with
\(\partial\bar p_s/\partial\bar\rho_s>0\), the EOS determines the local
intrinsic density and mass conservation determines the volume fraction.
Their implicit tangent provides an independent route to the coefficient.

Differentiating the residual system gives
\begin{equation}
 \pder{\mbf R}{\mbf y}
 \left.\pder{\mbf y}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s,\mbf F^p}
 =-\pder{\mbf R}{J}.
 \label{eq:implicit-local-state-tangent}
\end{equation}
For the physical density variables, its entries are
\begin{equation}
 \pder{\mbf R}{\mbf y}
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 J\phi_s & J\bar\rho_s\\
 \partial\bar p_s/\partial\bar\rho_s & 0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \pder{\mbf R}{J}
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 \phi_s\bar\rho_s\\
 (1/a^p)\partial\bar p_s/\partial J^e
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:elastic-local-residual-derivatives}
\end{equation}
The diagnostic material solves this small linear system.  Explicit
elimination gives
\begin{equation}
 \left.\pder{\mbf y}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s,\mbf F^p}
 =\frac{1}{J}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -J^e\dfrac{\partial\bar p_s/\partial J^e}{\partial\bar p_s/\partial\bar\rho_s}\\[6pt]
 \phi_s\left(\dfrac{J^e}{\bar\rho_s}\dfrac{\partial\bar p_s/\partial J^e}{\partial\bar p_s/\partial\bar\rho_s}-1\right)
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:explicit-local-state-tangent}
\end{equation}
The chain rule then gives
\begin{align}
 \left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s,\mbf F^p}
 &=-\frac{\bar\rho_{s0}}{\bar\rho_s^2}
 \left.\pder{\bar\rho_s}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s,\mbf F^p},
 \label{eq:constrained-mineral-jacobian-tangent}\\
 B&=1-\phi_{s0}
 \left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s,\mbf F^p}.
 \label{eq:general-implicit-biot-coefficient}
\end{align}
These relations recover \eqref{eq:poroplastic-biot-correction}.  For the
matched logarithmic law, it is convenient to evaluate the dimensionless
residual
\begin{equation}
 R_e=\ln\left(\frac{\bar\rho_s}{\bar\rho_{s0}}\right)
 +\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\ln J^e
 -\left(1-\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)\frac{p\bar J}{K_s}=0.
 \label{eq:numerical-dimensionless-mineral-residual}
\end{equation}
It equals \((1-K/(\phi_{s0}K_s))\bar J/K_s\) times the pressure
residual in \eqref{eq:local-mineral-eos-constraint}.  This positive scaling
preserves the implicit tangent on the equilibrium state.  Its Jacobian
entries are
\begin{equation}
 \pder{R_e}{\bar\rho_s}
 =\frac{1}{\bar\rho_s}\left[
 1+\left(1-\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)\frac{p\bar J}{K_s}\right],
 \qquad
 \pder{R_e}{J}=\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_sJ},
 \qquad \pder{R_e}{\phi_s}=0.
 \label{eq:numerical-solid-eos-derivatives}
\end{equation}
Substituting these entries in the two-state solve gives
\eqref{eq:poroplastic-biot-correction} exactly, including at a fixed plastic
state.  A more general mineral constitutive system retains the same implicit
construction when its state Jacobian is nonsingular.

The production material solves \eqref{eq:verification-mineral-factors} in
\(\ln\bar J\) on the stable branch, then evaluates the closed-form
coefficient.  Bracketing locates the scalar root; AD-valued Newton corrections
at the converged root retain its first and nested second derivatives.
Intrinsic density is stored in reference-normalized form.  The volume fraction
is computed from the current solved partial density.  The local mass residual
checks this reconstruction, while the independently evolved global balance
is checked through its referential-mass drift.  Admissibility is enforced for
positive volumes, \(0<\phi_s<1\), positive mineral stiffness, and a positive
scalar-state Jacobian.

\subsection{Active plastic update and outer differentiation}

For an active increment, the current \(\mbf F^p\) in
\eqref{eq:general-local-constitutive-system} is supplied by the coupled flow
and consistency equations
\eqref{eq:implicit-plastic-flow-increment}--\eqref{eq:discrete-consistency}.
Each candidate plastic state changes \(J^e\), the mineral state, and the
closed-form coefficient.  The resulting coefficient enters the reconstructed
stress before yield and flow are reevaluated.  Thus the local solve closes
the feedback between plastic deformation and pressure coupling.

The MOOSE implementation separates the elastic verification provider,
the general constrained-tangent material, and the active poroplastic material.
The elastic provider supplies the state, closed-form coefficient, and
residual derivatives; the general material independently solves
\eqref{eq:implicit-local-state-tangent} with AD-valued dense linear algebra.
The poroplastic material evaluates the same closed-form coefficient inside
its local return mapping.  Its seven-equation
Newton system uses a nested AD seed to differentiate the flow and consistency
residuals with respect to the local unknowns.  The matrix exponential is
evaluated by scaling and squaring with a Taylor expansion.

All current-state calculations use outer AD scalars.  The converged plastic
factor is copied to ordinary storage only for use as previous-step history;
its current AD value remains in the stress and coefficient.  Differentiating
the converged discrete update supplies the algorithmic response needed by
Newton iteration \citep{simotaylor1985,miehe1996}.  MOOSE carries this
response into the assembled residual Jacobian \citep{lindsayetal2021ad}.
The derivative of the active update therefore includes the evolution of
\(\mbf F^p\), although the constitutive derivative defining \(B\) holds
that current state fixed.

\subsection{Phase balances and finite-element residuals}
\label{sec:water-storage-and-transport}
\label{sec:weak-residuals-and-spaces}

The coupled boundary-value verification uses the elastic specialization of
the preceding constitutive model.  Solid mass evolves as a global balance-law
field; the local mineral state determines the solid volume fraction.  Water
storage uses that pore volume and the barotropic EOS
\begin{align}
 \bar\rho_f(p)&=\bar\rho_{f0}
 \exp\left(\frac{p-p_0}{K_f}\right).
 \label{eq:barotropic-water-density}
\end{align}
Here \(K_f\) is the constant water bulk modulus and
\((p_0,\bar\rho_{f0})\) is its reference state.  Let \(\mbf w_f\)
be the water mass flux relative to the solid.  Its reference flux is
\(\mbf W_f=J\mbf F^{-1}\mbf w_f\).  The water balance is
\begin{equation}
 \left.\pder{}{t}\right|_{\mbf X}
 \left[J(1-\phi_s)\bar\rho_f\right]
 +\nabla_{\mbf X}\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf W_f=0.
 \label{eq:reference-water-mass-balance}
\end{equation}
For isotropic permeability \(\kappa\), dynamic viscosity \(\mu_f\), and
zero gravity, Darcy flow pulls back to
\begin{equation}
 \mbf W_f=-\bar\rho_fJ\mbf F^{-1}
 \frac{\kappa}{\mu_f}\mbf F^{-T}\nabla_{\mbf X}p.
 \label{eq:reference-darcy-flux}
\end{equation}
The coefficient appears explicitly in momentum, while the same mineral-volume
response enters fluid accumulation through \(\phi_s\).

Let \(\Omega_0\) be the solid reference domain, and let \(\mbf w\), \(q\),
and \(\eta\) be test functions for displacement \(\mbf u=\mbf x-\mbf X\),
pressure, and solid partial density.  With zero body force and the boundary
terms omitted, the residuals are
\begin{align}
 R_u(\mbf w)
 &=\int_{\Omega_0}\nabla_{\mbf X}\mbf w:
 \left(\mbf P^{\prime\prime}-BpJ\mbf F^{-T}\right)\dd V_0,
 \label{eq:momentum-weak-residual}\\
 R_p(q)
 &=\int_{\Omega_0}q\pder{}{t}
 \left[J(1-\phi_s)\bar\rho_f(p)\right]\dd V_0
 -\int_{\Omega_0}\nabla_{\mbf X}q\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf W_f\dd V_0,
 \label{eq:water-pressure-weak-residual}\\
 R_{\rho_s}(\eta)
 &=\int_{\Omega_0}\eta\left(\dot J\rho_s+J\dot\rho_s\right)\dd V_0.
 \label{eq:solid-mass-weak-residual}
\end{align}
The overdot follows the solid reference coordinate.  The discrete fields use
\begin{equation}
 \mbf u_h\in[\mathcal Q_2]^2,
 \qquad p_h\in\mathcal Q_1,
 \qquad \rho_{s,h}\in\mathcal Q_2,
 \label{eq:finite-element-spaces}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal Q_k\) is the continuous tensor-product polynomial space.
The pressure is continuous Q1, with no enrichment or stabilization.  The
solid field is stored as \(\rho_s/\rho_{s0}\) for conditioning.

Backward Euler supplies the rates in the reported calculations.  The kernels
use the continuous chain-rule expansion of the solid and water accumulation
rates with MOOSE time-integrator derivatives.  This discretization generally
differs from a backward difference of the complete accumulation product.
Referential mass drift is therefore checked independently and under time
refinement.  A BDF2 regression exercises the same rate implementation.

\subsection{Independent derivative checks}
\label{sec:nested-automatic-differentiation}

The production closed-form coefficient is compared with the independent
two-state implicit tangent and with centered differences of true volume at
fixed pressure and referential mass.
At plastic states, the centered-difference perturbation also fixes the
current \(\mbf F^p\).  These tests check the derivative that defines \(B\).

A separate PETSc comparison checks the outer AD Jacobian.  The elastic test
assembles momentum, solid mass, water storage, and Darcy flow.  The active
plastic test uses algebraic material residuals containing the returned stress
invariants, plastic distention, and Biot coefficient at nonzero pressure and
unequal principal stretches.  Its perturbations exercise the active return
mapping.  These tests establish local and outer derivative consistency within
their respective scopes; an active coupled poroplastic boundary-value problem
requires a further verification of plastic contributions to fluid storage.
