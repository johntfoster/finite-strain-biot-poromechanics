\section{Constitutive update and finite-element implementation}
\label{sec:implicit-ad-implementation}

\subsection{Local fixed-pressure update}
\label{sec:fixed-pressure-constrained-tangent}

The additional global solid field is the partial density \(\rho_s\).  At a
quadrature point, its referential value \(J\rho_s\) is passed to the
constitutive update together with \(J\), the common intrinsic pressure \(p\),
and the previous constitutive history \(\mc H_n\).  The current local
constitutive state \(\mbf y\) contains the solid intrinsic density and any
inelastic internal variables, but not the solid volume fraction.  It satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \mbf R(\mbf y;J,p,J\rho_s,\mc H_n)=\mbf0.
 \label{eq:general-local-constitutive-system}
\end{equation}
The residual vector contains the intrinsic-pressure equation and the evolution
and consistency equations required by the selected constitutive model.  For
inelastic response, these additional equations may include a flow rule,
hardening laws, and an active yield constraint.  The global solid balance
determines \(J\rho_s\), the constitutive system determines \(\bar\rho_s\), and
\eqref{eq:solid-volume-fraction-relation} then gives \(\phi_s\).  When a
constitutive equation depends on volume fraction, \(\phi_s=\rho_s/\bar\rho_s\)
is substituted before forming \(\mbf R\).  Thus its dependence on \(J\),
\(J\rho_s\), and \(\mbf y\) is included by the chain rule without introducing a
separate material-mass residual.  In the partial derivatives below, the product
\(J\rho_s\) is treated as one independent constitutive argument and is held
fixed as \(J\) varies.

After the local update converges, differentiation of
\eqref{eq:general-local-constitutive-system} at fixed \(p\), fixed
\(J\rho_s\), and fixed previous history gives
\begin{equation}
 \left.\pder{\mbf y}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s,\mc H_n}
 =-\left(\pder{\mbf R}{\mbf y}\right)^{-1}
 \pder{\mbf R}{J}.
 \label{eq:implicit-local-state-tangent}
\end{equation}
For a nonsmooth inelastic update, this derivative is formed on the active set
selected by the converged return mapping.  Equation
\eqref{eq:implicit-local-state-tangent} differentiates the converged residual
system rather than the sequence of local Newton iterates.

The mineral distension \(\bar J=\bar\rho_{s0}/\bar\rho_s\) is evaluated from
the converged state.  Its constrained derivative follows by the chain rule,
\begin{equation}
 \left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s,\mc H_n}
 =\pder{\bar J}{\mbf y}\mathbin{\cdot}
  \left.\pder{\mbf y}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s,\mc H_n},
 \label{eq:constrained-mineral-jacobian-tangent}
\end{equation}
and the general coefficient returned to the stress calculation is
\begin{equation}
 B
 =1-\phi_{s0}
 \left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s,\mc H_n}.
 \label{eq:general-implicit-biot-coefficient}
\end{equation}
Thus an inelastic model changes \(B\) through every active component of
\(\mbf y_J\), rather than through a prescribed elastic modulus ratio.

The reported elastic calculations provide a closed-form test of this general
path.  Their only local state variable is \(\mbf y=(\bar\rho_s)\), and its
constitutive residual is
\begin{equation}
 R_e(\bar\rho_s;J,p)
 =\ln\left(\frac{\bar\rho_s}{\bar\rho_{s0}}\right)
 -\frac{1}{K_s}
  \left(p-\frac{K\ln J}{\phi_{s0}J}\right)=0.
 \label{eq:local-mineral-eos-constraint}
\end{equation}
The derivatives supplied to \eqref{eq:implicit-local-state-tangent} are
\begin{equation}
 \pder{R_e}{\bar\rho_s}=\frac{1}{\bar\rho_s},
 \qquad
 \pder{R_e}{J}
 =\frac{K(1-\ln J)}{\phi_{s0}K_sJ^2}.
 \label{eq:elastic-local-residual-derivatives}
\end{equation}
The scalar implicit tangent and
\eqref{eq:constrained-mineral-jacobian-tangent} then give
\begin{equation}
 \left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s}
 =\frac{K\,\bar J(1-\ln J)}{\phi_{s0}K_sJ^2},
 \label{eq:elastic-mineral-jacobian-derivative}
\end{equation}
and therefore \eqref{eq:elastic-finite-deformation-biot-coefficient}.  The
explicit elastic expression is used only as a verification oracle; the
coefficient passed to momentum is obtained from the general dense solve.

The implementation evaluates the local residual derivatives, the implicit solve
in \eqref{eq:implicit-local-state-tangent}, and the chain rule in
\eqref{eq:constrained-mineral-jacobian-tangent} with AD scalar operations.  The
resulting \(B\) remains in the outer MOOSE AD graph, which retains its
dependence on displacement, pressure, solid partial density, and constitutive
history without a hand-coded finite-element Jacobian
\citep{lindsayetal2021ad}.  Admissible states require \(J>0\),
\(\bar\rho_s>0\), and \(0<\rho_s/\bar\rho_s<1\).

\subsection{Weak residuals and approximation spaces}
\label{sec:weak-residuals-and-spaces}

Let \(\Omega_0\) be the solid reference domain and \(\dd V_0\) its volume
measure.  Write the skeleton displacement as \(\mbf u=\mbf x-\mbf X\), and let
\(\mbf w\), \(q\), and \(\eta\) be test functions for displacement, water
pressure, and solid partial density, respectively.  With zero body
force and the natural boundary terms omitted, the momentum residual is
\begin{equation}
 R_u(\mbf w)
 =\int_{\Omega_0}
 \nabla_{\mbf X}\mbf w:
 \left(\mbf P^{\prime\prime}-BpJ\mbf F^{-T}\right)\dd V_0.
 \label{eq:momentum-weak-residual}
\end{equation}
Multiplication of the water balance by \(q\) and integration of the relative
flux term by parts gives
\begin{equation}
 R_p(q)
 =\int_{\Omega_0}q
 \frac{\partial}{\partial t}
 \left[J(1-\phi_s)\bar\rho_f(p)\right]\dd V_0
 -\int_{\Omega_0}
 \nabla_{\mbf X}q\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf W_f\dd V_0.
 \label{eq:water-pressure-weak-residual}
\end{equation}
The spatial solid mass balance, pulled back to \(\Omega_0\), gives the third
global residual,
\begin{equation}
 R_{\rho_s}(\eta)
 =\int_{\Omega_0}\eta
 \frac{\partial}{\partial t}
 \left(J\rho_s\right)\dd V_0
 =\int_{\Omega_0}\eta
 \left(\dot J\,\rho_s+J\dot\rho_s\right)\dd V_0.
 \label{eq:solid-mass-weak-residual}
\end{equation}
The second equality is the continuous chain rule used by the reported time
kernel.  It is equivalent to the spatial balance at the continuum level but is
not, in general, an exact backward difference of \(J\rho_s\).  Retaining
\(\rho_s\) as a global field keeps solid mass as a balance-law residual and
permits spatially varying initial mass, phase sources, and reactive solid
production.  For the source-free benchmark,
\(J\rho_s=\rho_{s0}\) is also an independent
discretization check.  The implementation stores \(\rho_s/\rho_{s0}\) for
numerical conditioning; this constant scaling does not change the balance or
introduce an additional continuum variable.

The discrete spaces are
\begin{equation}
 \mbf u_h\in[\mathcal Q_2]^2,
 \qquad
 p_h\in\mathcal Q_1,
 \qquad
 \rho_{s,h}\in\mathcal Q_2.
 \label{eq:finite-element-spaces}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathcal Q_k\) is the continuous tensor-product polynomial space of
degree \(k\) in each coordinate.  Backward Euler discretizes the reported
calculations.  The solid kernel uses \(\dot J\) and \(\dot\rho_s\); the water
kernel uses \(\dot J\), \(\dot p\), and the locally derived
\(\dot\phi_s\).  A BDF2 regression exercises the
same residual paths.  Conservative product differences and a time-refined
global mass audit are separate verification targets because the chain-rule
rates do not by themselves prove exact stepwise conservation.

\subsection{Derivative verification}
\label{sec:nested-automatic-differentiation}

The production coefficient is computed by the general constrained material
from \eqref{eq:implicit-local-state-tangent}--
\eqref{eq:general-implicit-biot-coefficient}.  It is compared with the
independently evaluated elastic expression
\eqref{eq:elastic-finite-deformation-biot-coefficient} and with centered
differences of \(\bar J\) with respect to \(J\) at fixed \(p\) and
\(J\rho_s\).
A PETSc finite-difference comparison of the assembled transient system tests
the outer AD path through momentum, solid mass, water storage, and the Biot
pressure term.  Across the four nonlinear states evaluated during the test
step, the maximum relative Frobenius difference is
\(3.8413\times10^{-8}\), and the maximum absolute Frobenius difference is
\(1.9220\times10^{-6}\).  These checks distinguish the local fixed-pressure
derivative from its propagation through the global Newton system.
