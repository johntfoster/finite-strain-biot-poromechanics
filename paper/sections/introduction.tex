\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Deformation of a saturated porous solid redistributes pore fluid and changes
the stress carried by the skeleton.  When the mineral is compressible, the
Biot coefficient determines how pore pressure enters that stress.  At finite
deformation, the coefficient evolves with the constitutive state, and plastic
changes in pore volume carry the loading history into its value.  This
evolution also affects the stress that drives further plastic flow.  We
develop an implicit construction that couples the finite-deformation Biot
coefficient to the local poroplastic update and retains its constitutive
dependence in a monolithic finite-element formulation.

The physical role of the coefficient follows from the distinction between
the drained skeleton stress and the plastic driving stress.  Foster and Xu
identify the Biot effective stress \(\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}\) and the
Terzaghi effective stress \(\bs\sigma^{\prime}\) as the fixed-pressure and
fixed-density effective stresses of a finite-deformation solid--water mixture
\citep{fosterxu2025}.  They are related by
\(\bs\sigma^{\prime}=\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}+(1-B)p\mbf I\), where
\(B\) is the Biot coefficient, \(p\) is the pore pressure, and \(\mbf I\)
is the identity tensor.  The drained response supplies the Biot effective
stress, while the dissipation of inelastic deformation identifies the
Terzaghi effective stress as the plastic driving stress
\citep{drumheller2000}.  For a compressible mineral at nonzero pore pressure,
the term \((1-B)p\mbf I\) therefore enters the evaluation of yield and flow.
The incompressible-mineral limit recovers \(B=1\), in which the two stresses
coincide.

The finite-deformation coefficient measures the change of mineral distension
with skeleton Jacobian at fixed pore pressure \citep{fosterxu2025}.  Its
small-strain limit recovers the familiar relation between the drained
skeleton and mineral bulk moduli \citep{biotwillis1957}.  Here, we evaluate
the finite-deformation derivative by implicit differentiation of the complete
local constitutive residual system at fixed intrinsic phase pressure,
referential solid mass, and previous constitutive history.  The inelastic
construction combines isochoric true-plastic flow of the solid material with
a scalar plastic pore allocation that changes the solid volume fraction.
Their flow, consistency, and hardening equations enter the local system, and
the converged return mapping selects the active set for differentiation.
The coefficient and plastic state are coupled: the coefficient determines the
pore-pressure contribution to the driving stress, and the plastic state
determines the constitutive response from which the coefficient is obtained.
The resulting coefficient carries the effect of plastic pore allocation into
subsequent loading and unloading.  Measurements of a stress-dependent Biot
coefficient during drained sandstone compaction provide a related
experimental context \citep{ingraham2017evolution}.

The numerical formulation connects this local constitutive response to the
phase balances.  Spatial solid mass conservation evolves the bulk solid
partial density, and the local constitutive update supplies the solid
intrinsic density.  Their ratio gives the solid volume fraction.  The water
balance uses this evolving pore volume together with a barotropic
pressure--density equation of state.  Thus the same constitutive state enters
both the stress and fluid accumulation.  A consistent global Newton
linearization must retain these dependencies together with the derivatives
of the implicit Biot tangent.

Automatic differentiation provides the connection between the local update
and the finite-element residual.  Residual evaluators templated on scalar
type supply coupled finite-strain poromechanics Jacobians
\citep{sunostien2013}, and the Multiphysics Object Oriented Simulation
Environment (MOOSE) provides this residual-based approach through automatic
differentiation \citep{gastonetal2009moose,lindsayetal2021ad}.  Consistent
algorithmic tangents connect local material updates to the global Newton
method \citep{simotaylor1985,miehe1996}, while large-deformation porous-material
formulations couple skeleton deformation, fluid transport, and constitutive
state evolution \citep{hongetal2008,macminnetal2016,dehghanizilian2021}.
We use an automatic-differentiation-valued local linear solve to obtain the
fixed-pressure constitutive tangent and preserve its outer dependence in
the global Jacobian.  The central computational contribution is this
propagation of the implicitly defined Biot coefficient through the coupled
stress and phase-balance residuals.

We first state the finite-deformation solid--water equations and develop the
inelastic constitutive construction.  We then describe the local implicit
update and its finite-element implementation in
Sec.~\ref{sec:implicit-ad-implementation}.  The elastic specialization
provides analytical and centered-difference checks of the local tangent,
and a PETSc finite-difference comparison checks the assembled Jacobian.
The Mandel problem tests spatial water-pressure and displacement profiles
against the analytical plane-strain solution of Cheng and Detournay
\citep{chengdetournay1988}.  A separate finite-deformation continuation
shows how compression changes the coefficient.  Material-point calculations
then demonstrate the effect of plastic pore allocation on the coefficient,
its persistence on unloading, and its feedback into plastic flow at nonzero
pore pressure.  The boundary-value calculations verify the elastic limit;
the activated inelastic response is verified at the material level.
Verification of a coupled boundary-value problem with active plastic flow
and physical validation against a particular material remain subsequent steps.
