\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Pore pressure changes the stress carried by a deformable porous skeleton.  In
small-strain poroelasticity, the Biot coefficient represents this coupling and
is commonly evaluated from drained-skeleton and mineral bulk moduli
\citep{biotwillis1957}.  At finite deformation, the coupling must also reflect
the current intrinsic-solid state and the distinction between skeleton volume
change and mineral compression.

Foster and Xu express the finite-deformation coefficient through the change of
mineral distension with skeleton Jacobian at fixed pore pressure
\citep{fosterxu2025}.  The one-solid, one-fluid specialization retains
solid-reference kinematics, phase material measures, and the pressure Legendre
transform.  The coefficient is therefore a
fixed-pressure constitutive derivative.

That distinction controls the numerical implementation.  Solid mass
conservation is a spatial balance for the bulk solid partial density.  The local
constitutive system supplies the intrinsic solid state, and the solid volume
fraction is recovered algebraically from the partial and intrinsic densities.
The fixed-pressure derivative of the local mineral distension supplies the
Biot coefficient.  It is evaluated by implicit differentiation of the
constitutive residual system while holding intrinsic phase pressure and
referential solid mass \(J\rho_s\) fixed.  The
coefficient enters the stress residual, while the derived solid volume fraction
enters the complete nonlinear water accumulation.  Both
paths remain active in the global Newton linearization.

Sun, Ostien, and Salinger present a total-Lagrangian mixed finite-element
formulation for finite-strain poromechanics and show how residual evaluators
templated on scalar type provide automatic derivatives for the coupled
Jacobian \citep{sunostien2013}.  The Multiphysics Object Oriented Simulation
Environment (MOOSE) applies the same residual-based approach through automatic
differentiation (AD) \citep{gastonetal2009moose,lindsayetal2021ad}.
Large-deformation porous-material formulations also motivate careful separation
of skeleton deformation, fluid transport, and constitutive state evolution
\citep{hongetal2008,macminnetal2016,dehghanizilian2021}.  In computational
inelasticity, consistent algorithmic tangents provide the corresponding link
between a local material update and the global Newton method
\citep{simotaylor1985,miehe1996}.  We implement the residual-vector
architecture required by a coupled nonlinear local update: after the local
state converges, an AD-valued dense solve differentiates the active
constitutive system and returns its consistent fixed-pressure Biot tangent.
The elastic specialization has a closed form and is used only to verify this
general path.  AD retains the complete outer dependence without a hand-coded
global tangent.

The cited mixture theories supply the continuum coefficient and stress
transform.  The new content here is their monolithic finite-element
realization and verification for a finite-deformation solid--water system: a
spatial solid partial-density balance separates phase conservation from the
local constitutive constraints, the generally applicable implicit tangent
closes the Biot coefficient, the derived volume fraction closes the complete
water accumulation, and one AD graph differentiates the resulting
transient momentum and phase-mass residuals.  The computational contribution
is therefore a balance-law and constitutive architecture
that keeps stress measures and density measures distinct.  The global fields
are displacement, water pressure, and solid partial density.  The local update
returns the intrinsic state and its fixed-pressure tangent.  The solid volume
fraction and Biot coefficient are derived from those quantities.  Constitutive
models are written for the fixed-pressure
double-prime stress; the Biot transform reconstructs the single-prime material
stress when required, and the total stress enters momentum balance.  AD
propagates this sequence through solid storage, water storage, and momentum.
The same residual-vector interface admits inelastic internal variables and an
active-set-consistent tangent without assigning a balance law to a constitutive
state.

This paper has three objectives.  First, it states the single-solid,
single-water finite-deformation equations used by the implementation.  Second,
it specifies the spatial solid and water mass balances, their reference
pull-backs, the elastic mineral and water EOSs, and the fixed-pressure Biot
transform.  It then shows how the local constitutive outputs remain active in
the finite-element Jacobian.  Third, it verifies the implemented residual path with the
Mandel problem by comparing water-pressure and displacement profiles with the
analytical plane-strain solution of Cheng and Detournay
\citep{chengdetournay1988}.  A finite-deformation continuation of the same
solid--water system then demonstrates the coefficient change under ten-percent
platen compression.  The Mandel solution is formed using the storage obtained
by linearizing the same mineral EOS, and the vertical platen displacement is
prescribed from that solution.  The independent benchmark observables are
therefore pressure, lateral displacement, and recovered platen load.  The
continuation is a synthetic discrimination test rather than physical
validation.
