\section{Introduction}

The Biot coefficient controls the portion of pore pressure that enters the
macroscopic stress of a porous skeleton.  In the classical isotropic theory it
is related to the drained skeleton modulus \(K\) and the solid or unjacketed
modulus \(K_s\) by \(B=1-K/K_s\) \citep{biotwillis1957,nurbyerlee1971}.
This expression is frequently evaluated once and then treated as a constant.
That practice is unsuitable when cracks close, pores collapse, or the mineral
framework changes with stress, because the tangent modulus of the skeleton can
vary substantially along the loading path.

Foster and Xu \citep{fosterxu2025} derived a finite-deformation coefficient from
the intrinsic solid specific-volume response at fixed pore pressure.  The
definition gives the coefficient a direct constitutive meaning and recovers the
classical expression after linearization.  Its numerical realization is less
straightforward than the definition suggests.  The intrinsic specific volume
is not generally an explicit function of the skeleton Jacobian.  It is obtained
from coupled material-conservation, phase-volume, equation-of-state, and
equilibrium statements.  Consequently, the derivative needed by the Biot
coefficient is an implicit constrained tangent.  Moreover, the coefficient
appears inside the global mechanics residual, so the tangent itself must remain
differentiable with respect to the finite-element degrees of freedom.

This paper describes a nested differentiation strategy.  A small local
constitutive system is differentiated implicitly with respect to the skeleton
Jacobian at fixed equivalent pore pressure.  The local Jacobian and its solution
are represented by MOOSE automatic-differentiation (AD) scalars.  MOOSE then
differentiates the assembled coefficient through the global stress and storage
residuals.  The method avoids both finite-difference constitutive tangents and a
user-specified Biot function that could be inconsistent with material
conservation.

As a nonreacting application, we consider the hydrostatic dunite experiments of
Lawal and Kim \citep{lawal2026}.  Their Figure~3c reports a pronounced decrease
in Biot coefficient with pressure for a dry heat-treated control and specimens
serpentinized for 14 and 30 days.  The authors released the underlying
poromechanical workbook \citep{lawal2026dataset}, allowing the plotted values to
be reproduced without pixel digitization.  We fit a compact crack-closure law,
embed it in the constrained tangent, and compare the MOOSE result with all 46
published pressure--coefficient pairs.  Reaction is intentionally suppressed:
the goal is to isolate pressure-dependent poromechanical response before adding
reaction-state evolution.
