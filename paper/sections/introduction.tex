\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Plastic deformation changes the pore volume of a saturated solid and the
elastic response of its skeleton.  When the mineral is compressible, it also
changes how pore pressure contributes to stress.  The Biot coefficient must
then follow the constitutive state through loading and unloading.  Its value
enters the stress that drives further plastic flow, so the coefficient and
plastic state must be evaluated together.  We derive a closed-form
finite-deformation coefficient for matched logarithmic skeleton and mineral
laws, incorporate it into an implicit poroplastic update, and verify the
constitutive and finite-element derivatives.

The distinction between mineral volume and mixture volume is central to the
construction.  Drumheller separates the true deformation of a constituent
from the distention that describes its occupation of mixture volume
\citep{drumheller2000}.  In Foster and Xu, we expressed the finite-deformation
Biot coefficient through the change of intrinsic solid volume with skeleton
volume at fixed pore pressure \citep{fosterxu2025}.  That pressure transformation
relates the double-prime, fixed-pressure effective stress to the single-prime,
fixed-density effective stress.  For the constitutive model considered here,
the latter supplies the work conjugate for plastic deformation.  Consequently,
evolution of the Biot coefficient affects both the total mixture stress and
the evaluation of yield and flow.

The reference coefficient recovers the familiar relation between skeleton and
mineral bulk moduli \citep{biotwillis1957}.  At a plastically deformed state,
its value depends on the current elastic response and stored distention.
Measurements during sandstone compaction provide experimental motivation for
such state dependence \citep{ingraham2017evolution}.  Those measurements
identify an elastic unloading coefficient; the effective-stress coefficient
appropriate to failure is a separate constitutive question.  We therefore
specify the plastic energy and flow assumptions explicitly and examine their
consequences without fitting the model to a particular rock.

The first contribution is a closed-form coefficient in the current volume,
pore pressure, and mineral state.  We initially set out to compute the
coefficient by implicit automatic differentiation of the local constitutive
constraints.  Analytical elimination of that tangent for matched logarithmic
stress laws revealed that the state derivatives could be removed entirely.
The resulting expression retains the finite-deformation dependence and
recovers the reference relation between bulk moduli.  Only the scalar mineral
state equation remains to be solved.

The second contribution is a differentiable poroplastic implementation of
this coefficient.  Solid mass and the mineral EOS determine intrinsic density
and volume fraction at each candidate plastic state.  The return mapping
updates plastic distention and uses the closed-form coefficient in the
single-prime driving stress.  The global Newton derivative retains all of
these current-state dependencies.  The constitutive derivative defining the
coefficient holds the current plastic state fixed; the algorithmic derivative
advancing that state includes its evolution.  An independent implicit tangent
solve and centered differences verify the analytical elimination.

This construction connects established developments in constitutive
linearization and coupled porous-material simulation.  Consistent tangents
differentiate the discrete material update used by the nonlinear solver
\citep{simotaylor1985,miehe1996}.  Automatic differentiation (AD) supplies coupled
finite-strain poromechanics Jacobians from residual evaluations
\citep{sunostien2013}, and MOOSE supports AD-valued local calculations within
its finite-element assembly \citep{lindsayetal2021ad}.  Large-deformation
models of gels and porous solids describe the accompanying coupling between
material deformation and fluid transport \citep{hongetal2008,macminnetal2016},
while multiscale formulations evolve effective poroelastic properties with
microstructure \citep{dehghanizilian2021}.  Here the coefficient is calculated
directly from the constrained mineral state and carried through the local
plastic solve and outer AD calculation.

We first establish the kinematics and pressure transformation, then specify a
multiplicative poroplastic model with isochoric true-plastic flow and scalar
plastic distention.  An implicit exponential return mapping supplies the
updated state.  Material-point calculations examine history dependence,
unloading, and feedback at nonzero pressure, with independent checks of mass,
energy-derived stress, flow, yield, and derivatives.  The elastic specialization
is then embedded in solid and water mass conservation and momentum balance.
Spatial pressure and displacement comparisons with the Mandel solution test
the coupled finite-element formulation \citep{chengdetournay1988}, and a
finite-deformation continuation resolves the evolving coefficient field.
This separates the verified active constitutive response from the coupled
elastic benchmark and from subsequent physical validation.
