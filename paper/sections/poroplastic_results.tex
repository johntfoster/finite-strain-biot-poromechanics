\section{Plastic history and pressure feedback}
\label{sec:poroplastic-demonstration}

The material-point calculations show how plastic volume change affects the
Biot coefficient.  We prescribe homogeneous deformation
and pore pressure and solve the constitutive update at each increment.
The skeleton and mineral moduli are those listed for the coupled benchmark
in \cref{tab:mandel-parameters}.  The reference solid fraction is
\(\phi_{s0}=0.8\), which keeps the prescribed compression path within
\(0<\phi_s<1\); the plastic parameters are
\(M=0.6\), \(\beta=0.4\), and \(c=0\).  These values define a synthetic
material that dilates during plastic flow; they are used for verification.

\subsection{Loading, unloading, and reloading}

The drained path uses \(\mbf F=\operatorname{diag}(1,F_{yy},1)\).
The axial stretch decreases from unity to \(0.8\), increases to \(0.9\),
and then decreases to \(0.76\).  Each segment occupies one unit of the
load-path parameter.  The constitutive model is rate independent; this
parameter orders the imposed states.

\Cref{fig:implicit-poroplastic-history} shows the resulting coefficient and
plastic distention.  Loading activates dilation and changes the elastic
volume \(J^e=J/a^p\).  On unloading, the plastic factor is fixed while the
mineral and skeleton respond elastically.  The coefficient consequently
follows a different path from the virgin material at the same total
compression.  Reloading retraces this elastic branch until the previous yield
state is reached; further compression produces additional plastic distention.

The coefficient remains above the virgin elastic value at equal total
compression.  This agrees with \eqref{eq:poroplastic-biot-correction}:
plastic dilation reduces the elastic mineral volume and increases \(B\).

\subsection{Feedback at nonzero pore pressure}
\label{sec:poroplastic-b-feedback}

To examine pressure feedback, we compress the material to \(F_{yy}=0.8\)
while increasing pore pressure linearly from zero to a prescribed final
value.  A companion calculation follows the same deformation and pressure
history but substitutes the virgin elastic coefficient at the current total
\(J\) in the single-prime stress reconstruction.  This substitution is a
controlled numerical comparison; the consistent calculation obtains stress
and coefficient from the same current plastic state.

\Cref{fig:implicit-poroplastic-feedback} shows that the two plastic responses
coincide at zero pressure and separate as pressure increases.  In this example,
the higher coefficient of the plastic state reduces the isotropic term
\((1-B)pJ\mbf I\) in \eqref{eq:single-prime-kirchhoff-stress}.  The resulting
increase in compressive mean stress restrains dilative flow.
At \(p=400\,\mathrm{MPa}\), the consistent calculation gives
\(B=0.61560\) and \(a^p=1.04567\); the companion calculation gives
\(a^p=1.04592\).

\subsection{Constitutive verification}
\label{sec:poroplastic-verification}

Independent checks reconstruct the physical state from the MOOSE outputs.
The intrinsic density and volume fraction satisfy solid mass conservation and
the mineral EOS.  The mineral law is also checked against the intrinsic solid
stress trace.  Numerical differentiation of the pressure Legendre energy
recovers the returned
single-prime stress.  The logarithm of the incremental plastic factor checks
the flow rule, while its determinant checks the accumulated distention.
Yield admissibility, consistency on the active branch, and nonnegative
plastic dissipation are checked separately.

The same checks pass for a path with unequal principal stretches and nonzero
pore pressure.  Increment refinement reduces the difference between successive
returned plastic states on that path.  The active PETSc test described in
Sec.~\ref{sec:weak-residuals-and-spaces} verifies the Newton Jacobian
of the converged update.  These checks verify the constitutive update on the
smooth yield branch.
