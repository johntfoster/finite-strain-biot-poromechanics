 \section{Single-solid, single-water finite-deformation equations}
\label{sec:finite-deformation-biot}

At time \(t\), the solid motion \(\bs\chi\) maps a reference position
\(\mbf X\) to its current position \(\mbf x=\bs\chi(\mbf X,t)\).  The
deformation gradient and solid-reference Jacobian are
\begin{equation}
 \mbf F=\nabla_{\mbf X}\mbf x,
 \qquad
 J=\det\mbf F>0.
 \label{eq:skeleton-kinematics}
\end{equation}
The reference state is chosen with \(J_0=1\).  Here,
\(\nabla_{\mbf X}\) denotes the gradient with respect to \(\mbf X\).
Spatial balances use current mixture volume; their pull-backs use the solid
reference configuration employed by the finite-element residuals.

\subsection{Solid mass balance and solid state}
\label{sec:solved-solid-state}

Let \(\rho_s=\phi_s\bar\rho_s\) denote the solid partial density per unit
current mixture volume, where \(\phi_s\) is the solid volume fraction and
\(\bar\rho_s\) is the intrinsic solid density.  For a source-free solid moving
with the skeleton velocity \(\mbf v_s\), its spatial mass balance is
\begin{equation}
 \left.\pder{\rho_s}{t}\right|_{\mbf x}
 +\nabla_{\mbf x}\mathbin{\cdot}(\rho_s\mbf v_s)=0.
 \label{eq:spatial-solid-mass-balance}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, with \(D_s/Dt\) denoting the material rate following the solid,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{D_s\rho_s}{Dt}
 +\rho_s\nabla_{\mbf x}\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf v_s=0.
 \label{eq:material-solid-mass-balance}
\end{equation}
Using \(\dot J=J\nabla_{\mbf x}\cdot\mbf v_s\), the pull-back to the solid
reference configuration is
\begin{equation}
 \left.\pder{(J\rho_s)}{t}\right|_{\mbf X}=0,
 \label{eq:reference-solid-mass-balance}
\end{equation}
where the subscript \(0\) denotes the reference value and \(J_0=1\).  For the
uniform reference state, the source-free balance therefore gives
\(J\rho_s=\rho_{s0}=\phi_{s0}\bar\rho_{s0}\).  The mineral distension measures
the intrinsic solid volume change,
\begin{equation}
 \bar J
 :=\frac{\bar\rho_{s0}}{\bar\rho_s},
 \label{eq:distension}
\end{equation}
where \(\bar\rho_{s0}\) is the reference intrinsic solid density.

The solid phase is closed by a Helmholtz free energy.  Let
\(\psi_s(\mbf F,\bar\rho_s)\) be the solid free energy per unit mass, a general
function of the skeleton deformation gradient and the intrinsic solid density;
its deformation and density conjugates supply the intrinsic solid stress and
pressure and are developed in Sec.~\ref{sec:intrinsic-pressure}.  Through the
intrinsic pressure, the constitutive update supplies the intrinsic density.
The partial-density definition gives the solid volume fraction as the derived
quantity
\begin{equation}
 \phi_s=\frac{\rho_s}{\bar\rho_s}.
 \label{eq:solid-volume-fraction-relation}
\end{equation}
For the source-free uniform reference state, substitution of
\(J\rho_s=\phi_{s0}\bar\rho_{s0}\) and
\(\bar\rho_s=\bar\rho_{s0}/\bar J\) reduces this identity to
\(\phi_s=\phi_{s0}\bar J/J\).  The spatial balance evolves \(\rho_s\), and the
constitutive update supplies \(\bar\rho_s\), equivalently \(\bar J\), so
\eqref{eq:solid-volume-fraction-relation} recovers \(\phi_s\) locally.  With
sources, reactions, or nonuniform initial mass, the same identity uses the
current \(\rho_s\) without changing the local constitutive construction.

\subsection{Intrinsic pressure and effective stress}
\label{sec:intrinsic-pressure}

The intrinsic solid pressure is the negative mean of the total intrinsic solid
stress,
\begin{equation}
 \bar p_s
 =-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\bar{\bs\sigma}_s,
 \label{eq:intrinsic-pressure-mean-stress}
\end{equation}
where \(\bar{\bs\sigma}_s\) is the total intrinsic solid Cauchy stress, the
stress carried within the solid phase, and \(\operatorname{tr}\) denotes the
trace.  Local pressure equilibrium between the phases sets
\begin{equation}
 p=\bar p_f=\bar p_s,
 \label{eq:local-pressure-equilibrium}
\end{equation}
where \(p\) is the water pressure and \(\bar p_f\) is the intrinsic water
pressure.  The mixture stress is the volume-fraction-weighted sum of the
intrinsic stresses,
\begin{equation}
 \bs\sigma
 =\phi_s\bar{\bs\sigma}_s+\phi_f\bar{\bs\sigma}_f,
 \qquad
 \bar{\bs\sigma}_f=-p\mbf I,
 \label{eq:mixture-stress-split}
\end{equation}
where \(\phi_f=1-\phi_s\) and \(\mbf I\) is the identity tensor.

The intrinsic stress and pressure are the deformation and density conjugates of
the solid free energy per unit mass.  Let \(W_s=\bar\rho_{s0}\psi_s\) be the
same energy per unit reference solid volume.  The deformation conjugate is
\begin{equation}
 \bar{\bs\sigma}_s
 =\frac{1}{J}\pder{W_s}{\mbf F}\mbf F^T,
 \label{eq:intrinsic-stress-free-energy}
\end{equation}
and the density conjugate, evaluated at fixed deformation gradient, is
\begin{equation}
 \bar p_s
 =\bar\rho_s^2\left.\pder{\psi_s}{\bar\rho_s}\right|_{\mbf F}.
 \label{eq:intrinsic-pressure-free-energy}
\end{equation}
The two expressions for \(\bar p_s\), the negative mean stress in
\eqref{eq:intrinsic-pressure-mean-stress} and the density conjugate
\eqref{eq:intrinsic-pressure-free-energy}, coincide for a conservative solid.
The pressure equilibrium \eqref{eq:local-pressure-equilibrium} then eliminates
the intrinsic solid density as \(\bar\rho_s(p,\mbf F)\), whose dependence on
the skeleton deformation enters through the stress free energy.

The total intrinsic stress separates into a fixed-density part and the fluid
pressure.  Let \(\bar{\bs\sigma}^{\prime}_s\) be the single-prime intrinsic
stress, the deformation conjugate of the solid energy at fixed intrinsic
density, and let \(\bar{\bs\sigma}^{\prime\prime}_s\) be the double-prime
intrinsic stress, its fixed-pressure Legendre counterpart.  The Legendre
transform gives
\begin{equation}
 \bar{\bs\sigma}^{\prime}_s
 =\bar{\bs\sigma}^{\prime\prime}_s+\frac{1-B}{\phi_s}p\mbf I,
 \label{eq:primed-stress-transform}
\end{equation}
and the total intrinsic stress is
\begin{equation}
 \bar{\bs\sigma}_s
 =\bar{\bs\sigma}^{\prime}_s-p\mbf I
 =\bar{\bs\sigma}^{\prime\prime}_s+\frac{\phi_f-B}{\phi_s}p\mbf I.
 \label{eq:intrinsic-stress-decomposition}
\end{equation}
The drained skeleton stress is the aggregate double-prime stress,
\(\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}=\phi_s\bar{\bs\sigma}^{\prime\prime}_s\), and the
total mixture stress is
\begin{equation}
 \bs\sigma
 =\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}-Bp\mbf I.
 \label{eq:total-cauchy-stress}
\end{equation}
At equilibrium, \eqref{eq:intrinsic-pressure-mean-stress} and
\eqref{eq:intrinsic-stress-decomposition} give
\(\operatorname{tr}\bar{\bs\sigma}^{\prime}_s=0\), so the single-prime
intrinsic stress is deviatoric.  Because the free energy relates the intrinsic
pressure to the solid density, plasticity enters through the drained skeleton
stress \(\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}\) while the decomposition itself remains
valid.

\subsection{Biot coefficient and momentum balance}
\label{sec:biot-coefficient-and-momentum}

In terms of the mineral distension in \eqref{eq:distension}, the
finite-deformation Biot coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 B
 =1-\phi_{s0}
 \left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s}.
 \label{eq:finite-deformation-biot-coefficient}
\end{equation}
This is the one-solid reduction of the fixed-pressure coefficient in the
multicomponent theory and the binary-mixture coefficient of Foster and Xu
\citep[eq.~(39)]{fosterxu2025}.  The coefficient differentiates the constrained
constitutive state at fixed intrinsic pressure and fixed referential solid mass
\(J\rho_s\).  A general solid free energy changes \(B\) through the
fixed-pressure evolution of its density.  Internal variables and their
return-mapping equations join the local system, and implicit differentiation
then includes their response to \(J\).

Let \(\bs\sigma^{\prime}\) denote the deformation-conjugate material stress in
the pressure Legendre representation.  It is obtained from the drained stress
through the Biot transform
\begin{equation}
 \bs\sigma^{\prime}
 =\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}+(1-B)p\mbf I.
 \label{eq:single-prime-biot-transform}
\end{equation}
Thus a constitutive calculation formulated for
\(\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}\) reconstructs \(\bs\sigma^{\prime}\) before
using a law whose stress argument is single-prime.  Adding the isotropic fluid
stress gives the total mixture stress
\(\bs\sigma=\bs\sigma^{\prime}-p\mbf I
=\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}-Bp\mbf I\), and the total first Piola stress is
\begin{equation}
 \mbf P
 =\mbf P^{\prime\prime}-BpJ\mbf F^{-T}.
 \label{eq:total-first-piola-stress}
\end{equation}
The quasi-static momentum balance on the solid reference configuration is
\citep[eq.~(37)]{fosterxu2025}
\begin{equation}
 \nabla_{\mbf X}\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf P+J\rho\mbf g=\mbf0.
 \label{eq:reference-momentum-balance}
\end{equation}
Here, \(\rho\) is the current mixture mass density, \(\mbf g\) is body-force
acceleration, and \(\mbf0\) is the zero vector.  The elastic verification
closure supplies \(\mbf P^{\prime\prime}\) in
\eqref{eq:verification-effective-first-piola}.

\subsection{Inelastic response and the plastic driving stress}
\label{sec:inelastic-plastic-driving-stress}

An inelastic solid is described by a multiplicative split of the skeleton
deformation into a pore-allocation factor and the true deformation of the
solid material,
\begin{equation}
 \mbf F=\mbf A\bar{\mbf F},
 \qquad
 J=a\bar J,
 \qquad
 a=\det\mbf A,
 \qquad
 \bar J=\det\bar{\mbf F},
 \label{eq:inelastic-multiplicative-split}
\end{equation}
where \(\mbf A\) records the pore-space allocation and \(\bar{\mbf F}\) the
true deformation of the solid phase.  True solid mass conservation makes
\(\bar J\) coincide with the mineral volume ratio in
\eqref{eq:distension}, so that the allocation factor
\(a=J/\bar J=\phi_{s0}/\phi_s\) reproduces the solid volume fraction
\eqref{eq:solid-volume-fraction-relation}.  Extending the distension and
true-deformation split for porous media \citep{fosterxu2025}, each factor
carries an elastic--plastic split,
\begin{equation}
 \mbf A=\mbf A^e\mbf A^p,
 \qquad
 \bar{\mbf F}=\bar{\mbf F}^e\bar{\mbf F}^p,
 \label{eq:inelastic-kinematic-splits}
\end{equation}
so that the plastic factors \(\bar{\mbf F}^p\) and \(\mbf A^p\) are the stored
internal variables and the elastic factors are the dependent arguments,
\begin{equation}
 \bar{\mbf F}^e=\bar{\mbf F}(\bar{\mbf F}^p)^{-1},
 \qquad
 \mbf A^e=\mbf A(\mbf A^p)^{-1},
 \label{eq:inelastic-dependent-elastic-arguments}
\end{equation}
where the true-plastic flow is isochoric and the plastic allocation is
spherical,
\begin{equation}
 \det\bar{\mbf F}^p=1,
 \qquad
 \mbf A^p=(a^p)^{1/3}\mbf I,
 \label{eq:inelastic-plastic-isochoric-spherical}
\end{equation}
so that \(a=a^ea^p\).  The scalar plastic allocation \(a^p\) therefore records
the inelastic contribution to the solid volume fraction through
\(\phi_s=\phi_{s0}/a\), whereas the isochoric true-plastic flow leaves the
intrinsic density unchanged; \(\bar\rho_s\) remains governed by the elastic
true deformation \(\bar{\mbf F}^e\) and the mineral pressure law.

The phase-level dissipation analysis identifies the material stress that
drives each mechanism \citep{fosterxu2025}.  Plastic flow is conjugate to the
single-prime stress \(\bs\sigma^{\prime}\), the deformation conjugate at fixed
intrinsic density; the isochoric mechanism is driven by the deviatoric part of
\(\bs\sigma^{\prime}\) mapped through the elastic true-deformation factor, and
the scalar allocation mechanism by its mean part.  Because the single-prime
stress is reconstructed from the fixed-pressure skeleton stress by
\(\bs\sigma^{\prime}=\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}+(1-B)p\mbf I\)
\eqref{eq:single-prime-biot-transform}, a yield and flow rule formulated in
\(\bs\sigma^{\prime}\) requires the Biot coefficient before the plastic
response can be evaluated.  The coefficient itself depends on the active
plastic state through the fixed-pressure derivative
\eqref{eq:finite-deformation-biot-coefficient}.  For an inelastic solid the
local constitutive system therefore augments the pressure equilibrium with the
active yield, consistency, flow, and hardening equations, and the implicit
differentiation in Sec.~\ref{sec:implicit-ad-implementation} supplies the
consistent fixed-pressure tangent and hence \(B\).  The elastic closure in
Sec.~\ref{sec:elastic-verification-closure} is the inactive-mechanism limit in
which \(\bar{\mbf F}^p=\mbf I\) and \(a^p=1\).

Because the allocation factors are spherical, the factors commute and assemble
an aggregate elastic--plastic split of the skeleton deformation,
\begin{equation}
 \mbf F=\mbf F^e\mbf F^p,
 \qquad
 \mbf F^e=\mbf A^e\bar{\mbf F}^e,
 \qquad
 \mbf F^p=(a^p)^{1/3}\bar{\mbf F}^p,
 \label{eq:aggregate-plastic-split}
\end{equation}
so that \(J=J^eJ^p\) with \(J^p=a^p\) and \(\det\bar{\mbf F}^p=1\).  The
aggregate factor \(\mbf F^p\) is the plastic deformation observable in a drained
test: its determinant \(a^p\) is the plastic pore allocation that changes the
solid volume fraction through \(\phi_s=\phi_{s0}/a\), and its isochoric part
\(\bar{\mbf F}^p\) records the deviatoric rearrangement.  The elastic factor
\(\mbf F^e\) carries the drained (fixed-pressure) response, so a drained
loading--unloading cycle measures \(\mbf F^e\) and \(\mbf F^p\) directly.

Two stress measures therefore describe the inelastic response.  The drained
skeleton stress \(\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}\) is conjugate to the observable
elastic deformation, is the quantity a drained experiment reports, and is the
fixed-pressure stress entering momentum.  The dissipation analysis instead
identifies the single-prime stress
\(\bs\sigma^{\prime}=\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}+(1-B)p\mbf I\) as the driving
stress for plastic flow \citep{fosterxu2025}.  In the classical limit \(B=1\)
the two coincide, which is why effective-stress yield and flow rules in the
literature, calibrated against drained measurements, are phrased in what is
here \(\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}\).  For a compressible mineral the two differ,
and the coefficient \(B\) maps between the observable and the driving
formulation.

For the cohesionless skeleton considered here, the isochoric and pore-allocation
mechanisms share one activation.  At finite deformation the plastic power fixes
the stress measures that drive them.  The single-prime intrinsic stress is the
deformation conjugate of the solid energy at fixed intrinsic density,
\begin{equation}
 \bs\sigma'_s
 =\rho_s
 \left.\pder{\psi_s}{\bar{\mbf F}^e}\right|_{\mc X_s}
 (\bar{\mbf F}^e)^T,
 \label{eq:single-prime-kirchhoff-stress}
\end{equation}
a Kirchhoff-type current-configuration stress.  Because the isochoric
true-plastic rate is conjugate to the deviatoric part of this stress mapped
into the elastic true-deformation frame, the driving stress of the isochoric
mechanism is the Mandel-type tensor
\begin{equation}
 \mbf S
 =(\bar{\mbf F}^e)^{-1}
 \operatorname{dev}\bs\sigma'_s\,
 \bar{\mbf F}^e,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{tr}\mbf S=0 .
 \label{eq:mandel-driving-stress}
\end{equation}
The scalar pore-allocation mechanism is instead driven by the mean stress
\(\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}\bs\sigma'_s\).  Define the compressive mean and the
equivalent shear stress by
\begin{equation}
 p
 =-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\bs\sigma'_s,
 \qquad
 q
 =\sqrt{\tfrac32}\,\lVert\mbf S\rVert .
 \label{eq:finite-plastic-stress-invariants}
\end{equation}
The remaining derivation states the plastic flow rules and the surface that
selects them, following the two-mechanism construction of the multicomponent
theory \citep{fosterxu2025} and its Drucker--Prager specialization in the
companion theory.  Two scalar plastic rates describe the inelastic response:
the isochoric (true-plastic) rate \(\dot\gamma\ge0\), and the pore-allocation
(volumetric) rate \(\dot\eta=\dot a^p/a^p\), with \(\dot\eta>0\) a dilation and
\(\dot\eta<0\) a compaction.  With the isochoric rate aligned
with its driving stress,
\begin{equation}
 \dot{\bar{\mbf F}}^p(\bar{\mbf F}^p)^{-1}
 =\dot\gamma\,\mbf N',
 \qquad
 \mbf N'=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\mbf S}{q},
 \label{eq:isochoric-flow-direction}
\end{equation}
the trace of \(\mbf N'\) vanishes, so \(\det\bar{\mbf F}^p=1\) is preserved,
and \(\operatorname{tr}[\mbf S\,\dot{\bar{\mbf F}}^p(\bar{\mbf F}^p)^{-1}]
=q\dot\gamma\).  The plastic power is therefore
\begin{equation}
 \mc D^p
 =q\dot\gamma-p\dot\eta,
 \label{eq:plastic-power}
\end{equation}
where the first term is the deviatoric power of the isochoric mechanism and the
second is the pressure power of the pore-allocation mechanism (the pressure
resists dilation and drives compaction).

The dissipation analysis supplies two independent plastic mechanisms and the
driving stresses for each \citep{fosterxu2025}.  In the uncoupled, associated
specialization each mechanism is separately dissipative and has its own yield
condition, advancing at its own rate \(\dot\gamma\) or \(\dot\eta\).  The
isochoric true-plastic mechanism yields on the deviatoric condition and flows
in the direction of \eqref{eq:isochoric-flow-direction},
\begin{equation}
 f_d=q-q_y\le0,
 \qquad
 \dot\gamma\ge0,
 \qquad
 \dot\gamma f_d=0,
 \qquad
 \mc D^p_d=q\dot\gamma\ge0,
 \label{eq:isochoric-yield}
\end{equation}
where \(q_y\) is the constant shear strength.  The pore-allocation mechanism
yields on the volumetric condition and, on its compressive (compaction) branch,
\(\dot\eta\le0\),
\begin{equation}
 f_v=p-p_y\le0,
 \qquad
 \dot\eta\le0,
 \qquad
 \mc D^p_v=-p\dot\eta\ge0,
 \label{eq:distension-yield}
\end{equation}
where \(p_y\) is the constant compaction strength.  Under monotone compressive
loading the independent response sits at the corner \(q=q_y\), \(p=p_y\), with
the imposed volume reduction accommodated by plastic pore collapse rather than
by dilation, and the combined plastic rate is not normal to either surface.

A Drucker--Prager-type response is obtained by coupling the two mechanisms
through a shared rate constraint together with a stress-dependent dissipation,
following the thermomechanical construction of Collins and Houlsby
\citep[see][]{fosterxu2025}.  The shared rate constraint is the dilatancy rule
that locks the volumetric plastic rate to the isochoric one,
\begin{equation}
 \dot\eta=\beta\dot\gamma,
 \qquad
 \beta\ge0 .
 \label{eq:dilatancy-constraint}
\end{equation}
Physically it states that shear rearrangement of a frictional skeleton is
accompanied by a proportional volume change of slope \(\beta\) (the dilation
angle), so the two mechanisms advance under the single rate \(\dot\gamma\).
The accompanying stress-dependent dissipation assigns to each unit of shear
rate a cost proportional to the compressive mean stress,
\begin{equation}
 \mc R(p;\dot\gamma,\dot\eta)
 =\mu p\dot\gamma,
 \qquad
 \mu\ge0 ,
 \label{eq:stress-dependent-dissipation}
\end{equation}
admissible only for the rate pairs satisfying
\eqref{eq:dilatancy-constraint}.  A stress state can then sit on the yield
boundary only if no admissible plastic rate performs more work than this
dissipation.  Substituting \eqref{eq:plastic-power} and
\eqref{eq:dilatancy-constraint} into that admissibility inequality gives
\begin{equation}
 (q-\beta p)\dot\gamma\le\mu p\dot\gamma
 \quad\text{for all }\dot\gamma\ge0
 \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
 f=q-Mp\le0,
 \quad
 M=\mu+\beta .
 \label{eq:dp-yield}
\end{equation}
The single surface \eqref{eq:dp-yield} is the Drucker--Prager condition, with
yield slope \(M=\mu+\beta\).  On the surface the plastic rate follows by
normality in the single rate coordinate, \(\dot\eta=\beta\dot\gamma\), i.e. in
tensor form
\begin{equation}
 \dot{\mbf F}^p(\mbf F^p)^{-1}
 =\dot\gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}\frac{\mbf S}{q}+\frac{\beta}{3}\mbf I\right),
 \qquad
 \dot\gamma\ge0,
 \qquad
 \dot\gamma f=0,
 \label{eq:dp-flow}
\end{equation}
and the dissipation on the surface is
\begin{equation}
 \mc D^p
 =q\dot\gamma-p\beta\dot\gamma
 =\mu p\dot\gamma\ge0 .
 \label{eq:dp-dissipation}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:dp-yield}--\eqref{eq:dp-dissipation} are the coupled
Drucker--Prager-type model.  Because the flow direction follows from the
plastic potential \(g=q-\beta p\) (dilation slope \(\beta\)) whereas the yield
surface has slope \(M=\mu+\beta\), the model is non-associated whenever
\(\mu>0\); the frictional part \(\mu\) supplies the dissipation and the
difference \(M-\beta=\mu\) is the non-associativity.

The rate in \eqref{eq:dp-flow} is the total plastic deformation rate of the
skeleton, \(\dot{\mbf F}^p(\mbf F^p)^{-1}\), the quantity observable in a
drained test.  The two internal mechanisms are not separately observable: they
are projected onto this single surface and rate, and once the aggregate
rate is known the internal fields are reconstructed from the aggregate split
\eqref{eq:aggregate-plastic-split}.  Taking the determinant of
\eqref{eq:dp-flow} gives the pore-allocation rate and the isochoric factor,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot a^p}{a^p}
 =\operatorname{tr}\left[\dot{\mbf F}^p(\mbf F^p)^{-1}\right]
 =\beta\dot\gamma,
 \qquad
 a^p=\det\mbf F^p,
 \qquad
 \bar{\mbf F}^p=(a^p)^{-1/3}\mbf F^p,
 \label{eq:reconstruct-pore-allocation}
\end{equation}
with \(\det\bar{\mbf F}^p=1\), which is the dilation rate
\eqref{eq:dilatancy-constraint} expressed through the aggregate factor.  The
plastic pore allocation changes the solid volume fraction through
\(\phi_s=\phi_{s0}/a\) with \(a=a^ea^p\).  At small strain
\(\bar{\mbf F}^e\to\mbf I\) and the intrinsic and aggregate single-prime
stresses coincide with \(\bs\sigma'=\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}+(1-B)p\mbf I\),
so \eqref{eq:dp-yield}--\eqref{eq:dp-dissipation} reduce to the conventional
Cauchy Drucker--Prager form \(q'-Mp'\le0\) with
\(q'=\sqrt{\tfrac32}\,\lVert\operatorname{dev}\bs\sigma'\rVert\) and
\(p'=-\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}\bs\sigma'\).  Because
\(\operatorname{dev}\bs\sigma'=\operatorname{dev}\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}\) and
\(p'=p^{\prime\prime}-(1-B)p\), that reduced form reads in the drained
observables as
\begin{equation}
 f=q'-M\big[p^{\prime\prime}-(1-B)p\big]\le0,
 \qquad
 p^{\prime\prime}=-\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime},
 \label{eq:dp-yield-observable}
\end{equation}
which exposes the explicit Biot correction omitted by a \(B=1\) or
fixed-elastic-\(B\) approximation.  At finite deformation that correction
enters through the single-prime Kirchhoff/Mandel measures of
\eqref{eq:single-prime-kirchhoff-stress}--\eqref{eq:finite-plastic-stress-invariants};
forming them requires the elastic true deformation \(\bar{\mbf F}^e\), which
therefore joins the inelastic local state together with \(a^p\) and the
hardening variables.

The Biot coefficient is the system coefficient of
\eqref{eq:finite-deformation-biot-coefficient},
\(B=1-\phi_{s0}\,\partial\bar J/\partial J|_{p,J\rho_s,\mc H_n}\); the phase
form of the multicomponent theory reduces to this same coefficient in the
single-solid limit and therefore need not be carried separately
\citep{fosterxu2025}.  Because \(B\) is the fixed-pressure tangent of the
active local system, evaluating the yield and flow of the poroplastic skeleton
requires the implicit coefficient; the local system and its active-set tangent
are developed in Sec.~\ref{sec:implicit-ad-implementation}.

\subsection{Plastic loading and the incremental rate}
\label{sec:plastic-loading-incremental-rate}

Loading and unloading of the Drucker--Prager-type surface obey the
complementarity (Kuhn--Tucker) conditions
\begin{equation}
 \dot\gamma\ge0,
 \qquad
 f\le0,
 \qquad
 \dot\gamma f=0,
 \label{eq:kuhn-tucker}
\end{equation}
together with the consistency condition during plastic loading,
\begin{equation}
 \dot f=0
 \quad\text{whenever}\quad \dot\gamma>0 .
 \label{eq:consistency-dot-f}
\end{equation}
The response is elastic when \(f<0\), and also when \(f=0\) but the trial
stress rate would move the stress inside the surface (unloading, \(\dot f<0\)).
When \(f=0\) and loading continues, the complementarity conditions leave
\(\dot\gamma\) undetermined; it is fixed by the consistency condition
\eqref{eq:consistency-dot-f}.  Because the flow rule \eqref{eq:dp-flow} changes
the plastic state, which enters the stress through the elastic relation and the
coefficient \(B\), the consistency condition is an equation for \(\dot\gamma\),
not a prescribed value.

The model is rate independent: the complementarity and consistency above are
rate conditions that select the instantaneous \(\dot\gamma\), but no viscous
or overstress regularization replaces the consistency condition
\eqref{eq:consistency-dot-f}.  In the time-discrete calculations reported below
(backward Euler) the unknown of the plastic update is the increment
\(\Delta\gamma=\int\dot\gamma\,\dd t\), and the update is a standard
elastic-predictor/return-mapping step:
advance the state with \(\Delta\gamma=0\) (plastic factors frozen) and evaluate
the trial driving stress; if the trial satisfies \(f\le0\) the step is elastic
and \(\Delta\gamma=0\); otherwise the discrete consistency condition
\begin{equation}
 f\big(\bs\sigma'(\Delta\gamma),\,p(\Delta\gamma),\,\mc H_{n+1}\big)=0
 \label{eq:discrete-consistency}
\end{equation}
is solved for \(\Delta\gamma\ge0\), with the state advanced by the integrated
flow rule \eqref{eq:dp-flow} and the reconstruction
\eqref{eq:reconstruct-pore-allocation}.  Because the driving stress is the
single-prime stress \(\bs\sigma'=\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}+(1-B)p\mbf I\), the
discrete consistency \eqref{eq:discrete-consistency} couples to the coefficient
\(B\), which is itself the fixed-pressure tangent of the active local system.
The implementation therefore does not solve \eqref{eq:discrete-consistency} for
\(\Delta\gamma\) in isolation; it enters as one scalar component of the local
residual system of Sec.~\ref{sec:implicit-ad-implementation} on the active set.
That system is solved by Newton's method with the residual and its Jacobian
evaluated in automatic-differentiation (AD) types, so the algorithmic tangent
is obtained exactly and consistently with the global residual; no hand
differentiation of the return mapping is required.  The converged iterate
returns the plastic state, the stress, and \(B\), and the AD derivatives retain
the result in the global Jacobian.  The elastic verification closure in
Sec.~\ref{sec:elastic-verification-closure} is the limit \(\Delta\gamma=0\) of
this update.

\subsection{Water storage and transport}
\label{sec:water-storage-and-transport}

The spatial source-free water mass balance is
\begin{equation}
 \left.\pder{(\phi_f\bar\rho_f)}{t}\right|_{\mbf x}
 +\nabla_{\mbf x}\mathbin{\cdot}
 \left(\phi_f\bar\rho_f\mbf v_s+\mbf w_f\right)=0,
 \label{eq:spatial-water-mass-balance}
\end{equation}
where \(\mbf w_f\) is the water mass flux relative to the skeleton.  Pulling
this balance back to the solid reference configuration gives
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
 \left(J\phi_f\bar\rho_f\right)\right|_{\mbf X}
 +\nabla_{\mbf X}\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf W_f=0,
 \label{eq:reference-water-mass-balance}
\end{equation}
where \(\phi_f=1-\phi_s\), \(\bar\rho_f\) is intrinsic water density, and
\(\mbf W_f=J\mbf F^{-1}\mbf w_f\).

Define the water bulk modulus by
\(K_f=\bar\rho_f\,\mathrm{d}p/\mathrm{d}\bar\rho_f\).  Taking \(K_f\)
constant and integrating from the reference state gives the pressure--density
relation and its material time derivative,
\begin{subequations}
 \label{eq:barotropic-water-eos}
 \begin{align}
  \bar\rho_f(p)
  &=\bar\rho_{f0}\exp\left(\frac{p-p_0}{K_f}\right),
  \label{eq:barotropic-water-density}\\
  \frac{\dot{\bar\rho}_f}{\bar\rho_f}
  &=\frac{\dot p}{K_f}.
  \label{eq:barotropic-water-density-rate}
 \end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here \(p_0\) and \(\bar\rho_{f0}\) define the reference water state, and an
overdot denotes \(\left.\partial/\partial t\right|_{\mbf X}\).  Substitution
into \eqref{eq:reference-water-mass-balance} expands the accumulation rate into
the rate of \(J(1-\phi_s)\) plus \(J(1-\phi_s)\dot p/K_f\).  The Lagrangian
pore volume \(J\phi_f=J(1-\phi_s)\) \citep{biotwillis1957} is computed from
the solid volume fraction field, so the water balance
retains the complete nonlinear accumulation
\(J(1-\phi_s)\bar\rho_f(p)\) at the continuous level.  The Biot
coefficient enters momentum explicitly, while the same local solid response
affects water accumulation through the solid volume fraction.

For isotropic permeability \(\kappa\), water dynamic viscosity \(\mu_f\), and
zero gravity, the pulled-back Darcy mass flux is
\citep{sunostien2013}
\begin{equation}
 \mbf W_f
 =-\bar\rho_f J\mbf F^{-1}
 \frac{\kappa}{\mu_f}\mbf F^{-T}\nabla_{\mbf X}p.
 \label{eq:reference-darcy-flux}
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:total-first-piola-stress},
\eqref{eq:reference-water-mass-balance}, and
\eqref{eq:reference-darcy-flux} retain the finite-deformation stress, storage,
and transport maps used in the numerical residual.

\subsection{Elastic verification closure}
\label{sec:elastic-verification-closure}

The reported calculations use an elastic mineral and an elastic skeleton.  The
intrinsic stress follows from the deformation conjugate
\eqref{eq:intrinsic-stress-free-energy}, while the intrinsic pressure is
closed by a two-input split: a drained skeleton potential supplies the
deformation response and a mineral pressure law supplies the intrinsic
pressure.  A unified free energy of the additively separable form
\(\psi_s(\mbf F,\bar\rho_s)=\psi_s^{\mathrm{sk}}(\mbf F)+\psi_s^{\mathrm d}(\bar\rho_s)\)
is not used here, because its density and deformation conjugates are
inconsistent for the pressure decomposition below; the skeleton and mineral
responses therefore enter as separate constitutive inputs.  The zero-pressure
skeleton potential is the decoupled compressible neo-Hookean form
\begin{equation}
 W_0(\mbf F)
 =\frac{G}{2}\left(J^{-2/3}I_1-3\right)
 +\frac{K}{2}(\ln J)^2,
 \qquad I_1=\mbf F:\mbf F,
 \label{eq:verification-skeleton-potential}
\end{equation}
where \(K\) and \(G\) are the drained skeleton bulk and shear moduli,
respectively.  The zero-pressure skeleton Cauchy stress
\(\bs\sigma_0=\frac{1}{J}\pder{W_0}{\mbf F}\mbf F^T\) has volumetric part
\(\bs\sigma_0^{\mathrm{vol}}=K\ln J\,\mbf I/J\), whose mean normal stress is
\(\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\bs\sigma_0=K\ln J/J\).  The mineral law sets
the intrinsic pressure to the mineral pressure law plus the volumetric contact
of the skeleton,
\begin{equation}
 \bar p_s
 =\hat p_s^{\mathrm{min}}(\bar J,\bs\xi)
 +\frac{1}{\phi_{s0}}\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\bs\sigma_0,
 \label{eq:general-mineral-law}
\end{equation}
where \(\hat p_s^{\mathrm{min}}\) is the mineral pressure law and \(\bs\xi\)
its internal variables.  The elastic mineral takes
\(\hat p_s^{\mathrm{min}}=-K_s\ln\bar J\), where \(K_s\) is the mineral bulk
modulus, giving
\begin{equation}
 \bar p_s
 =-K_s\ln\bar J+\frac{1}{\phi_{s0}}\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}\bs\sigma_0
 =-K_s\ln\bar J+\frac{K\ln J}{\phi_{s0}J}.
 \label{eq:elastic-mineral-pressure}
\end{equation}
Local equilibrium
\eqref{eq:local-pressure-equilibrium} gives the elastic distension
\begin{equation}
 \bar J(p,J)
 =\exp\left[-\frac{1}{K_s}\left(p-\frac{K\ln J}{\phi_{s0}J}\right)\right],
 \qquad
 \bar J_0(J)=\bar J(0,J),
 \label{eq:verification-mineral-factors}
\end{equation}
so \(J<1\) decreases \(\bar J\) and increases the intrinsic solid density,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\bar\rho_s}{\bar\rho_{s0}}=\frac{1}{\bar J(p,J)}.
 \label{eq:elastic-mineral-density}
\end{equation}
Substitution into \eqref{eq:finite-deformation-biot-coefficient} gives the
closed form
\begin{equation}
 B
 =1-\frac{K\,\bar J(p,J)(1-\ln J)}{K_sJ^2},
 \label{eq:elastic-finite-deformation-biot-coefficient}
\end{equation}
and hence \(B_0=1-K/K_s\) at \(J=1\) and \(p=0\).

The fixed-pressure potential used by the verification is the zero-pressure
potential evaluated along the elastic mineral path,
\begin{equation}
 W^{\prime\prime}(\mbf F,p)
 =W_0(\mbf F)
 +\phi_{s0}
 \left\{K_s\left[\bar J_0(J)-\bar J(p,J)\right]-p\,\bar J(p,J)\right\}.
 \label{eq:verification-fixed-pressure-potential}
\end{equation}
This is the coupled skeleton--mineral energy restricted to the
EOS-selected specific-volume path; it reduces to \(W_0\) at \(p=0\).
Differentiation at fixed \(p\), with
\(\partial J/\partial\mbf F=J\mbf F^{-T}\), gives the double-prime first
Piola--Kirchhoff stress
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mbf P^{\prime\prime}
 ={}&GJ^{-2/3}\left(\mbf F-\frac{I_1}{3}\mbf F^{-T}\right)
 +K\ln J\,\mbf F^{-T}\\
 &-K(1-\ln J)
 \left[\bar J(p,J)-\bar J_0(J)+\frac{p}{K_s}\bar J(p,J)\right]
 \frac{\mbf F^{-T}}{J}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:verification-effective-first-piola}
\end{equation}
The pressure Legendre potential and its deformation-conjugate single-prime
stress are
\begin{align}
 W^{\prime}(\mbf F,p)
 &=W^{\prime\prime}(\mbf F,p)+\phi_{s0}p\,\bar J(p,J),
 \label{eq:pressure-legendre-potential}\\
 \mbf P^{\prime}
 &=\mbf P^{\prime\prime}
 +\phi_{s0}p\left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_pJ\mbf F^{-T},
 \label{eq:single-prime-first-piola-mineral-transform}\\
 \mbf P^{\prime}
 &=\mbf P^{\prime\prime}+(1-B)pJ\mbf F^{-T},
 \label{eq:single-prime-first-piola-biot-transform}
\end{align}
where the last equality uses
\(1-B=\phi_{s0}\left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_p\), the energetic origin of
the Biot transform.  The Mandel calculation sets \(\mbf g=\mbf0\) and uses
\eqref{eq:verification-effective-first-piola} for \(\mbf P^{\prime\prime}\).
