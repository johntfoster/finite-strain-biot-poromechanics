\section{Deformation, distention, and pressure coupling}
\label{sec:finite-deformation-biot}

A saturated porous solid changes volume through deformation of its constituent
material and through changes in pore volume.  These mechanisms enter the stress
and the phase balances through different quantities.  We first identify those
quantities and then obtain the Biot coefficient from the mineral-volume
response at fixed pore pressure.

\subsection{Solid motion and phase mass}
\label{sec:solved-solid-state}

Let the solid motion map a reference position \(\mbf X\) to
\(\mbf x=\bs\chi(\mbf X,t)\).  Its deformation gradient and solid-reference
Jacobian are
\begin{equation}
 \mbf F=\nabla_{\mbf X}\bs\chi,
 \qquad J=\det\mbf F>0.
 \label{eq:skeleton-kinematics}
\end{equation}
Following Drumheller \citep[Sec.~3]{drumheller2000}, we separate the true
motion of the solid material from the distention gradient,
\begin{equation}
 \mbf F=\mbf A\bar{\mbf F},
 \qquad \bar J=\det\bar{\mbf F},
 \qquad a=\det\mbf A,
 \qquad J=a\bar J.
 \label{eq:inelastic-multiplicative-split}
\end{equation}
Here \(\bar{\mbf F}\) is the true-deformation gradient, \(\bar J\) is the
true-deformation Jacobian, \(\mbf A\) is the distention gradient, and \(a\)
is the distention.  We choose a reference state with \(J_0=\bar J_0=a_0=1\).
The ratio \(\bar J\) measures the change in mineral volume.  Distention
measures the additional volume change of the porous structure: increasing
\(a\) at fixed mineral volume increases pore volume.

Let \(\bar\rho_s\) be the solid intrinsic density and \(\phi_s\) its volume
fraction.  The partial density per unit current mixture volume is
\begin{equation}
 \rho_s=\phi_s\bar\rho_s.
 \label{eq:solid-volume-fraction-relation}
\end{equation}
For a source-free solid moving with velocity \(\mbf v_s\), mass conservation
in spatial and solid-reference forms is
\begin{align}
 \left.\pder{\rho_s}{t}\right|_{\mbf x}
 +\nabla_{\mbf x}\mathbin{\cdot}(\rho_s\mbf v_s)&=0,
 \label{eq:spatial-solid-mass-balance}\\
 \left.\pder{(J\rho_s)}{t}\right|_{\mbf X}&=0.
 \label{eq:reference-solid-mass-balance}
\end{align}
For the uniform reference state used here,
\(J\rho_s=\rho_{s0}=\phi_{s0}\bar\rho_{s0}\).  The true motion preserves
mineral mass, giving
\begin{equation}
 \bar J\equiv\frac{\bar\rho_{s0}}{\bar\rho_s},
 \qquad \phi_s=\frac{\phi_{s0}\bar J}{J},
 \qquad \phi_s a=\phi_{s0}.
 \label{eq:distension}
\end{equation}
Thus the intrinsic density determines true volume change, while the ratio of
partial to intrinsic density determines the solid volume fraction.  Saturation
then gives the water volume fraction \(\phi_f=1-\phi_s\).

\subsection{Pressure and the two effective stresses}
\label{sec:intrinsic-pressure}

Let \(\psi_s\) denote the solid free energy per unit mass, with skeleton
deformation, intrinsic density, and constitutive internal state as its
arguments.  Local equilibrium identifies the density conjugate with the pore
pressure \citep[eq.~(27c)]{fosterxu2025},
\begin{equation}
 p=\bar p_f=\bar p_s
 =\bar\rho_s^2\left.\pder{\psi_s}{\bar\rho_s}\right|_{\mbf F},
 \label{eq:local-pressure-equilibrium}
\end{equation}
where \(\bar p_f\) and \(\bar p_s\) are the intrinsic water and solid
thermodynamic pressures, respectively, and any internal variables are held
fixed.  The intrinsic solid stress also contains the contribution of
skeleton deformation.  Consequently, pressure equilibrium does not require
the effective solid stress to be deviatoric.

Write \(W_s=\rho_{s0}\psi_s\) per unit reference mixture volume.  In terms
of \(\bar J\), the density conjugacy becomes
\begin{equation}
 p=-\frac{1}{\phi_{s0}}
 \left.\pder{W_s}{\bar J}\right|_{\mbf F}.
 \label{eq:intrinsic-pressure-free-energy}
\end{equation}
The independent arguments of \(W_s\) are \(\mbf F\) and
\(\bar\rho_s\), equivalently \(\mbf F\) and \(\bar J\), with internal
variables held fixed.  Solving this pressure relation determines
\(\bar J(\mbf F,p)\).  Denote \(W_s\) evaluated on that state by
\(W^{\prime\prime}(\mbf F,p)\).  The pressure Legendre transform replaces
the intrinsic-density argument by pressure and defines the potential
\begin{equation}
 W^{\prime}(\mbf F,p)
 =W^{\prime\prime}(\mbf F,p)+\phi_{s0}p\bar J(\mbf F,p).
 \label{eq:pressure-legendre-potential}
\end{equation}
The single-prime first Piola stress is the deformation derivative of
\(W_s\) at fixed intrinsic density, equivalently the derivative of the
transformed potential \(W^{\prime}\) at fixed pressure.  The density-response
terms cancel in the latter derivative by
\eqref{eq:intrinsic-pressure-free-energy}.  The double-prime stress differentiates \(W^{\prime\prime}\) at
fixed pressure \citep[eqs.~(34)--(36)]{fosterxu2025}:
\begin{align}
 \mbf P^{\prime}
 &=\left.\pder{W_s}{\mbf F}\right|_{\bar J}
 ,\label{eq:single-prime-fixed-density-conjugate}\\
 \mbf P^{\prime}&=\left.\pder{W^{\prime}}{\mbf F}\right|_p,
 \label{eq:single-prime-energy-conjugate}\\
 \mbf P^{\prime\prime}
 &=\left.\pder{W^{\prime\prime}}{\mbf F}\right|_p.
 \label{eq:double-prime-energy-conjugate}
\end{align}
Internal variables remain fixed in these constitutive derivatives.

\subsection{The finite-deformation Biot coefficient}
\label{sec:biot-coefficient-and-momentum}

For the isotropic volume response considered here, the fixed-state dependence
of \(\bar J\) on deformation is through \(J\).  Differentiating
\eqref{eq:pressure-legendre-potential} gives
\begin{equation}
 \mbf P^{\prime}
 =\mbf P^{\prime\prime}
 +\phi_{s0}p\left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s}
 J\mbf F^{-T}.
 \label{eq:single-prime-first-piola-mineral-transform}
\end{equation}
The corresponding Biot coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 B=1-\phi_{s0}\left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s}.
 \label{eq:finite-deformation-biot-coefficient}
\end{equation}
This is the one-solid coefficient that we derived in Foster and Xu
\citep[eq.~(39)]{fosterxu2025}.  It measures the fraction of a skeleton-volume
increment available to change pore volume when pressure, referential solid
mass, and the current internal state are fixed.  An incompressible mineral
has \(\bar J=1\) and \(B=1\).

Pushing the stresses forward gives
\begin{align}
 \bs\sigma^{\prime}
 &=\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}+(1-B)p\mbf I,
 \label{eq:single-prime-biot-transform}\\
 \bs\sigma
 &=\bs\sigma^{\prime}-p\mbf I,
 \label{eq:total-cauchy-single-prime}\\
 \bs\sigma
 &=\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}-Bp\mbf I.
 \label{eq:total-cauchy-stress}
\end{align}
Here \(\bs\sigma^{\prime}=J^{-1}\mbf P^{\prime}\mbf F^T\), with the same
push-forward for the double-prime stress, and \(\mbf I\) is the identity
tensor.  The total first Piola stress and quasi-static balance are
\begin{gather}
 \mbf P=\mbf P^{\prime\prime}-BpJ\mbf F^{-T},
 \label{eq:total-first-piola-stress}\\
 \nabla_{\mbf X}\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf P+J\rho\mbf g=\mbf0,
 \label{eq:reference-momentum-balance}
\end{gather}
where \(\rho\) is total mixture density and \(\mbf g\) is body-force
acceleration.  The coefficient therefore determines both the pressure term
in total stress and the single-prime stress used below to drive plastic flow.

\section{Poroplastic constitutive response}
\label{sec:inelastic-plastic-driving-stress}

We choose an isotropic model to examine how permanent changes in pore volume
affect pressure coupling.  The logarithmic skeleton and mineral laws give a
closed-form coefficient at each plastic state.  The mineral equation of
state (EOS), yield surface, and dilation rule below specify the material
response.

\subsection{Plastic distention and mineral compression}

We specialize the solid mixture energy to one nonreacting constituent at
constant temperature, with spherical distention and isochoric true-plastic
flow.  The elastic and plastic factors satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \bar{\mbf F}=\bar{\mbf F}^e\bar{\mbf F}^p,
 \qquad \mbf A=\mbf A^e\mbf A^p,
 \qquad \det\bar{\mbf F}^p=1,
 \qquad
 \mbf A^e=(a^e)^{1/3}\mbf I,
 \qquad \mbf A^p=(a^p)^{1/3}\mbf I.
 \label{eq:inelastic-kinematic-splits}
\end{equation}
Because the plastic distention factor is spherical, the factors combine to
give
\begin{equation}
 \mbf F=\mbf F^e\mbf F^p,
 \qquad \mbf F^e=(a^e)^{1/3}\bar{\mbf F}^e,
 \qquad \mbf F^p=(a^p)^{1/3}\bar{\mbf F}^p,
 \qquad \det\mbf F^p=a^p.
 \label{eq:aggregate-plastic-split}
\end{equation}
Consequently, \(J^e=\det\mbf F^e=J/a^p\).  True mass conservation and
isochoric true-plastic flow give
\(\det\bar{\mbf F}^e=\bar J=\bar\rho_{s0}/\bar\rho_s\), so that
\(J^e=a^e\bar J\).  Elastic skeleton volume is thus divided between
distention of the porous structure and compression of the mineral.

\subsection{Energy-consistent skeleton and mineral response}
\label{sec:elastic-verification-closure}
\label{sec:numerical-solid-eos}

The constitutive construction must reproduce the drained skeleton response
and the mineral compression law through the same stored energy.  Let \(K\)
and \(K_s\) be their reference bulk moduli, with
\(0<K<\phi_{s0}K_s\).  We prescribe the drained volumetric energy per unit
original mixture reference volume as \(K(\ln J^e)^2/2\), and the mineral
energy per unit reference mineral volume as
\(K_s(\ln\bar J)^2/2\).  We first identify the stresses conjugate to these
energies, then match their contributions and impose pressure equilibrium.

Here \(\delta\) denotes an infinitesimal virtual increment about the current
state.  We evaluate reversible elastic work with the plastic deformation
held fixed, \(\delta\mbf F^p=\mbf0\), so \(\delta a^p=0\).
The current elastic deformation may be finite; only the increment is
infinitesimal.

The tensor \(\delta\mbf F^e(\mbf F^e)^{-1}\) describes the incremental
deformation relative to the current elastic configuration.  Its symmetric
part is the infinitesimal strain increment, and its skew part is the
incremental rotation.  The determinant differential gives
\begin{align}
 \delta\ln J^e&=\frac{\delta J^e}{J^e},
 \label{eq:elastic-logarithmic-volume-increment}\\
 &=\tr\left[\delta\mbf F^e(\mbf F^e)^{-1}\right].
 \label{eq:elastic-volume-increment-trace}
\end{align}
A purely volumetric increment has equal normal strains in all three
directions, zero shear strain, and zero rotation.  Its incremental
deformation tensor is therefore spherical.  Since \(\tr\mbf I=3\),
\begin{equation}
 \delta\mbf F^e(\mbf F^e)^{-1}
 =\frac13\delta\ln J^e\,\mbf I.
 \label{eq:elastic-spherical-deformation-increment}
\end{equation}
The factor \(1/3\) distributes the fractional volume change equally among
the three directions.  With \(J=a^pJ^e\) and fixed plastic deformation,
\begin{equation}
 \delta\ln J^e=\delta\ln J,
 \qquad \delta a^p=0.
 \label{eq:fixed-plastic-log-volume-variation}
\end{equation}
Multiplying \eqref{eq:elastic-spherical-deformation-increment} on the
right by \(\mbf F^e\) gives
\begin{equation}
 \delta\mbf F^e=\frac13\mbf F^e\,\delta\ln J^e.
 \label{eq:elastic-volumetric-deformation-variation}
\end{equation}
Then differentiating \(\mbf F=\mbf F^e\mbf F^p\) at fixed
\(\mbf F^p\) gives
\begin{align}
 \delta\mbf F&=\delta\mbf F^e\mbf F^p,
 \label{eq:fixed-plastic-deformation-variation}\\
 &=\frac13\mbf F\,\delta\ln J^e.
 \label{eq:total-volumetric-deformation-variation}
\end{align}
This increment preserves elastic shape, as can be checked directly:
\begin{align}
 \delta\left[(J^e)^{-1/3}\mbf F^e\right]
 &=(J^e)^{-1/3}\left(\delta\mbf F^e
 -\frac13\mbf F^e\,\delta\ln J^e\right),
 \label{eq:elastic-shape-increment-expansion}\\
 &=\mbf0.
 \label{eq:elastic-shape-increment-zero}
\end{align}
Thus fixing normalized elastic shape permits a nonzero volume increment
\(\delta J^e\).

Hold pore pressure constant during the volume increment, so \(\delta p=0\).
The prescribed pressure may be nonzero.
The mineral volume \(\bar J\) adjusts to satisfy local pressure equilibrium
as \(J^e\) changes.  Both \(\bar J\) and \(a^e=J^e/\bar J\) can therefore
vary during this increment.  At fixed pressure and plastic state, the
energy increment is
\(\delta W^{\prime\prime}=\mbf P^{\prime\prime}:\delta\mbf F\), by
\eqref{eq:double-prime-energy-conjugate}.  Substituting
\eqref{eq:total-volumetric-deformation-variation} gives
\begin{align}
 \delta W^{\prime\prime}
 &=\mbf P^{\prime\prime}:\delta\mbf F,
 \label{eq:drained-energy-first-piola-work}\\
 &=\frac13\left(\mbf P^{\prime\prime}:\mbf F\right)
 \delta\ln J^e,
 \label{eq:drained-energy-contraction-work}\\
 &=\frac13\tr\left(\mbf P^{\prime\prime}\mbf F^T\right)
 \delta\ln J^e,
 \label{eq:drained-energy-volumetric-work}\\
 &=\frac J3\tr\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}\,\delta\ln J^e.
 \label{eq:drained-energy-cauchy-work}
\end{align}
The contraction identity
\(\mbf P^{\prime\prime}:\mbf F=\sum_{i,A}P^{\prime\prime}_{iA}F_{iA}\)
equals \(\tr(\mbf P^{\prime\prime}\mbf F^T)\), because the diagonal
entries of the product are \(\sum_A P^{\prime\prime}_{iA}F_{iA}\).
The last step uses the Cauchy-stress transformation
\(\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}=J^{-1}\mbf P^{\prime\prime}\mbf F^T\).
It identifies the Kirchhoff stress as
\begin{equation}
 \bs\tau^{\prime\prime}=J\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}.
 \label{eq:drained-kirchhoff-volume-convention}
\end{equation}
The factor \(J\) converts work per unit current mixture volume to work per
unit original reference volume.  The elastic ratio \(J^e\) measures the
recoverable volume change in the energy.

To determine the drained reference response, we now choose the constant
pore pressure to be zero.  This is the reference condition for the
prescribed energy \(K(\ln J^e)^2/2\); the definition of the double-prime
stress requires only \(\delta p=0\).  At \(p=0\), the isochoric contribution
is constant in this volumetric increment, so direct differentiation gives
\begin{align}
 \delta W^{\prime\prime}
 &=\delta\left[\frac K2(\ln J^e)^2\right],
 \label{eq:drained-logarithmic-energy-variation}\\
 &=K\ln J^e\,\delta\ln J^e.
 \label{eq:drained-logarithmic-energy-differential}
\end{align}
Reversible work must equal the change in stored energy for every admissible
volumetric increment.  Equating the coefficients of \(\delta\ln J^e\)
in \eqref{eq:drained-energy-cauchy-work} and
\eqref{eq:drained-logarithmic-energy-differential} yields
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac13\tr\bs\tau^{\prime\prime}\right|_{p=0}
 =K\ln J^e.
 \label{eq:drained-volumetric-energy-derivative}
\end{equation}
Thus the drained mean skeleton Cauchy stress is \(K\ln J^e/J\).
In the elastic specialization, \(a^p=1\) and \(J^e=J\), this becomes
\(K\ln J/J\).

For the mineral, we prescribe the volumetric energy per unit reference
mineral volume as
\begin{equation}
 \bar W_s(\bar J)=\frac{K_s}{2}(\ln\bar J)^2.
 \label{eq:mineral-volumetric-reference-energy}
\end{equation}
To identify its conjugate stress, consider a purely volumetric increment
of the true elastic deformation, holding the true-plastic factor and
elastic distention \(a^e\) fixed.  The same determinant identity and
spherical-increment condition give
\begin{equation}
 \delta\bar{\mbf F}^e(\bar{\mbf F}^e)^{-1}
 =\frac13\frac{\delta\bar J}{\bar J}\mbf I.
 \label{eq:mineral-spherical-deformation-increment}
\end{equation}
Multiplying on the right by \(\bar{\mbf F}^e\) gives
\begin{equation}
 \delta\bar{\mbf F}^e
 =\frac13\bar{\mbf F}^e\frac{\delta\bar J}{\bar J}.
 \label{eq:mineral-volumetric-deformation-variation}
\end{equation}
The normalized true-elastic shape is unchanged by the same cancellation
shown in \eqref{eq:elastic-shape-increment-expansion}.
This increment changes \(J^e=a^e\bar J\) together with \(\bar J\), and
pressure is allowed to change.  The drained skeleton increment instead
fixes pressure and allows elastic distention to adjust.
Let \(\bar{\bs\sigma}_s\) denote the intrinsic solid Cauchy stress.
Its contraction with
\(\delta\bar{\mbf F}^e(\bar{\mbf F}^e)^{-1}\) is work per unit current
mineral volume.  Multiplication by \(\bar J\) converts that work to the
reference mineral volume used by \(\bar W_s\).
Equating reversible work and stored-energy change gives
\begin{align}
 \delta\bar W_s
 &=\bar J\bar{\bs\sigma}_s:
 \left[\delta\bar{\mbf F}^e(\bar{\mbf F}^e)^{-1}\right],
 \label{eq:mineral-volumetric-stress-work}\\
 &=\bar J\bar{\bs\sigma}_s:
 \left(\frac13\mbf I\frac{\delta\bar J}{\bar J}\right),
 \label{eq:mineral-volumetric-work-substitution}\\
 &=\frac13\tr\bar{\bs\sigma}_s\,\delta\bar J.
 \label{eq:mineral-volumetric-work-trace}
\end{align}
Direct variation of \eqref{eq:mineral-volumetric-reference-energy} gives
\begin{align}
 \delta\bar W_s
 &=\frac{K_s}{2}\,2\ln\bar J\frac{\delta\bar J}{\bar J},
 \label{eq:mineral-logarithmic-energy-variation}\\
 &=\frac{K_s\ln\bar J}{\bar J}\,\delta\bar J.
 \label{eq:logarithmic-mineral-energy-derivative}
\end{align}
Equating the coefficients of the arbitrary volume increment
\(\delta\bar J\) yields
\begin{equation}
 \frac13\tr\bar{\bs\sigma}_s=\frac{K_s\ln\bar J}{\bar J}.
 \label{eq:matched-logarithmic-mineral-stress}
\end{equation}
The mean intrinsic stress is negative in compression, where \(\bar J<1\).
This equality prescribes the intrinsic mean-stress response.  The
deviatoric response is specified by the skeleton shear law below.

The tangent bulk modulus measures the change in mean intrinsic stress per
fractional change in current mineral volume.  Since that volume increment is
\(\mathrm d\bar J/\bar J=\mathrm d\ln\bar J\), the modulus is
\(\mathrm d(\tfrac13\tr\bar{\bs\sigma}_s)/\mathrm d\ln\bar J\).  Differentiating
\eqref{eq:matched-logarithmic-mineral-stress} gives
\begin{align}
 \frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\ln\bar J}
 \left(\frac13\tr\bar{\bs\sigma}_s\right)
 &=\bar J\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\bar J}
 \left(\frac13\tr\bar{\bs\sigma}_s\right),
 \label{eq:mineral-tangent-log-volume-definition}\\
 &=K_s\bar J\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\bar J}
 \left(\frac{\ln\bar J}{\bar J}\right),
 \label{eq:mineral-tangent-stress-substitution}\\
 &=K_s\bar J\left(\frac{1}{\bar J^2}
 -\frac{\ln\bar J}{\bar J^2}\right),
 \label{eq:mineral-tangent-logarithmic-differentiation}\\
 &=\frac{K_s(1-\ln\bar J)}{\bar J}.
 \label{eq:mineral-current-tangent-bulk-modulus}
\end{align}
At the reference mineral volume, \(\bar J=1\), this reduces to
\begin{equation}
 \left.\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm d\ln\bar J}
 \left(\frac13\tr\bar{\bs\sigma}_s\right)\right|_{\bar J=1}
 =K_s.
 \label{eq:mineral-reference-tangent-bulk-modulus}
\end{equation}
Thus \(K_s\) is the reference bulk modulus.  The mean intrinsic stress
describes mineral compression, while the pressure \(\bar p_s=p\) is
defined by the energy derivative in \eqref{eq:local-pressure-equilibrium}.

The two stress laws are connected by the solid's contribution to mixture
stress.  Water contributes the intrinsic stress \(-p\mbf I\) and occupies
volume fraction \(1-\phi_s\).  Adding the volume-weighted phase stresses
gives
\begin{equation}
 \bs\sigma=\phi_s\bar{\bs\sigma}_s-(1-\phi_s)p\mbf I.
 \label{eq:constitutive-phase-stress-sum}
\end{equation}
Substitution into \(\bs\sigma^{\prime}=\bs\sigma+p\mbf I\) gives
\begin{align}
 \bs\sigma^{\prime}
 &=\phi_s\bar{\bs\sigma}_s-(1-\phi_s)p\mbf I+p\mbf I,
 \label{eq:constitutive-single-prime-phase-substitution}\\
 &=\phi_s\left(\bar{\bs\sigma}_s+p\mbf I\right).
 \label{eq:constitutive-single-prime-phase-stress}
\end{align}
Taking one third of the trace and using \(\tr\mbf I=3\) yields
\begin{equation}
 \frac13\tr\bs\sigma^{\prime}
 =\phi_s\left(\frac13\tr\bar{\bs\sigma}_s+p\right).
 \label{eq:constitutive-single-prime-phase-trace}
\end{equation}
Finally, form \(\bs\tau^{\prime}=J\bs\sigma^{\prime}\), substitute
\eqref{eq:matched-logarithmic-mineral-stress}, and use solid mass
conservation, \(J\phi_s=\phi_{s0}\bar J\):
\begin{align}
 \frac13\tr\bs\tau^{\prime}
 &=J\phi_s\left(\frac13\tr\bar{\bs\sigma}_s+p\right),
 \label{eq:constitutive-kirchhoff-intrinsic-stress-trace}\\
 &=J\phi_s\left(p+\frac{K_s\ln\bar J}{\bar J}\right),
 \label{eq:constitutive-kirchhoff-mineral-substitution}\\
 &=\phi_{s0}\bar J
 \left(p+\frac{K_s\ln\bar J}{\bar J}\right),
 \label{eq:constitutive-kirchhoff-solid-mass-substitution}\\
 &=\phi_{s0}\left(p\bar J+K_s\ln\bar J\right).
 \label{eq:constitutive-single-prime-mineral-trace}
\end{align}
This relation also identifies the work conjugates of elastic distention
and mineral volume.  At fixed plastic state and elastic shape, the energy
differential in the independent arguments \((J^e,\bar J)\) is
\begin{align}
 \delta W_s
 &=\frac13\tr\bs\tau^{\prime}\,\delta\ln J^e
 -\phi_{s0}p\,\delta\bar J,
 \label{eq:coupled-volume-energy-work}\\
 &=\frac13\tr\bs\tau^{\prime}\,\delta\ln a^e
 +\phi_{s0}\frac13\tr\bar{\bs\sigma}_s\,\delta\bar J.
 \label{eq:distention-mineral-energy-work}
\end{align}
The first line uses the fixed-density stress derivative and the density
conjugacy in \eqref{eq:intrinsic-pressure-free-energy}.  In the second,
\(\delta\ln J^e=\delta\ln a^e+\delta\bar J/\bar J\), and
\eqref{eq:constitutive-kirchhoff-intrinsic-stress-trace} cancels the
pressure contribution to the coefficient of \(\delta\bar J\).
Thus, at fixed distention, mineral work per reference mixture volume is
\(\phi_{s0}\) times mineral work per reference mineral volume.  This is
the energy equivalence required by the mineral increment above.

Because the prescribed intrinsic mean stress depends only on \(\bar J\),
integration of \eqref{eq:distention-mineral-energy-work} at fixed \(a^e\)
gives the mineral term \(\phi_{s0}K_s(\ln\bar J)^2/2\).  The remaining
volumetric energy depends on \(a^e\) alone.  Its derivative with respect
to \(\ln a^e\) must equal \(\tr\bs\tau^{\prime}/3\).
At \(p=0\), the two effective stresses coincide, and matching the drained
and mineral stresses yields
\begin{align}
 K\ln J^e&=\phi_{s0}K_s\ln\bar J,
 \label{eq:drained-mineral-volume-matching}\\
 \ln a^e&=\left(1-\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)\ln J^e.
 \label{eq:drained-distention-volume-matching}
\end{align}
Expressing the drained mean Kirchhoff stress in terms of \(\ln a^e\)
and integrating fixes the distention energy.  With zero energy at
\(a^e=\bar J=1\), the combined volumetric energy is
\begin{equation}
 W_{s,\mathrm{vol}}(J^e,\bar J)
 =\frac{K}{2\left(1-K/(\phi_{s0}K_s)\right)}
 \left[\ln\left(\frac{J^e}{\bar J}\right)\right]^2
 +\frac{\phi_{s0}K_s}{2}(\ln\bar J)^2.
 \label{eq:distention-mineral-volumetric-energy}
\end{equation}
Here \(a^e=J^e/\bar J\) has been substituted to recover the independent
arguments used for pressure equilibrium.  On the drained curve,
the two terms reduce respectively to
\(K[1-K/(\phi_{s0}K_s)](\ln J^e)^2/2\) and
\(K^2(\ln J^e)^2/(2\phi_{s0}K_s)\).  Their sum is exactly the prescribed
drained skeleton energy \(K(\ln J^e)^2/2\).

The constructed energy determines mineral volume at a prescribed pore
pressure through \eqref{eq:intrinsic-pressure-free-energy}.  To evaluate
that derivative, vary \(\bar J\) at fixed \(J^e\), elastic shape,
and plastic state.  These constraints fix \(\mbf F^e\) and hence
\(\mbf F\), while \(a^e=J^e/\bar J\) changes.  Both volumetric energy
terms therefore contribute.  Differentiating
\eqref{eq:distention-mineral-volumetric-energy} gives
\begin{equation}
 \left.\pder{W_s}{\bar J}\right|_{\mbf F^e}
 =-\frac{K\ln(J^e/\bar J)}
 {\left(1-K/(\phi_{s0}K_s)\right)\bar J}
 +\frac{\phi_{s0}K_s\ln\bar J}{\bar J}.
 \label{eq:matched-energy-density-derivative}
\end{equation}
Applying the pressure-equilibrium relation
\eqref{eq:intrinsic-pressure-free-energy} sets this derivative equal to
\(-\phi_{s0}p\).  Rearranging gives
\begin{equation}
 p\bar J+K_s\ln\bar J
 =\frac{KK_s}{\phi_{s0}K_s-K}\ln\left(\frac{J^e}{\bar J}\right).
 \label{eq:constitutive-characteristic-volume-relation}
\end{equation}
Collecting the logarithmic terms yields the scalar mineral equation
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 K_s\ln\bar J
 +\left(1-\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)p\bar J
 -\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}}\ln J^e=0.}
 \label{eq:verification-mineral-factors}
\end{equation}
Once its positive root is found, mass conservation gives
\(\bar\rho_s=\bar\rho_{s0}/\bar J\) and
\(\phi_s=\phi_{s0}\bar J/J\).  Written as local pressure equilibrium,
the same equation is
\begin{equation}
 \bar p_s(\bar\rho_s,J^e)
 =\frac{(K/\phi_{s0})\ln J^e-K_s\ln\bar J}
 {\left(1-K/(\phi_{s0}K_s)\right)\bar J}=p.
 \label{eq:elastic-mineral-pressure}
\end{equation}
For \(p\geq0\), the residual in
\eqref{eq:verification-mineral-factors} is strictly increasing from
negative to positive infinity as \(\bar J\) increases, so its positive
root is unique.  For negative pore pressure, more than one positive root
can exist.  At fixed \(J^e\), we select the root that varies continuously
from \(\bar J=(J^e)^{K/(\phi_{s0}K_s)}\) as pressure decreases from zero.
We retain this solution only while the mineral-equation residual has a
positive derivative with respect to \(\bar J\), which requires
\begin{equation}
 K_s+\left(1-\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)p\bar J>0.
 \label{eq:trace-mineral-stability-domain}
\end{equation}
This condition keeps the scalar derivative positive.  We also require
\(0<\bar J<\exp(1)\) for positive mineral stiffness and
\(0<\phi_s<1\) for positive solid and pore volumes.

We next verify that the mineral-volume response and the single-prime
stress are compatible derivatives of the same pressure potential.
For the mineral-volume solution satisfying
\eqref{eq:trace-mineral-stability-domain}, the positive residual derivative
allows \(\bar J\) to be expressed locally as a smooth function of
\(\mbf F\) and \(p\) at fixed plastic state.  Evaluating the pressure
potential \eqref{eq:pressure-legendre-potential} at this solution and
differentiating gives
\begin{align}
 \delta W^{\prime}
 &=\delta W^{\prime\prime}+\phi_{s0}p\,\delta\bar J
 +\phi_{s0}\bar J\,\delta p,
 \label{eq:pressure-potential-increment}\\
 &=\left.\pder{W_s}{\mbf F}\right|_{\bar J}:\delta\mbf F
 +\left(\left.\pder{W_s}{\bar J}\right|_{\mbf F}
 +\phi_{s0}p\right)\delta\bar J
 +\phi_{s0}\bar J\,\delta p.
 \label{eq:pressure-potential-chain-rule}
\end{align}
The second line applies the chain rule to \(W_s\) evaluated at
\(\bar J(\mbf F,p)\).  Pressure equilibrium,
\eqref{eq:intrinsic-pressure-free-energy}, makes the coefficient of
\(\delta\bar J\) zero.  Using the single-prime stress definition
\eqref{eq:single-prime-fixed-density-conjugate} therefore gives
\begin{equation}
 \delta W^{\prime}=\mbf P^{\prime}:\delta\mbf F
 +\phi_{s0}\bar J\,\delta p.
 \label{eq:pressure-potential-conjugate-increment}
\end{equation}
Here pressure and deformation can both change; the mineral volume adjusts
to maintain pressure equilibrium.  For a volumetric increment at fixed
elastic shape, substitution of
\eqref{eq:total-volumetric-deformation-variation} gives
\begin{align}
 \delta W^{\prime}
 &=\frac13\left(\mbf P^{\prime}:\mbf F\right)\delta\ln J^e
 +\phi_{s0}\bar J\,\delta p,
 \label{eq:pressure-potential-volumetric-contraction}\\
 &=\frac13\tr\bs\tau^{\prime}\,\delta\ln J^e
 +\phi_{s0}\bar J\,\delta p.
 \label{eq:pressure-potential-volumetric-increment}
\end{align}
The last line uses \(\bs\tau^{\prime}=\mbf P^{\prime}\mbf F^T\).
Identifying the coefficients of the independent increments
\(\delta\ln J^e\) and \(\delta p\), with plastic state and elastic shape
fixed, yields
\begin{equation}
 \left.\pder{W^{\prime}}{\ln J^e}\right|_p
 =\frac13\tr\bs\tau^{\prime},
 \qquad
 \left.\pder{W^{\prime}}{p}\right|_{J^e}=\phi_{s0}\bar J.
 \label{eq:constitutive-legendre-volume-derivatives}
\end{equation}
Smoothness of the pressure potential requires its mixed derivatives with
respect to \(p\) and \(\ln J^e\) to agree.  Differentiating the first
identity with respect to pressure and the second with respect to
\(\ln J^e\), equating the results, and substituting the single-prime
stress trace \eqref{eq:constitutive-single-prime-mineral-trace} gives
\begin{equation}
 \phi_{s0}\left[\bar J+
 \left(p+\frac{K_s}{\bar J}\right)
 \left.\pder{\bar J}{p}\right|_{J^e}\right]
 =\phi_{s0}J^e\left.\pder{\bar J}{J^e}\right|_p.
 \label{eq:constitutive-mixed-energy-derivative-equality}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
 J^e\left.\pder{\bar J}{J^e}\right|_p
 -\left(p+\frac{K_s}{\bar J}\right)
 \left.\pder{\bar J}{p}\right|_{J^e}=\bar J.
 \label{eq:constitutive-compatible-mineral-pde}
\end{equation}
Implicit differentiation of \eqref{eq:verification-mineral-factors}
satisfies this identity.  It expresses the energy compatibility between
the pressure dependence of mean stress and the deformation dependence of
mineral volume.  The latter response determines the Biot coefficient below.

\subsection{Closed-form coefficient from implicit differentiation}

The derivative defining \(B\) fixes pore pressure, referential solid mass,
and the current plastic configuration.  Because \(J^e=J/a^p\),
\(\partial\ln J^e/\partial J=1/J\) under this constraint.
Differentiating \eqref{eq:verification-mineral-factors} therefore gives
\begin{align}
 \left[\frac{K_s}{\bar J}
 +\left(1-\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)p\right]
 \left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s,\mbf F^p}
 &=\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}J},
 \label{eq:solid-pressure-implicit-tangent}\\
 \left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s,\mbf F^p}
 &=\frac{K\bar J}
 {\phi_{s0}J\left[K_s+\left(1-\dfrac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)p\bar J\right]}.
 \label{eq:solid-density-eos-tangent}
\end{align}
Substitution into the definition of \(B\) yields
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{B=1-\frac{K\bar J}
 {J\left[K_s+\left(1-\dfrac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)p\bar J\right]}.}
 \label{eq:poroplastic-biot-correction}
\end{equation}
The coefficient can therefore be evaluated directly from \(J\), \(p\),
and the mineral-volume ratio \(\bar J\) obtained by solving
\eqref{eq:verification-mineral-factors}.
Plastic history enters through
\(J^e=J/a^p\) in that equation, while the coefficient's denominator
contains total \(J\), because its defining derivative is with respect to
total volume.

Solid mass conservation also gives \(\bar J=J\phi_s/\phi_{s0}\).
Substituting this identity into \eqref{eq:poroplastic-biot-correction}
and clearing the nested fractions gives the solid-volume-fraction form
\begin{equation}
 B=1-\frac{KK_s\phi_{s0}\phi_s}
 {\phi_{s0}^2K_s^2+(\phi_{s0}K_s-K)pJ\phi_s}.
 \label{eq:biot-solid-volume-fraction-form}
\end{equation}
Let \(\phi=1-\phi_s\) be the current porosity and
\(\phi_0=1-\phi_{s0}\) its reference value.  The equivalent porosity form is
\begin{equation}
 B=1-\frac{KK_s(1-\phi_0)(1-\phi)}
 {(1-\phi_0)^2K_s^2+\left[(1-\phi_0)K_s-K\right]pJ(1-\phi)}.
 \label{eq:biot-porosity-form}
\end{equation}
Both expressions use the current phase volumes determined by the same
mineral equation and retain the fixed-plastic-state definition of \(B\).

For a virgin elastic state, \(a^p=1\) and \(J^e=J\).  We denote the
coefficient evaluated with this mineral state by \(B_{\mathrm{el}}(p,J)\).
At \(J=\bar J=1\) and \(p=0\), the expression recovers
\(B_0=1-K/K_s\) \citep{biotwillis1957}.  At fixed total volume and pressure,
increased plastic distention reduces \(J^e\) and \(\bar J\) on the stable
branch, and hence increases \(B\).  This dependence follows from the
specified constitutive laws.

Differentiating the same mineral equation at fixed deformation and plastic
state gives the pressure response needed for fluid storage:
\begin{equation}
 \left.\pder{\bar J}{p}\right|_{J,\mbf F^p}
 =-\frac{\left(1-\dfrac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)\bar J^2}
 {K_s+\left(1-\dfrac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)p\bar J}.
 \label{eq:trace-mineral-pressure-tangent}
\end{equation}
It is negative under \eqref{eq:trace-mineral-stability-domain}: increasing
pore pressure at fixed skeleton volume compresses the mineral and increases
the volume available to water.

\subsection{Compatible energy and stress}

Completing the square in
\eqref{eq:distention-mineral-volumetric-energy} separates the drained energy
from the energy associated with departure from the drained mineral state.
Adding a neo-Hookean isochoric response of shear modulus \(G\) gives the
potential per unit original mixture reference volume,
\begin{align}
 W_s(\mbf F^e,\bar J)
 &=\frac{G}{2}\left[(J^e)^{-2/3}\tr(\mbf F^e(\mbf F^e)^T)-3\right]
 +\frac K2(\ln J^e)^2 \notag\\
 &\quad+\frac{\phi_{s0}K_s}{2\left(1-K/(\phi_{s0}K_s)\right)}
 \left[\ln\bar J-\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\ln J^e\right]^2.
 \label{eq:coupled-skeleton-mineral-energy}
\end{align}
Its derivative \(-\phi_{s0}^{-1}\partial W_s/\partial\bar J\) gives
\eqref{eq:elastic-mineral-pressure}.  Evaluating this energy on the mineral
root gives
\begin{align}
 W^{\prime\prime}(\mbf F^e,p)
 &=\frac{G}{2}\left[(J^e)^{-2/3}\tr(\mbf F^e(\mbf F^e)^T)-3\right]
 +\frac K2(\ln J^e)^2 \notag\\
 &\quad+\frac{\phi_{s0}}{2K_s}
 \left(1-\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)(p\bar J)^2.
 \label{eq:verification-fixed-pressure-potential}
\end{align}
Adding \(\phi_{s0}p\bar J\) gives \(W^{\prime}\), as defined in
\eqref{eq:pressure-legendre-potential}.  Both fixed-pressure deformation
derivatives include the dependence of
\(\bar J\) on \(J^e\).  The resulting single-prime Kirchhoff stress is
\begin{equation}
 \bs\tau^{\prime}
 =G(J^e)^{-2/3}\operatorname{dev}\left(\mbf F^e(\mbf F^e)^T\right)
 +K\left(\ln J^e+\frac{p\bar J}{K_s}\right)\mbf I.
 \label{eq:matched-logarithmic-single-prime-stress}
\end{equation}
The Biot transform gives the double-prime stress,
\begin{align}
 \bs\tau^{\prime\prime}
 &=G(J^e)^{-2/3}\operatorname{dev}\left(\mbf F^e(\mbf F^e)^T\right)
 \notag\\
 &\quad+K\left\{\ln J^e+
 \frac{\left(1-\dfrac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)(p\bar J)^2}
 {K_s\left[K_s+\left(1-\dfrac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)p\bar J\right]}
 \right\}\mbf I.
 \label{eq:matched-logarithmic-double-prime-stress}
\end{align}
Dividing by total \(J\) gives the corresponding Cauchy stresses.  The
pressure correction to the double-prime stress vanishes quadratically near
the reference state.  The single-prime stress supplies the work conjugate
for the plastic update below.

For an elastic specimen under equal hydrostatic pressure on the pore fluid
and external boundary (unjacketed loading), \(J=\bar J\),
\(p=-K_s\ln J/J\), and \(\phi_s=\phi_{s0}\).  The single-prime stress
vanishes and the total stress is \(-p\mbf I\).  In the incompressible-mineral
limit, \(K_s\to\infty\), the mineral volume remains fixed,
\(B\to1\), and the double-prime stress reduces to the drained skeleton
response.

\subsection{Plastic driving stress, yield, and flow}
\label{sec:plastic-loading-incremental-rate}

Plastic flow changes \(\mbf F^e\) at fixed total deformation.  The
single-prime Kirchhoff stress and the deviatoric stress in the intermediate
configuration are
\begin{align}
 \bs\tau^{\prime}
 &=\mbf P^{\prime}\mbf F^T,
 \label{eq:kirchhoff-stress-definition}\\
 \bs\tau^{\prime}&=\mbf P^{\prime\prime}\mbf F^T+(1-B)pJ\mbf I,
 \label{eq:single-prime-kirchhoff-stress}\\
 \mbf S&=(\mbf F^e)^T
 \operatorname{dev}\bs\tau^{\prime}(\mbf F^e)^{-T}.
 \label{eq:mandel-driving-stress}
\end{align}
For the isotropic energy, \(\mbf S\) is symmetric.  Define the
compression-positive mean and equivalent shear stresses by
\begin{equation}
 p^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{3}\tr\bs\tau^{\prime},
 \qquad q=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\mbf S:\mbf S}.
 \label{eq:finite-plastic-stress-invariants}
\end{equation}
The prime distinguishes the plastic mean-stress argument from pore pressure
\(p\).  We choose the Drucker--Prager surface and dilation rule
\begin{align}
 f&=q-Mp^{\prime}-c\leq0,
 \label{eq:dp-yield-observable}\\
 \dot{\mbf F}^p(\mbf F^p)^{-1}
 &=\dot\gamma\left(\frac{3\mbf S}{2q}+\frac{\beta}{3}\mbf I\right),
 \label{eq:dp-flow}\\
 \dot\gamma&\geq0,
 \qquad \dot\gamma f=0.
 \label{eq:plastic-complementarity}
\end{align}
Here \(M\) is the friction slope, \(c\) is cohesion, and \(\beta\) is the
dilation slope, with \(0\leq\beta\leq M\) and \(c\geq0\).  We consider
the smooth cone branch \(q>0\).  Taking the trace of the flow rule gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot a^p}{a^p}=\beta\dot\gamma.
 \label{eq:reconstruct-pore-allocation}
\end{equation}
The remaining plastic factor is isochoric.  On the yield surface, the
plastic dissipation per unit reference volume is
\begin{align}
 \mc D^p&=\dot\gamma(q-\beta p^{\prime}),
 \label{eq:plastic-power}\\
 \mc D^p&=\dot\gamma\left[c+(M-\beta)p^{\prime}\right]\geq0.
 \label{eq:plastic-dissipation-on-yield}
\end{align}
The inequality follows from \(q\geq0\), the yield condition, and the stated
parameter restrictions.  These equations specify a dilative model without
hardening; pore-collapse, cap, and apex mechanisms require additional
constitutive branches.

Over a load increment, introduce the symmetric plastic flow increment
\(\mbf W\) and solve
\begin{align}
 \mbf F^p_{n+1}&=\exp(\mbf W)\mbf F^p_n,
 \label{eq:plastic-exponential-update}\\
 \mbf W&=\Delta\gamma\left(
 \frac{3\mbf S_{n+1}}{2q_{n+1}}+\frac{\beta}{3}\mbf I\right),
 \label{eq:implicit-plastic-flow-increment}\\
 f_{n+1}&=0
 \quad\text{on the active branch}.
 \label{eq:discrete-consistency}
\end{align}
This exponential update evaluates the flow direction at the returned state.
The elastic trial uses \(\mbf F^p_n\); an admissible trial leaves the history
unchanged.  An active trial requires a coupled solve for the six independent
components of \(\mbf W\) and \(\Delta\gamma\).  At every local iteration,
\(\mbf F^e\), intrinsic density, \(B\), and stress are recomputed from the
same candidate state.  The determinant identity
\(a^p_{n+1}=a^p_n\exp(\beta\Delta\gamma)\) provides an independent check
of this update.
