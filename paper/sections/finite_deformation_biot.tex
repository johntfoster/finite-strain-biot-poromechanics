\section{Deformation, distention, and pressure coupling}
\label{sec:finite-deformation-biot}

A saturated porous solid changes volume through deformation of its constituent
material and through changes in pore volume.  These mechanisms enter the stress
and the phase balances through different quantities.  We first identify those
quantities and then obtain the Biot coefficient from the mineral-volume
response at fixed pore pressure.

\subsection{Solid motion and phase mass}
\label{sec:solved-solid-state}

Let the solid motion map a reference position \(\mbf X\) to
\(\mbf x=\bs\chi(\mbf X,t)\).  Its deformation gradient and solid-reference
Jacobian are
\begin{equation}
 \mbf F=\nabla_{\mbf X}\bs\chi,
 \qquad J=\det\mbf F>0.
 \label{eq:skeleton-kinematics}
\end{equation}
Following Drumheller \citep[Sec.~3]{drumheller2000}, we separate the true
motion of the solid material from the distention gradient,
\begin{equation}
 \mbf F=\mbf A\bar{\mbf F},
 \qquad \bar J=\det\bar{\mbf F},
 \qquad a=\det\mbf A,
 \qquad J=a\bar J.
 \label{eq:inelastic-multiplicative-split}
\end{equation}
Here \(\bar{\mbf F}\) is the true-deformation gradient, \(\bar J\) is the
true-deformation Jacobian, \(\mbf A\) is the distention gradient, and \(a\)
is the distention.  We choose a reference state with \(J_0=\bar J_0=a_0=1\).
The true-deformation Jacobian measures intrinsic solid volume change;
distention describes how that material occupies mixture volume.

Let \(\bar\rho_s\) be the solid intrinsic density and \(\phi_s\) its volume
fraction.  The partial density per unit current mixture volume is
\begin{equation}
 \rho_s=\phi_s\bar\rho_s.
 \label{eq:solid-volume-fraction-relation}
\end{equation}
For a source-free solid moving with velocity \(\mbf v_s\), mass conservation
in spatial and solid-reference forms is
\begin{align}
 \left.\pder{\rho_s}{t}\right|_{\mbf x}
 +\nabla_{\mbf x}\mathbin{\cdot}(\rho_s\mbf v_s)&=0,
 \label{eq:spatial-solid-mass-balance}\\
 \left.\pder{(J\rho_s)}{t}\right|_{\mbf X}&=0.
 \label{eq:reference-solid-mass-balance}
\end{align}
For the uniform reference state used here,
\(J\rho_s=\rho_{s0}=\phi_{s0}\bar\rho_{s0}\).  The true motion preserves
mineral mass, giving
\begin{equation}
 \bar J\equiv\frac{\bar\rho_{s0}}{\bar\rho_s},
 \qquad \phi_s=\frac{\phi_{s0}\bar J}{J},
 \qquad \phi_s a=\phi_{s0}.
 \label{eq:distension}
\end{equation}
Thus the intrinsic density determines true volume change, while the ratio of
partial to intrinsic density determines the solid volume fraction.  Saturation
then gives the water volume fraction \(\phi_f=1-\phi_s\).

\subsection{Pressure and the two effective stresses}
\label{sec:intrinsic-pressure}

Let \(\psi_s\) denote the solid free energy per unit mass, with skeleton
deformation, intrinsic density, and constitutive internal state as its
arguments.  Local equilibrium identifies the density conjugate with the pore
pressure \citep[eq.~(27c)]{fosterxu2025},
\begin{equation}
 p=\bar p_f=\bar p_s
 =\bar\rho_s^2\left.\pder{\psi_s}{\bar\rho_s}\right|_{\mbf F},
 \label{eq:local-pressure-equilibrium}
\end{equation}
where \(\bar p_f\) and \(\bar p_s\) are the intrinsic water and solid
thermodynamic pressures, respectively, and any internal variables are held
fixed.  The grain pressure \(\bar p_g\) is defined as the negative mean
intrinsic solid stress and also contains the contribution of skeleton
deformation.  Consequently, pressure equilibrium does not require
the effective solid stress to be deviatoric.

Write \(W_s=\rho_{s0}\psi_s\) per unit reference mixture volume.  In terms
of \(\bar J\), the density conjugacy becomes
\begin{equation}
 p=-\frac{1}{\phi_{s0}}
 \left.\pder{W_s}{\bar J}\right|_{\mbf F}.
 \label{eq:intrinsic-pressure-free-energy}
\end{equation}
The independent arguments of \(W_s\) are \(\mbf F\) and
\(\bar\rho_s\), equivalently \(\mbf F\) and \(\bar J\), with internal
variables held fixed.  Solving this pressure relation determines
\(\bar J(\mbf F,p)\).  Denote \(W_s\) evaluated on that state by
\(W^{\prime\prime}(\mbf F,p)\).  The pressure Legendre transform replaces
the intrinsic-density argument by pressure and defines the potential
\begin{equation}
 W^{\prime}(\mbf F,p)
 =W^{\prime\prime}(\mbf F,p)+\phi_{s0}p\bar J(\mbf F,p).
 \label{eq:pressure-legendre-potential}
\end{equation}
The single-prime first Piola stress is the deformation derivative of
\(W_s\) at fixed intrinsic density, equivalently the derivative of the
transformed potential \(W^{\prime}\) at fixed pressure.  The density-response
terms cancel in the latter derivative by
\eqref{eq:intrinsic-pressure-free-energy}.  The double-prime stress differentiates \(W^{\prime\prime}\) at
fixed pressure \citep[eqs.~(34)--(36)]{fosterxu2025}:
\begin{align}
 \mbf P^{\prime}
 &=\left.\pder{W_s}{\mbf F}\right|_{\bar J}
 ,\label{eq:single-prime-fixed-density-conjugate}\\
 \mbf P^{\prime}&=\left.\pder{W^{\prime}}{\mbf F}\right|_p,
 \label{eq:single-prime-energy-conjugate}\\
 \mbf P^{\prime\prime}
 &=\left.\pder{W^{\prime\prime}}{\mbf F}\right|_p.
 \label{eq:double-prime-energy-conjugate}
\end{align}
Internal variables remain fixed in these constitutive derivatives.

\subsection{The finite-deformation Biot coefficient}
\label{sec:biot-coefficient-and-momentum}

For the isotropic volume response considered here, the fixed-state dependence
of \(\bar J\) on deformation is through \(J\).  Differentiating
\eqref{eq:pressure-legendre-potential} gives
\begin{equation}
 \mbf P^{\prime}
 =\mbf P^{\prime\prime}
 +\phi_{s0}p\left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s}
 J\mbf F^{-T}.
 \label{eq:single-prime-first-piola-mineral-transform}
\end{equation}
The corresponding Biot coefficient is
\begin{equation}
 B=1-\phi_{s0}\left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s}.
 \label{eq:finite-deformation-biot-coefficient}
\end{equation}
This is the one-solid coefficient that we derived in Foster and Xu
\citep[eq.~(39)]{fosterxu2025}.  It measures the fraction of a skeleton-volume
increment available to change pore volume when pressure, referential solid
mass, and the current internal state are fixed.  An incompressible mineral
has \(\bar J=1\) and \(B=1\).

Pushing the stresses forward gives
\begin{align}
 \bs\sigma^{\prime}
 &=\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}+(1-B)p\mbf I,
 \label{eq:single-prime-biot-transform}\\
 \bs\sigma
 &=\bs\sigma^{\prime}-p\mbf I,
 \label{eq:total-cauchy-single-prime}\\
 \bs\sigma
 &=\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}-Bp\mbf I.
 \label{eq:total-cauchy-stress}
\end{align}
Here \(\bs\sigma^{\prime}=J^{-1}\mbf P^{\prime}\mbf F^T\), with the same
push-forward for the double-prime stress, and \(\mbf I\) is the identity
tensor.  The total first Piola stress and quasi-static balance are
\begin{gather}
 \mbf P=\mbf P^{\prime\prime}-BpJ\mbf F^{-T},
 \label{eq:total-first-piola-stress}\\
 \nabla_{\mbf X}\mathbin{\cdot}\mbf P+J\rho\mbf g=\mbf0,
 \label{eq:reference-momentum-balance}
\end{gather}
where \(\rho\) is total mixture density and \(\mbf g\) is body-force
acceleration.  The same coefficient that enters the total stress also
connects the drained response to the single-prime stress.  This connection
becomes consequential when the latter supplies the work conjugate for
plastic flow.

\section{Poroplastic constitutive response}
\label{sec:inelastic-plastic-driving-stress}

We choose an isotropic constitutive model to examine how irreversible
changes in distention enter the pressure coupling.  The matched logarithmic skeleton and mineral laws yield a closed-form
coefficient at each current plastic state.  The
multiplicative kinematics follow Drumheller; the mineral EOS, yield
surface, and dilation rule specified below are constitutive choices.

\subsection{Plastic distention and mineral compression}

We specialize the solid mixture energy to one nonreacting constituent at
constant temperature, with spherical distention and isochoric true-plastic
flow.  The elastic and plastic factors satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \bar{\mbf F}=\bar{\mbf F}^e\bar{\mbf F}^p,
 \qquad \mbf A=\mbf A^e\mbf A^p,
 \qquad \det\bar{\mbf F}^p=1,
 \qquad
 \mbf A^e=(a^e)^{1/3}\mbf I,
 \qquad \mbf A^p=(a^p)^{1/3}\mbf I.
 \label{eq:inelastic-kinematic-splits}
\end{equation}
The spherical plastic factor commutes with the true elastic deformation,
giving the aggregate decomposition
\begin{equation}
 \mbf F=\mbf F^e\mbf F^p,
 \qquad \mbf F^e=(a^e)^{1/3}\bar{\mbf F}^e,
 \qquad \mbf F^p=(a^p)^{1/3}\bar{\mbf F}^p,
 \qquad \det\mbf F^p=a^p.
 \label{eq:aggregate-plastic-split}
\end{equation}
Consequently, \(J^e=\det\mbf F^e=J/a^p\).  True mass conservation and
isochoric true-plastic flow give
\(\det\bar{\mbf F}^e=\bar J=\bar\rho_{s0}/\bar\rho_s\), so that
\(J^e=a^e\bar J\).  Elastic skeleton volume is thus divided between
distention of the porous structure and compression of the mineral.

\subsection{Matched logarithmic skeleton and mineral laws}
\label{sec:elastic-verification-closure}
\label{sec:numerical-solid-eos}

Let \(K\) and \(K_s\) be the reference drained skeleton and mineral bulk
moduli, with \(0<K<\phi_{s0}K_s\).  We prescribe the same logarithmic
volumetric form for the drained skeleton and the mineral.  At fixed plastic
state, their mean stresses satisfy
\begin{align}
 \left.\frac13\tr\bs\tau^{\prime\prime}\right|_{p=0}
 &=K\ln J^e,
 \label{eq:drained-volumetric-energy-derivative}\\
 \bar p_g=-\frac13\tr\bar{\bs\sigma}_s
 &=-\frac{K_s\ln\bar J}{\bar J}.
 \label{eq:matched-logarithmic-mineral-stress}
\end{align}
Here \(\bs\tau^{\prime\prime}=J\bs\sigma^{\prime\prime}\) is the
Kirchhoff stress formed with the total mixture Jacobian.  In the elastic
specialization, \(J^e=J\), and the mean skeleton Cauchy stress is
\(K\ln J/J\), matching the mineral's logarithmic Cauchy-stress form.
The mechanical grain pressure \(\bar p_g\) is distinct from the
thermodynamic pressure \(\bar p_s=p\).
The mineral tangent modulus is \(K_s(1-\ln\bar J)/\bar J\), so \(K_s\)
is its reference value.

The solid stress trace relates the two volume changes.  Volume weighting
of the intrinsic phase stresses gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac13\tr\bs\tau^{\prime}
 =\phi_{s0}\left(p\bar J+K_s\ln\bar J\right).
 \label{eq:constitutive-single-prime-mineral-trace}
\end{equation}
On the drained path this fixes
\(\bar J=(J^e)^{K/(\phi_{s0}K_s)}\).  Equality of the mixed derivatives
of the pressure Legendre potential extends that path to finite pressure;
Appendix~\ref{sec:stress-trace-biot-construction} gives the integration.
Holding the current plastic state fixed gives the scalar mineral equation
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 K_s\ln\bar J
 +\left(1-\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)p\bar J
 -\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}}\ln J^e=0.}
 \label{eq:verification-mineral-factors}
\end{equation}
Once its positive root is found, mass conservation gives
\(\bar\rho_s=\bar\rho_{s0}/\bar J\) and
\(\phi_s=\phi_{s0}\bar J/J\).  Written as local pressure equilibrium,
the same equation is
\begin{equation}
 \bar p_s(\bar\rho_s,J^e)
 =\frac{(K/\phi_{s0})\ln J^e-K_s\ln\bar J}
 {\left(1-K/(\phi_{s0}K_s)\right)\bar J}=p.
 \label{eq:elastic-mineral-pressure}
\end{equation}
For \(p\geq0\), the scalar residual is strictly increasing from negative
to positive infinity, and its positive root is unique.  At negative pressure,
we continue the drained branch with
\(K_s+(1-K/(\phi_{s0}K_s))p\bar J>0\).
We also require \(0<\bar J<\exp(1)\) and \(0<\phi_s<1\).

\subsection{Closed-form coefficient from implicit differentiation}

The derivative defining \(B\) fixes pore pressure, referential solid mass,
and the current plastic configuration.  Because \(J^e=J/a^p\),
\(\partial\ln J^e/\partial J=1/J\) under this constraint.
Differentiating \eqref{eq:verification-mineral-factors} therefore gives
\begin{align}
 \left[\frac{K_s}{\bar J}
 +\left(1-\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)p\right]
 \left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s,\mbf F^p}
 &=\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}J},
 \label{eq:solid-pressure-implicit-tangent}\\
 \left.\pder{\bar J}{J}\right|_{p,J\rho_s,\mbf F^p}
 &=\frac{K\bar J}
 {\phi_{s0}J\left[K_s+\left(1-\dfrac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)p\bar J\right]}.
 \label{eq:solid-density-eos-tangent}
\end{align}
Substitution into the definition of \(B\) yields
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{B=1-\frac{K\bar J}
 {J\left[K_s+\left(1-\dfrac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)p\bar J\right]}.}
 \label{eq:poroplastic-biot-correction}
\end{equation}
This is a closed-form expression in the current states \(J\), \(p\), and
\(\bar J\): evaluating the coefficient requires no state derivatives or
additional tangent solve.  The mineral state itself remains the solution of
\eqref{eq:verification-mineral-factors}.  Plastic history enters through
\(J^e=J/a^p\) in that equation, while the coefficient's denominator
contains total \(J\), because its defining derivative is with respect to
total volume.

For a virgin elastic state, the same expression is evaluated with
\(\bar J(p,J)\):
\begin{equation}
 B_{\mathrm{el}}(p,J)
 =1-\frac{K\bar J(p,J)}
 {J\left[K_s+\left(1-\dfrac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)p\bar J(p,J)\right]}.
 \label{eq:elastic-finite-deformation-biot-coefficient}
\end{equation}
At \(J=\bar J=1\) and \(p=0\), both expressions recover
\(B_0=1-K/K_s\) \citep{biotwillis1957}.  At fixed total volume and pressure,
increased plastic distention reduces \(J^e\) and \(\bar J\) on the stable
branch, and hence increases \(B\).  This dependence follows from the
specified constitutive laws and provides a direct link between stored
plastic volume change and subsequent pressure coupling.

\subsection{Compatible energy and stress}

The stress trace construction also determines an energy consistent with
local pressure equilibrium.  With a neo-Hookean isochoric response of shear
modulus \(G\), a potential per unit original mixture reference volume is
\begin{align}
 W_s(\mbf F^e,\bar J)
 &=\frac{G}{2}\left[(J^e)^{-2/3}\tr(\mbf F^e(\mbf F^e)^T)-3\right]
 +\frac K2(\ln J^e)^2 \notag\\
 &\quad+\frac{\phi_{s0}K_s}{2\left(1-K/(\phi_{s0}K_s)\right)}
 \left[\ln\bar J-\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\ln J^e\right]^2.
 \label{eq:coupled-skeleton-mineral-energy}
\end{align}
Its derivative \(-\phi_{s0}^{-1}\partial W_s/\partial\bar J\) gives
\eqref{eq:elastic-mineral-pressure}.  Evaluating this energy on the mineral
root and taking the pressure Legendre transform gives
\begin{align}
 W^{\prime\prime}(\mbf F^e,p)
 &=\frac{G}{2}\left[(J^e)^{-2/3}\tr(\mbf F^e(\mbf F^e)^T)-3\right]
 +\frac K2(\ln J^e)^2 \notag\\
 &\quad+\frac{\phi_{s0}}{2K_s}
 \left(1-\frac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)(p\bar J)^2,
 \label{eq:verification-fixed-pressure-potential}\\
 W^{\prime}(\mbf F^e,p)
 &=W^{\prime\prime}(\mbf F^e,p)+\phi_{s0}p\bar J.
 \label{eq:constitutive-pressure-legendre-energy}
\end{align}
Their fixed-pressure deformation derivatives include the dependence of
\(\bar J\) on \(J^e\).  The resulting single-prime Kirchhoff stress is
\begin{equation}
 \bs\tau^{\prime}
 =G(J^e)^{-2/3}\operatorname{dev}\left(\mbf F^e(\mbf F^e)^T\right)
 +K\left(\ln J^e+\frac{p\bar J}{K_s}\right)\mbf I.
 \label{eq:matched-logarithmic-single-prime-stress}
\end{equation}
The Biot transform gives the double-prime stress,
\begin{align}
 \bs\tau^{\prime\prime}
 &=G(J^e)^{-2/3}\operatorname{dev}\left(\mbf F^e(\mbf F^e)^T\right)
 \notag\\
 &\quad+K\left\{\ln J^e+
 \frac{\left(1-\dfrac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)(p\bar J)^2}
 {K_s\left[K_s+\left(1-\dfrac{K}{\phi_{s0}K_s}\right)p\bar J\right]}
 \right\}\mbf I.
 \label{eq:matched-logarithmic-double-prime-stress}
\end{align}
Dividing by total \(J\) gives the corresponding Cauchy stresses.  The
pressure correction to the double-prime stress vanishes quadratically near
the reference state.  The single-prime stress supplies the work conjugate
for the plastic update below.

\subsection{Work conjugacy, yield, and flow}
\label{sec:plastic-loading-incremental-rate}

Plastic flow changes \(\mbf F^e\) at fixed total deformation.  The
single-prime Kirchhoff stress and the deviatoric stress in the intermediate
configuration are
\begin{align}
 \bs\tau^{\prime}
 &=\mbf P^{\prime}\mbf F^T,
 \label{eq:kirchhoff-stress-definition}\\
 \bs\tau^{\prime}&=\mbf P^{\prime\prime}\mbf F^T+(1-B)pJ\mbf I,
 \label{eq:single-prime-kirchhoff-stress}\\
 \mbf S&=(\mbf F^e)^T
 \operatorname{dev}\bs\tau^{\prime}(\mbf F^e)^{-T}.
 \label{eq:mandel-driving-stress}
\end{align}
For the isotropic energy, \(\mbf S\) is symmetric.  Define the
compression-positive mean and equivalent shear stresses by
\begin{equation}
 p^{\prime}=-\frac{1}{3}\tr\bs\tau^{\prime},
 \qquad q=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\mbf S:\mbf S}.
 \label{eq:finite-plastic-stress-invariants}
\end{equation}
The prime distinguishes the plastic mean-stress argument from pore pressure
\(p\).  We choose the Drucker--Prager surface and dilation rule
\begin{align}
 f&=q-Mp^{\prime}-c\leq0,
 \label{eq:dp-yield-observable}\\
 \dot{\mbf F}^p(\mbf F^p)^{-1}
 &=\dot\gamma\left(\frac{3\mbf S}{2q}+\frac{\beta}{3}\mbf I\right),
 \label{eq:dp-flow}\\
 \dot\gamma&\geq0,
 \qquad \dot\gamma f=0.
 \label{eq:plastic-complementarity}
\end{align}
Here \(M\) is the friction slope, \(c\) is cohesion, and \(\beta\) is the
dilation slope, with \(0\leq\beta\leq M\) and \(c\geq0\).  We consider
the smooth cone branch \(q>0\).  Taking the trace of the flow rule gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\dot a^p}{a^p}=\beta\dot\gamma.
 \label{eq:reconstruct-pore-allocation}
\end{equation}
The remaining plastic factor is isochoric.  On the yield surface, the
plastic dissipation per unit reference volume is
\begin{align}
 \mc D^p&=\dot\gamma(q-\beta p^{\prime}),
 \label{eq:plastic-power}\\
 \mc D^p&=\dot\gamma\left[c+(M-\beta)p^{\prime}\right]\geq0.
 \label{eq:plastic-dissipation-on-yield}
\end{align}
The inequality follows from \(q\geq0\), the yield condition, and the stated
parameter restrictions.  These equations specify a dilative model without
hardening; pore-collapse, cap, and apex mechanisms require additional
constitutive branches.

Over a load increment, introduce the symmetric plastic flow increment
\(\mbf W\) and solve
\begin{align}
 \mbf F^p_{n+1}&=\exp(\mbf W)\mbf F^p_n,
 \label{eq:plastic-exponential-update}\\
 \mbf W&=\Delta\gamma\left(
 \frac{3\mbf S_{n+1}}{2q_{n+1}}+\frac{\beta}{3}\mbf I\right),
 \label{eq:implicit-plastic-flow-increment}\\
 f_{n+1}&=0
 \quad\text{on the active branch}.
 \label{eq:discrete-consistency}
\end{align}
This exponential update evaluates the flow direction at the returned state.
The elastic trial uses \(\mbf F^p_n\); an admissible trial leaves the history
unchanged.  An active trial requires a coupled solve for the six independent
components of \(\mbf W\) and \(\Delta\gamma\).  At every local iteration,
\(\mbf F^e\), intrinsic density, \(B\), and stress are recomputed from the
same candidate state.  The determinant identity
\(a^p_{n+1}=a^p_n\exp(\beta\Delta\gamma)\) provides an independent check
of this update.
