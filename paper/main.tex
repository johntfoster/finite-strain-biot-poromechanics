\begin{abstract}
Plastic deformation changes the pore volume of a saturated solid and the
contribution of pore pressure to stress.  We derive
a closed-form finite-deformation Biot coefficient for matched logarithmic
skeleton and mineral laws.  Implicit differentiation of solid mass and the
mineral equation of state at fixed pressure and current plastic configuration
gives the coefficient directly in terms of current volume, pressure, and
intrinsic solid density.  A single scalar equation determines the mineral
volume.
A multiplicative split separates isochoric true-plastic flow from plastic
distention.  An implicit exponential return mapping updates these mechanisms
and recomputes the mineral state, coefficient, and driving stress together.
Automatic differentiation includes these coupled changes in the Jacobian
used by Newton's method.

Independent checks verify mass conservation, the mineral equation of state,
energy-derived stress, plastic flow, yield consistency, and the derivatives
used to evaluate the coefficient and solve the discrete equations.  Material-point calculations demonstrate a history-dependent
coefficient on unloading and feedback into plastic flow at nonzero pore
pressure.  The elastic specialization is coupled to solid and water mass
conservation and momentum balance and tested against the Mandel solution.
A larger compression test shows how the coefficient varies across the
specimen during drainage.  These calculations demonstrate how mineral
compressibility and plastic volume change affect pressure coupling.
Verification of coupled flow with plastic deformation and comparison with
material data remain future work.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} finite-deformation poromechanics; Biot coefficient;

mass balance; Mandel problem
