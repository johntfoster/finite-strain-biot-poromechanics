\begin{abstract}
Pore pressure couples deformation and plastic flow in a saturated porous solid
through the Biot coefficient.  For a compressible mineral, this coefficient
connects the drained skeleton stress to the stress that drives inelastic
deformation.  Its evolution therefore changes both the mechanical response
and the subsequent plastic flow.  We develop an implicit constitutive
construction of the finite-deformation Biot coefficient and its coupling to
poroplasticity.  The coefficient follows from the mineral-volume response to
skeleton deformation at fixed intrinsic phase pressure and referential solid
mass.  Implicit differentiation of the complete local constitutive residual
system evaluates this response on the active set selected by the converged
return mapping.  A multiplicative split separates isochoric true-plastic flow
from plastic pore allocation, which changes the solid volume fraction and
carries the deformation history into the coefficient.

The finite-element formulation couples this local update to spatial solid and
water mass conservation and momentum balance.  Automatic differentiation
retains the dependence of the constitutive state and implicit Biot tangent in
the global Newton Jacobian.  Analytical and finite-difference checks verify
the elastic constitutive tangent and its global propagation, while the Mandel
problem tests pressure and displacement against the analytical solution.
A finite-deformation continuation demonstrates the evolution of the
coefficient under compression.  Material-point calculations show how plastic
pore allocation changes the coefficient and how that change feeds back into
plastic flow at nonzero pore pressure.  These results establish the
constitutive coupling and verify its elastic finite-element limit and
material-level inelastic response.  Coupled finite-element verification with
active plastic flow and physical validation remain subsequent steps.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} finite-deformation poromechanics; Biot coefficient;

mass balance; Mandel problem
