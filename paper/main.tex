\begin{abstract}
Mineral compression and plastic changes in pore volume govern how pore
pressure couples to deformation in a saturated porous solid.  For
logarithmic skeleton and mineral laws, we derive a coupled stored energy,
its effective stresses, and a closed-form Biot coefficient at finite
strain.  Mineral compression introduces a pressure correction to the
effective stress obtained from the energy at fixed pressure.  Linearization
about the undeformed, zero-pressure state recovers classical Biot
elasticity.  A scalar equation determines the mineral volume, from which
the Biot coefficient follows directly using the current total volume and
pore pressure.  The plastic formulation separates volume-preserving
mineral deformation from permanent changes in pore volume.  We solve for
the mineral state and plastic deformation together, using automatic
differentiation to retain their coupling in Newton's method.

Independent checks verify mass conservation, the constitutive equations,
and the derivatives used in the numerical solution.  The coupled
finite-element formulation recovers the elastic Mandel solution and shows
reversible changes in the Biot coefficient under larger compression and
drainage.  Material-point calculations show how plastic history changes
the coefficient during unloading and how its current value affects
further plastic flow.  A poroplastic compression test verifies the plastic
contribution to fluid storage and shows a persistent increase in the
coefficient relative to its elastic value at the same pressure and total
volume.  For the selected synthetic geomaterial, plastic dilation reverses
fluid exchange from outflow to inflow.  These results connect mineral
compression and permanent pore-volume change to evolving pressure
coupling; comparison with material data remains future work.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} finite-deformation poromechanics; Biot coefficient;
