\begin{abstract}
The finite-deformation Biot coefficient measures the mineral-distension
response to skeleton deformation at fixed pore pressure.  We
separate this local constitutive derivative from phase mass conservation.
Spatial solid mass balance evolves the bulk solid partial density, while a
local constitutive update supplies the intrinsic solid state.  The solid volume
fraction then follows algebraically from the partial and intrinsic densities.
The nonlinear Biot coefficient is obtained by
implicit differentiation of the complete local constitutive residual system
at fixed intrinsic phase pressure and referential solid mass \(J\rho_s\).  The
spatial water balance is closed by a
barotropic pressure--density equation of state.  The Biot transform then maps
the constitutive double-prime stress to the single-prime material stress and
the total mixture stress while preserving their distinct energetic meanings.

The solid-reference finite-element formulation uses quadratic displacement,
continuous linear water pressure, and quadratic solid partial density.
Automatic differentiation propagates the local constitutive residual
derivatives, implicit tangent, Biot coefficient, derived volume fraction,
and both phase-storage rates through the monolithic residual.  The elastic
specialization supplies closed-form and centered-difference checks of the
fixed-pressure constitutive tangent, and a PETSc
finite-difference comparison checks the assembled Jacobian.
A displacement-driven, small-load Mandel benchmark compares pressure and
lateral displacement with the Cheng--Detournay plane-strain series.  The
maximum plotted-profile pressure difference is \(1.956\times10^{-2}\) of the
analytical pressure scale, and the maximum horizontal-displacement-profile
difference is \(3.022\times10^{-4}\) of its scale.  A synthetic
finite-deformation continuation to \(10\%\) platen compression decreases the
spatially averaged coefficient from \(0.600\) to \(0.51417\).  These tests
verify the balance-law and constitutive coupling for the stated single-solid,
single-water elastic model; they do not constitute independent validation of
the mineral equation of state.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} finite-deformation poromechanics; Biot coefficient;
