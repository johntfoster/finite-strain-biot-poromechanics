\begin{abstract}
Plastic deformation changes the pore volume of a saturated solid and the
contribution of pore pressure to stress.  We derive a closed-form
finite-deformation Biot coefficient for matched logarithmic skeleton and
mineral laws.  Differentiating solid mass conservation together with the
mineral equation of state at fixed pore pressure and fixed plastic
configuration gives a single scalar equation for the mineral volume, and the
Biot coefficient then follows directly from the current total volume, the
pore pressure, and the mineral volume ratio.  The deformation split separates
volume-preserving plastic flow of the mineral from plastic distention of the
pore space.  An implicit exponential return mapping advances both mechanisms
together with the mineral state, the Biot coefficient, and the plastic
driving stress.  Automatic differentiation carries their coupled change into the
Jacobian used by Newton's method.

Independent checks verify mass conservation, the mineral equation of state,
the stress recovered from the stored energy, plastic flow, yield consistency,
and the derivatives used to evaluate the Biot coefficient and solve the discrete
equations.  Material-point calculations show how the Biot coefficient depends
on loading history during unloading and how it feeds back into plastic flow
at nonzero pore pressure.  The elastic specialization is coupled to solid and
water mass conservation and to momentum balance, and is tested against the
Mandel solution.  A larger compression test shows how the Biot coefficient
varies across the specimen during drainage.  Together, these calculations show
how mineral compressibility and plastic volume change affect pressure
coupling.
Verification of coupled flow with plastic deformation and comparison with
material data remain future work.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} finite-deformation poromechanics; Biot coefficient;
