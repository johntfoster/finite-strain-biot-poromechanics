\title{Implicit automatic differentiation of the nonlinear Biot coefficient\\
in multicomponent finite-deformation poromechanics}
\author{John T. Foster and collaborators}
\date{Draft: \today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We derive and implement the nonlinear Biot coefficient appearing in the
solid-reference specialization of a multicomponent, multiphase porous-medium
theory. The coefficient is a constrained constitutive tangent. The intrinsic
skeleton specific volume is formed from the registered solid-phase/component
material conservation statements, and its derivative with respect to the
skeleton Jacobian is evaluated at fixed equivalent pore pressure.
Local phase, volume, and equation-of-state constraints are differentiated
implicitly at each quadrature point.  The resulting dense tangent solve is
carried out with automatic-differentiation scalars, allowing MOOSE to apply a
second, outer chain rule through the state-dependent coefficient in the global
Newton system. The implementation recovers the classical \(B=1-K/K_s\) limit
and passes a coupled PETSc Jacobian test. We then calibrate a three-parameter
crack-closure compliance law to the drained tangent moduli released by Lawal
and Kim for HT14, SP14, and SP30 dunite specimens. Because their plotted
coefficient is computed from those same drained moduli and the unjacketed
modulus, the comparison is a calibration and constitutive-path verification,
with coefficient root-mean-square errors between \(0.0053\) and \(0.0087\).
The resulting workflow defines the numerical requirements for a
pressure-controlled boundary-value solve and an independent or holdout
validation study.
\end{abstract}
