\title{Implicit automatic differentiation of a nonlinear Biot coefficient:\\
finite-deformation implementation and pressure-dependent dunite response}
\author{John T. Foster and collaborators}
\date{Draft: \today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The Biot coefficient is commonly supplied to a poromechanical calculation as a
constant, even though measurements on cracked rocks show a strong dependence on
confining pressure.  We present a finite-deformation implementation in which the
coefficient is a constrained constitutive tangent rather than an independent
input.  The intrinsic skeleton specific volume is formed from the
solid-phase/component material conservation statements, and its derivative with
respect to the skeleton Jacobian is evaluated at fixed equivalent pore pressure.
Local phase, volume, and equation-of-state constraints are differentiated
implicitly at each quadrature point.  The resulting dense tangent solve is
carried out with automatic-differentiation scalars, allowing MOOSE to apply a
second, outer chain rule through the state-dependent coefficient in the global
Newton system.  The implementation recovers the classical
\(B=1-K/K_s\) limit, passes a coupled PETSc Jacobian test, and is applied to the
nonreacting pressure-dependent measurements of Lawal and Kim.  A three-parameter
crack-closure compliance law reproduces their HT14, SP14, and SP30 data with
Biot-coefficient root-mean-square errors between \(0.0053\) and \(0.0087\).
The example demonstrates that the observed decline in Biot coefficient follows
from the evolution of the drained tangent modulus relative to the unjacketed
modulus, while retaining a thermodynamically interpretable finite-deformation
coefficient and a consistent global Jacobian.
\end{abstract}
