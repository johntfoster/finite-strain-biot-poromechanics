# Independent peer review for JMPS

Date: 2026-09-18. Manuscript: *Pressure coupling and the Biot coefficient in finite-strain poroelasticity and poroplasticity*. Review target: *Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids* (JMPS).

**Recommendation: major revision before submission to JMPS.** The formulation and verification are substantial, and I did not identify a fatal algebraic inconsistency in the central energy, mineral-equilibrium, and Biot-coefficient derivation. The principal concern is whether the paper establishes a sufficiently distinct and consequential mechanics result beyond the elastic equivalence that it already acknowledges. The numerical examples establish a functioning constitutive implementation more convincingly than they establish the general physical importance of the proposed plastic coupling. This is an editorial assessment, not a finding that the model is incorrect.

The publisher describes JMPS as seeking work of lasting significance in solid mechanics, including fundamental mechanics concepts and understanding of mechanical response and physical mechanisms. The present topic fits that scope. The judgment that its contribution needs stronger discrimination is mine; experimental validation is not a blanket journal requirement. Source: [Elsevier's JMPS description](https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/journal-of-the-mechanics-and-physics-of-solids/0022-5096), accessed 2026-09-18.

## Contribution and strengths

The paper constructs a solid stored energy separating mineral compression from elastic distention, eliminates mineral volume through pressure equilibrium, and derives the reversible pressure-coupling coefficient at fixed plastic history. Multiplicative plasticity introduces permanent distention; a coupled return mapping and automatic differentiation transmit the constitutive dependencies into the finite-element equations. Elastic Mandel verification, finite compression, and a dilative poroplastic example support the implementation.

Several features merit retention:

- The distinction between differentiating at fixed intrinsic density and at fixed pressure is explicit. The energy, scalar mineral equation, stress transformation, and pressure tangent agree under the stated assumptions (`paper/sections/finite_deformation_biot.tex:90`, `:519`, `:558`, `:621`, `:683`, `:789`).
- The reference Biot coefficient, classical storage relation, incompressible-mineral limit, and unjacketed elastic response provide useful checks (`paper/sections/finite_deformation_biot.tex:729`, `:798`; `paper/sections/mandel_analytical_appendix.tex:68`).
- The manuscript identifies the imposed vertical Mandel displacement as a boundary-condition check, distinguishes numerical verification from physical validation, admits mass drift, and limits its acoustic assessment to sampled states (`paper/sections/mandel_verification.tex:42`, `:325`, `:353`, `:392`). These qualifications materially improve its credibility.
- The repository includes independent energy/stress checks, pressure tangents, objectivity checks, nonaxisymmetric loading, provenance, and spatial diagnostics. The material-point record reports stress-objectivity error near `1.0e-14` and decreasing nonaxisymmetric increment-refinement differences (`validation/implicit_poroplastic_verification.json`, keys `objectivity_stress_error` and `nonaxisymmetric_refinement_differences`). These are recorded results, not simulations rerun for this review.

## Major comments

### 1. Establish the mechanics advance beyond the acknowledged elastic equivalence

**Evidence.** The introduction states that Gajo's logarithmic energy gives the elastic mineral-pressure contribution and that his rate equations contain the elastic coefficient obtained here (`paper/sections/introduction.tex:27`). It also credits the earlier Foster–Xu work with the fixed-pressure mineral-volume definition (`:38`). The equivalence recurs in the constitutive derivation (`paper/sections/finite_deformation_biot.tex:564`, `:714`). Yet the abstract and conclusions lead with deriving the coupled energy and closed-form coefficient (`paper/main.tex:38`; `paper/sections/conclusions.tex:5`).

**Assessment.** The manuscript acknowledges prior work responsibly, but the reader must still infer which result constitutes the new mechanics. A clear derivation and reliable AD implementation are useful; their significance for JMPS needs to be distinguished from the new poroplastic consequence. This concern follows from the manuscript's own account of equivalence. It is not an independently established priority verdict on the cited literature.

**Required revision.** State explicitly which results reproduce earlier elasticity, which provide a new interpretation, and which extend the constitutive prediction. Organize the main contribution around the plastic-history dependence and its measurable consequences if that is the intended advance. Develop at least one result beyond a single selected loading path: for example, a derived sensitivity of the coefficient to plastic distention, a nondimensional regime map, or a demonstrated change in a coupled observable relative to another thermodynamically admissible constitutive choice. Show why that result matters for mechanics independently of the software architecture.

### 2. The pressure-feedback comparison does not yet establish the importance of evolving coupling

**Evidence.** The companion material-point calculation replaces only the coefficient in the double-prime to single-prime transformation and is explicitly acknowledged to lack the consistent calculation's coupled energy (`paper/sections/poroplastic_results.tex:62`). At the largest reported pressure, `1 GPa`, the stored results give final plastic distentions `1.08403965` and `1.08484806` (`validation/implicit_poroplastic_feedback.csv:7`). Their relative difference is approximately `0.0746%`, and their logarithmic difference is approximately `7.45e-4`.

**Assessment.** This is a legitimate sensitivity test, but it cannot by itself establish an improvement over a competing physical model. Substitution of the derived double-prime stress into the transformation cancels the explicit coefficient and recovers the single-prime stress `K[ln(Je) + p Jbar/Ks] I` plus the isochoric part (`paper/sections/finite_deformation_biot.tex:689`, `:698`, `:815`). Thus the coefficient expresses the same energy coupling; it is not an independently adjustable physical mechanism within that energy. Changing only the transformation breaks that relation. The present wording recognizes this, but the experiment remains a weak centerpiece for a claim about the importance of evolving coupling.

**Required revision.** Retain the modified transformation as a clearly labeled diagnostic, report the effect size, and supplement it with either a comparison between complete admissible energies or an analytical sensitivity argument linking mineral/plastic coupling to an observable such as pressure, reaction force, or exchanged fluid. Assess the effect against uncertainty in dilation, hardening, and elastic parameters. A physically admissible comparator need not keep every fitted parameter fixed if matching a reference calibration is the meaningful comparison.

### 3. Demonstrate which physical conclusions survive beyond the selected synthetic regime

**Evidence.** The material-point example reaches 35% axial compression and `1 GPa` pore pressure (`paper/sections/poroplastic_results.tex:62`). The coupled example uses one dilation slope and one positive hardening modulus (`paper/sections/mandel_verification.tex:236`), and continues a saturated barotropic fluid into tension (`:274`). The sampled edge-pressure histories reach approximately `-22.24 MPa`, as read from `validation/poroplastic_mandel_history.csv`. The final averaged direct plastic contribution is about `0.00723` (`validation/poroplastic_mandel_numerical_notes.md:51`). The manuscript explicitly defers calibration and cavitation/desaturation (`paper/sections/mandel_verification.tex:392`).

**Assessment.** These admissions are appropriate, so the issue is not an undisclosed domain violation. The question is which claimed physical consequence remains informative outside a synthetic extrapolation. The monotonic increase of the coefficient with dilation at fixed pressure and total volume follows from the selected energy. Fluid-exchange reversal, its magnitude, and the history of the coefficient in the boundary-value problem also depend on drainage, stiffness, hardening, and the fluid model. One example does not separate these dependencies.

**Required revision.** Add a compact parameter study or analytical scaling in the mineral-to-skeleton stiffness ratio, drainage time versus loading time, dilation, and hardening. Include a regime where the assumed saturated fluid description remains defensible, with an explicit pressure reference and operating range, or incorporate the relevant phase-change/desaturation mechanism if large suction is essential to the main claim. Identify which findings are exact consequences of the assumed energy and which are contingent numerical predictions. Experiments would strengthen the result, but a well-defined theoretical regime study could also address this concern.

### 4. Justify the accumulation discretization and connect mass drift to the reported observables

**Evidence.** The solid residual uses `Jdot rho_s + J rho_sdot`, and fluid accumulation uses constitutive chain-rule rates supplied from backward-Euler field rates (`paper/sections/implicit_ad_implementation.tex:118`, `:137`). The text correctly states that these differ from backward differences of complete mass accumulations (`:182`). The reported poroplastic drift decreases under time refinement; the plotted fine-grid result has a maximum water-mass mismatch of `0.291%` of initial water mass when evaluated with Darcy boundary flux (`paper/sections/mandel_verification.tex:353`). The corresponding boundary-reaction defect is about `0.0445%` (`validation/poroplastic_mandel_numerical_notes.md:61`).

**Assessment.** These results support consistency as the step decreases. They do not make the finite-step method exactly mass conserving. Moreover, the constitutive derivation uses the exact identity `J rho_s = rho_s0`, whereas the numerical update obtains solid fraction from the independently evolved density (`paper/sections/finite_deformation_biot.tex:54`; `paper/sections/implicit_ad_implementation.tex:22`). The finite-step deviation from that identity should be connected to uncertainty in the coefficient/storage relation, rather than presented only as a separate residual metric. Normalizing a mass defect by initial fluid inventory also does not show its importance relative to the fluid exchanged during reversal.

**Required revision.** Explain the benefit of choosing this time discretization over backward differences of complete accumulations. Report mass defects relative to both initial inventory and the magnitude of accumulated exchanged fluid, with a sensible treatment near zero exchange. Demonstrate that the reversal time, pressure extrema, reaction force, and plastic coefficient contrast remain stable under refinement. A conservative accumulation implementation would provide a particularly useful comparison; this is a request for numerical justification and an error budget, not a claim that the existing results fail to converge.

### 5. Bring the quantitative verification evidence into the paper

**Evidence.** The elastic benchmark discussion describes decreasing sensitivity without giving the numerical errors (`paper/sections/mandel_verification.tex:139`). The implementation discussion refers test procedures and tolerances to a companion repository (`paper/sections/implicit_ad_implementation.tex:186`). The existing record gives maximum normalized pressure error about `3.71%` for the uniform-step history, plotted-profile error about `0.98%`, and maximum load error about `0.238%` (`validation/mandel_implicit_biot.yml:15`, `:47`). The manuscript correctly distinguishes the two time sequences but does not quantify their different accuracy.

**Assessment.** The repository has more evidence than the paper conveys. Readers cannot assess an algorithm from statements that checks “pass,” and visual agreement can obscure the worst part of a transient. There is no basis here for calling the reported errors unacceptable; the missing piece is an explicit accuracy claim and its evidence.

**Required revision.** Add a concise verification table giving definitions, normalization, sampling windows, measured errors, and tolerances for the local implicit tangent, energy-derived stress, assembled Jacobian, Mandel pressure/horizontal displacement/load, and mass balances. Separate uniform-step history results from figure-profile results. Explain initialization of the Mandel step load and identify when the largest pressure error occurs. Retain the candid statement that pairwise refinement measures sensitivity rather than establishing an asymptotic order. Include a brief nonlinear-solver robustness record if robustness is claimed as an algorithmic contribution.

## Minor comments

1. **Provide a resolvable source-archive reference.** The manuscript refers to a companion repository and source archive without identifying it by URL, DOI, or release (`paper/sections/implicit_ad_implementation.tex:187`; `paper/sections/mandel_verification.tex:401`). Add the archived revision, software availability statement, and a short reproduction entry point. The detailed local provenance is a strength but is not a substitute for an accessible publication artifact.

2. **Shorten elementary kinematics in the main text.** The sequence from determinant variation through spherical variation, trace contraction, and work conversion occupies `paper/sections/finite_deformation_biot.tex:242–336`; a similar sequence is repeated for the mineral at `:369–425`. Preserve the distinct fixed-pressure and fixed-distention operations, but move routine algebra to an appendix. Use the space for the mechanics consequences and quantitative evidence requested above.

3. **Keep the acoustic conclusion precisely scoped.** The fixed-pressure, in-plane, sampled diagnostic and its limits are stated well (`paper/sections/mandel_verification.tex:325`). Preserve those qualifications in any abstract or conclusion revision. The positive determinant does not alone establish strong ellipticity of a nonsymmetric acoustic tensor or stability of the fully coupled transient. The repository also records minimum eigenvalues of its symmetric part; reporting that distinction would improve the discussion. The observed determinant sign change is useful evidence of a singular direction for the tested tangent.

4. **Explain the different reference fractions at first comparison.** The material-point example has solid fraction `0.8`, while the coupled example has `0.9` (`paper/sections/poroplastic_results.tex:8`; `paper/sections/mandel_verification.tex:244`). Both are stated, but a brief explanation of which trends can be compared across them would prevent readers from attributing all differences to coupling or drainage.

5. **Clarify the hardening and yield stress measures.** The model uses Mandel/Kirchhoff invariants and a deliberately dissipative cohesion law (`paper/sections/finite_deformation_biot.tex:854–886`). State the corresponding calibration convention when discussing synthetic or eventual experimental parameters. A cohesion measured in Cauchy stress cannot simply be inserted unchanged at finite volume change.

## Scope and limits of this review

This review read the current working-tree manuscript through `paper/main.tex` and `paper/defs.tex`, including all six main sections and the analytical appendix. It inspected selected curated numerical records, verification documentation, and the reference-flux implementation, and used the repository derivation-surface scanner. It independently checked the central differentiation, stress transformation, and limiting relations at source level. It did not use the existing peer-review or consistency reports as its basis.

No simulations, clean-environment reproduction, full code audit, or new PDF build were performed. Recorded acceptance flags and numerical metrics have not been independently reproduced. No systematic full-text literature-priority or citation audit was performed; comments on novelty use the manuscript's express acknowledgments and should be supplemented by such an audit before submission. No assertion is made that every rendered page or equation reference is correct. The report concerns the current uncommitted working-tree version, not a released manuscript. Existing source, reports, and numerical records were left unchanged.
